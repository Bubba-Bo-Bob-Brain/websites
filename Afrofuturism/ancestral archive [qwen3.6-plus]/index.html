Textile Gallery

◆

Every pattern tells a story. Every thread carries meaning. Explore the visual languages woven into cloth.

Kente Cloth

Ashanti & Ewe, Ghana

Handwoven strips of silk and cotton, each color and pattern carrying proverbs and philosophical concepts. Worn by royalty, Kente is a visual language of prestige and wisdom.

Gold — Royalty Green — Renewal Red — Sacrifice Blue — Peace

Bògòlanfini (Mud Cloth)

Bambara, Mali

Fermented mud painted onto handwoven cotton, creating geometric symbols that encode history, social status, and spiritual protection. Each design is a chapter in a visual epic.

Mud — Earth Black — Ancestors White — Purity

Adinkra Cloth

Akan, Ghana

Stamped with symbolic motifs using dye from the bark of the badie tree, Adinkra cloth communicates complex philosophical concepts. Each symbol is a visual proverb.

Gye Nyame — Except God Sankofa — Return and Get It Dwennimmen — Ram's Horns

Kuba Raffia Cloth

Kuba Kingdom, DRC

Intricate geometric patterns created through cut-pile embroidery on raffia palm fiber. The Kuba textile tradition represents one of the most sophisticated design systems in the world.

Amber — Prosperity Brown — Earth Ivory — Purity
