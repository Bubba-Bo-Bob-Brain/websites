The Creation of the World

In the beginning, before time had meaning, there existed only the great Nyame—the Sky God who watched over all creation. Below, stretched the endless waters of the primeval sea, and Asase Ya, the Earth Mother, slept within the depths.

Nyame looked upon the waters and saw his loneliness reflected, and from that loneliness, he wept tears of gold. Where his tears fell upon the waters, the earth began to rise.

The Earth Mother awakened from her long slumber and rose to meet the Sky God. Together, they brought forth all living things—the plants that carpet the earth, the animals that walk and crawl and fly, and finally, humanity.

And so we are born from the union of Sky and Earth, from the tears of a lonely god and the dreams of a sleeping goddess. This is our origin. This is our inheritance.

— End of Transmission —

The Dogon and the Nommo

c. 3200 years of astronomical precision

The Dogon people of Mali possess knowledge of the Sirius star system that wasn't confirmed by Western astronomy until the 20th century. They knew of Sirius B—a white dwarf star invisible to the naked eye—centuries before telescopic discovery.

According to their oral traditions, the Nommo—amphibious beings from a planet orbiting Sirius—visited Earth in remote antiquity. They brought civilization, agriculture, and sacred knowledge.

"The star Sigi Tolo (Sirius) has a companion star... called Po Tolo (Sirius B)..."
☥

Egyptian Stellar Theology

Alignment with eternity

The ancient Egyptians didn't merely observe stars—they became them. Their pyramids at Giza are architectural representations of Orion's Belt, and the Nile River mirrors the Milky Way in the night sky.

For the Egyptians, the soul journeyed through the Duat (underworld) guided by the stars, ultimately joining the "Imperishable Ones"—the circumpolar stars that never set.

"I am yesterday, today, and tomorrow, for I am reborn in the stars."

The Word and the Waters

Bambara cosmological paradigm

The Bambara people of West Africa teach that existence began with Faro, the Word/Life Force, moving upon the primordial waters. This divine speech didn't create from nothing—it organized chaos into cosmos.

Humans are conceived as "seed of the Word," carrying divine creative potential within. This theology places humanity as co-creators with the divine, not passive recipients of creation.

"The seed carries the tree within it, folded in darkness, waiting for the word to unfold it."
