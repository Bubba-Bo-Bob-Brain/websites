The Four Pillars of Wayfinding

Ngā Whetū

The Stars

Stars are the primary compass. Navigators memorize rising and setting points of over 200 stars, using them to maintain latitude and course across thousands of miles of open ocean.

Nga Ngaru

The Swells

Ocean swells are the roads of the sea. Navigators feel the rhythm of swells through the hull, detecting intersecting wave patterns to determine direction and proximity to land.

Ngā Manu

The Birds

Land-finding birds like the Tropic Bird and Wedge-tailed Shearwater guide canoes toward islands. Their flight paths at dawn and dusk reveal the direction of hidden land.

Te Tau

The Seasons

Seasonal patterns govern when voyaging is possible. The rising of Matariki marks the beginning of the voyaging season, when winds and currents are most favorable.

"E mau tonu te ora i te wā o te whakatere" — Life endures through the art of navigation
