Wayfinding Lore

The Star Compass

Ancient navigators divided the horizon into 32 houses, each marking the rising and setting points of celestial bodies. Stars, sun, moon, and planets became trusted guides across thousands of miles of open ocean.

Hawai'i · The Northern Beacon

Guided by Hōkūpa'a (the North Star) and the constellation Nā Hiku, voyagers sailed northward to these fertile islands, following the flight path of the Golden Plover.

Rapa Nui · Navel of the World

Located using the setting points of Matariki (Pleiades) and Tautoru (Orion's Belt). The moai stand sentinel, gazing toward the ancestral homeland.

Tahiti · Sacred Center

The Society Islands shimmer under the guidance of Ta'urua (Venus) and the great bird constellations that mark the seasons for planting and voyaging.

Aotearoa · Land of the Long Cloud

Southern voyages relied on the Magellanic Clouds and the pointers of the Southern Cross to reach these shores of greenstone and mist.

Samoa · Heart of Polynesia

The cradle of navigation, where the art of reading swells and the flight of frigate birds first taught the way to distant horizons.
