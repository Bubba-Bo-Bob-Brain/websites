⚗ The Potion Cabinet ⚗

Time-tested brews and elixirs, transcribed by moonlight.

Calming

Moonlight Tincture

A silvery draught for restless nights and overwrought spirits. Brewed under the full moon with chamomile, lavender, and a whisper of valerian root.

🕐 Steep 3 nights 🌙 Best under full moon

Ingredients

  • 2 handfuls of dried chamomile flowers
  • 1 handful of lavender buds, freshly harvested
  • 3 sprigs of valerian root, cleaned
  • 1 cup of spring water, collected at dawn
  • 2 tablespoons of raw honey
  • A sliver of moonstone (optional, for charging)

Method

  1. Begin at moonrise. Place chamomile and lavender in a ceramic bowl.
  2. Pour heated (not boiling) spring water over the herbs. Stir thrice clockwise, thrice widdershins.
  3. Add valerian root. Cover with a linen cloth and leave on the windowsill.
  4. Allow to steep for three consecutive nights, stirring gently each evening.
  5. Strain through cheesecloth on the morning of the fourth day.
  6. Stir in raw honey until dissolved. Decant into a dark glass bottle.
  7. Take one spoonful before bed, whispered intentions welcome.

⚠ Not for use before operating broomsticks or handling familiars.

Warming

Ember & Ginger Broth

A fiery restorative for cold bones and stubborn winter chills. Woven from ginger root, cinnamon bark, and the essence of hearth-warmed stones.

🔥 Simmer 1 hour ❄️ Winter essential

Ingredients

  • 4 inches of fresh ginger root, grated
  • 2 cinnamon sticks, preferably Ceylon
  • 1 tablespoon of dried elderberries
  • 5 whole cloves
  • A thumb-sized piece of fresh turmeric
  • 3 cups of rainwater
  • 1 orange, sliced into crescents
  • Honey or maple syrup to taste

Method

  1. Gather your ingredients by the hearth. Light a fire if possible.
  2. Place rainwater in a copper pot over low flames.
  3. Add grated ginger, cinnamon sticks, and cloves. Stir with a wooden spoon carved from rowan.
  4. Add elderberries and sliced turmeric. Allow to come to a gentle murmur.
  5. Float orange crescents on top. Cover and simmer for one hour.
  6. Strain into clay mugs. Sweeten with honey while still warm.
  7. Drink wrapped in a woolen blanket, ideally while watching snow fall.

⚠ May cause spontaneous feelings of coziness and an irresistible urge to knit.

Refreshing

Morning Dew Elixir

A bright, clarifying tonic for foggy minds and sluggish mornings. Gathered from nettle tips, lemon balm, and the first light of dawn.

🌅 Harvest at dawn 🌿 Spring & Summer

Ingredients

  • A generous bunch of fresh nettle tops (wear gloves!)
  • 1 bunch of lemon balm leaves
  • 5 fresh mint sprigs
  • 1 tablespoon of dried rosemary
  • Juice of 1 lemon
  • 1 tablespoon of local honey
  • 2 cups of boiled water, cooled to warm

Method

  1. Rise before the sun. Gather nettles (with thick gloves) and lemon balm from the garden.
  2. Rinse herbs gently in cold water. Roughly chop the lemon balm.
  3. Place all herbs in a large teapot or mason jar.
  4. Pour warm (not hot) water over the herbs. Let steep for 15 minutes.
  5. Strain into cups. Add lemon juice and honey. Stir with a fresh mint sprig.
  6. Drink slowly, facing east, while the world wakes up around you.

⚠ Nettles sting! Respect their boundaries. They respect yours in return (eventually).

Protective

Bramble Guardian Oil

A potent ward against mischievous spirits and ill-wishing neighbors. Distilled from blackthorn berries, hawthorn leaves, and iron-rich spring water.

🛡 Protective charm 🌑 New moon ritual

Ingredients

  • A cup of blackthorn berries, carefully de-spined
  • Hawthorn leaves and flowers, dried
  • 1/2 cup of iron-rich spring water
  • 1 cup of olive oil (virgin, cold-pressed)
  • A pinch of dried mugwort
  • 3 drops of pine resin
  • A small iron nail (blessed, for charging)

Method

  1. On the night of the new moon, crush blackthorn berries in a stone mortar.
  2. Combine with hawthorn, mugwort, and iron water in a small cauldron or cast-iron pot.
  3. Warm gently over a low flame. Stir with an iron spoon.
  4. Add olive oil and pine resin. Continue stirring until the mixture darkens.
  5. Remove from heat. Place the blessed iron nail in the pot and cover.
  6. Let rest overnight. Strain through a dark cloth into a small glass vial.
  7. Anoint doorframes, windowsills, and the threshold of your hearth.

⚠ Keep away from children, familiars with curious paws, and anyone who has not been properly introduced.

🗺 The Forager's Map 🗺

Where to look, what to gather, and when to whisper thanks to the earth.

Spring Awakening

As the frost loosens its grip and the first green shoots push through darkened soil, the hedgerows come alive with tender offerings. Move gently.

🌱

Wild Garlic (Allium ursinum)

Where: Damp woodlands, along stream banks, beneath ancient oaks.

When: March through May, before the flowers open.

Use: Pesto, soups, infused butter, protective sachets. The leaves smell distinctly of garlic.

⚠ Beware of lily of the valley, which is toxic and superficially similar.
🌻

Stinging Nettle (Urtica dioica)

Where: Everywhere! Field edges, woodland clearings, abandoned gardens.

When: Early spring, when shoots are 15-20cm tall. Harvest the top four leaves.

Use: Tea, soup, cordial, hair rinse, fabric dye. Wear thick gloves.

🌼

Dandelion (Taraxacum officinale)

Where: Meadows, lawns, roadsides.

When: Leaves in early spring; roots in autumn; flowers in late spring.

Use: Greens in salad, root roasted for coffee, flowers for fritters and wine.

Summer's Bounty

The hedgerows bow heavy with fruit and the meadows hum with pollinator song. Gather in the early morning.

🫐

Elderflower (Sambucus nigra)

Where: Hedgerows, woodland edges, churchyards, old orchards.

When: Late May to mid-June.

Use: Cordial, champagne, fritters, infused vinegar.

🍓

Wild Strawberry (Fragaria vesca)

Where: Woodland edges, meadow borders, beneath bramble arches.

When: June through August. Tiny but intensely flavored.

Use: Eaten fresh, in tarts, infused in cream or gin.

🌿

Yarrow (Achillea millefolium)

Where: Dry meadows, paths, banks.

When: June through September.

Use: Wound herb, tea for fever, bitter seasoning, dried arrangements.

Autumn's Farewell

The trees don their russet cloaks and the hedgerows offer their final, richest gifts. This is the season of preservation.

🥩

Chanterelle (Cantharellus cibarius)

Where: Mossy woodlands, beneath birch and oak.

When: August through October, after warm rain.

Use: Sauteed in butter, dried for winter sauces. Smells faintly of apricots.

⚠ Forage only with an experienced guide. Many toxic lookalikes exist.
🌰

Hazelnut (Corylus avellana)

Where: Woodland edges, hedgerows, coppiced groves.

When: September through October.

Use: Roasted, in baking, pressed for oil, ground into flour.

🍂

Rosehip (Rosa canina)

Where: Hedgerows, field margins, along old stone walls.

When: October onwards, after the first frost.

Use: Syrup, tea, jam, oil. Rich in vitamin C.

Winter's Wisdom

The land sleeps beneath frost and snow, but even in the deepest cold, the patient forager finds sustenance.

🌲

Pine Needle (Pinus sylvestris)

Where: Pine forests, heathland, shelterbelts.

When: Year-round, but winter needles are especially rich in vitamin C.

Use: Tea, syrup, infusion, bath soak. Always identify correctly.

🧒

Burdock Root (Arctium lappa)

Where: Waste ground, field edges, woodland margins.

When: First year roots in late autumn and winter.

Use: Roasted as coffee substitute, in stews, pickled.

🍆

Ivy Berry (Hedera helix)

Where: Old walls, tree trunks, woodland floors.

When: December through February.

Use: External use only. A sacred winter plant, beloved by birds.

⚠ Ivy berries are NOT edible for humans. External use only.

📜 Handwritten Remedy Notes 📜

Scattered pages from Elara's personal journal, pressed between the leaves of time.

14th March 1892

For the Persistent Cough

Take of thyme leaves — fresh, mind you, not that dusty shop sort — a generous handful. Bruise them gently in your mortar, releasing their green spirit.

Pour over one cup of just-boiled water. Cover with a saucer (the china one with the blue flowers, if you please). Let it steep until the colour of pale amber.

Strain. Add honey — local, raw, still tasting of clover — to taste. A squeeze of lemon if the patient is fretful.

Sip slowly, wrapped in the patchwork quilt. Three times daily until the cough loosens its stubborn grip.

7th September 1894

Lavender Pillow for Sleepless Nights

Harvest lavender when the morning dew has just evaporated — this is when the oils are richest, caught by the sun but not yet scattered to the wind.

Dry the stems upside down in the attic, in bundles of a dozen, tied with red thread. Hang where the breeze can reach them but not the rain.

Once thoroughly dry — they should crumble at a touch — strip the buds from the stems. Fill a small linen sachet, no larger than a plum.

Place beneath the pillow. If the sleeper is very restless, add a sprig of rosemary for remembrance and a small amethyst chip for peaceful dreams.

21st December 1896

Solstice Fire Cider — A Winter Shield

This is not a gentle remedy. This is armor in a bottle.

Fill a large jar with finely chopped horseradish, ginger, garlic, onion, and fresh rosemary. Add a sliced lemon, seeds of a chili pepper, and enough raw apple cider vinegar to cover everything by an inch.

Seal with a plastic lid (vinegar eats metal — learned that the hard way). Shake daily for a moon's cycle. Strain on the next new moon.

Add raw honey to soften the fire. Drink a small glass at the first sign of winter illness, or take a spoonful daily as a tonic.

Made on the longest night, when the dark holds the most power. The fire cider carries that strength through the cold months.

3rd May 1893

Simple Salve for Worn Hands

For hands roughened by garden work, stained with berry juice, and cracked by wind — this is the remedy.

Melt 2 tablespoons of beeswax with 4 tablespoons of olive oil in a double boiler. Stir until liquid and golden.

Remove from heat. Stir in 1 tablespoon of calendula-infused oil (steep dried calendula petals in olive oil for a fortnight beforehand).

Add 5 drops of lavender essential oil. Pour into small tins while still liquid. Let set overnight.

Apply generously before bed. Wear old cotton gloves to sleep. By morning, your hands will be soft as rose petals.
