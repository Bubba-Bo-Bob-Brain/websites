Remedy Notes

Personal observations, marginalia, and field notes scrawled across many a moonlit evening

Tincture Timing

The elderflower tincture must be begun at the dark of the moon and strained precisely 28 days later. I learned this the hard way when the batch I started at the full moon turned bitter — the faeries do not approve of rushing.

tincture moon phase
Written: April 3rd

On Mushroom Foraging

Never take more than a third of what grows in any one place. The forest remembers greed. I leave an offering of tobacco and honey at the base of the stump before I harvest — it has kept me safe these twenty years.

foraging ethics fungi
Written: September 12th

A Warning About Digitalis

Foxglove is both healer and killer. The margin between medicine and poison is three leaves. I mark every foxglove plant in my garden with a red stone — the children know never to touch the red stones.

warning toxic heart
Written: June 21st

Cold Remedy for the Grandchildren

Honey, ginger, lemon, and a whisper of cinnamon. Simmer gently — never boil honey. Give a spoonful before bed and the child will sleep like a kitten. Add a sprig of thyme if the cough is stubborn.

The Grimoire

A living book — spells, incantations, and wisdom gathered over a lifetime of wandering

The Thornwood Grimoire Est. 1987
ᚠᚢᚦᚨᚱᚲ

Page I — On the Nature of Herbs

Every herb carries within it the memory of the soil from which it grew, the rain that nourished it, and the sun that coaxed it toward the light. To work with herbs is to enter into conversation with these forces.

One must never approach the craft with arrogance. The plant spirits are ancient and wise beyond our reckoning. Approach with humility, an open heart, and clean hands.

Page II — The Three Sacred Tools

The mortar and pestle grounds not only herbs but also the ego. In the grinding, we learn patience.

The cauldron transforms — what enters as raw material emerges as medicine. So too does the witch transform through practice and time.

The athame directs intention. It cuts through confusion and reveals the path forward. Never use it in anger — the blade remembers.

Page III — Moon Phase Guide

New Moon: Plant seeds of intention. Begin new projects. Detox and cleanse.

Waxing Moon: Growth, attraction, and building. Gather herbs above ground.

Full Moon: Maximum potency. Harvest roots and powerful herbs. Charge crystals and tools.

Waning Moon: Release, banish, and diminish. Prune. Harvest roots below ground.
