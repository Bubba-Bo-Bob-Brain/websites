Draught of Slumber

For restful sleep and sweet dreams

Requisites:

  • 3 sprigs dried lavender
  • 1 chamomile blossom, whole
  • A pinch of valerian root
  • 2 drops moonwater
  • Honey to taste

Method:

Steep the lavender and chamomile in water brought to a rolling boil. Add valerian root with your left hand, stirring thrice clockwise. Let cool until the steam forms a crown, then add moonwater and honey. Drink before the last ember dies.

⚠ Do not take before operating a broomstick.
Tincture of
Courage

Tincture of Courage

To warm the heart and steady the hand

Requisites:

  • 1 dried chili pepper, crushed
  • A sliver of ginger root
  • 5 saffron threads
  • 1 rosemary sprig, fresh
  • A whisper of cinnamon bark

Method:

Crush the pepper and ginger together in a mortar of stone. Add saffron and let the mixture rest in sunlight from dawn to noon. Simmer with rosemary and cinnamon until the tincture glows like captured sunset. Take three drops under the tongue.

⚠ May cause spontaneous singing.
Philtre of
Forgetting

Philtre of Forgetting

To release what no longer serves

Requisites:

  • 1 white rose petal, dried
  • Mist from a morning meadow
  • Willow bark, shaved thin
  • A lock of hair (your own)
  • Rainwater collected in silence

Method:

Place the rose petal in rainwater and speak aloud the memory you wish to release. Add willow bark and let the philtre sit beneath a waning moon. At dawn, stir with the lock of hair and pour the whole upon the earth. What the soil takes, the mind releases.

⚠ Cannot undo what was meant to be remembered.

Mending

Salve of Mending

For wounds of body and spirit

Requisites:

  • 2 comfrey leaves, fresh
  • 1 calendula head, full
  • Beeswax, a thumb's worth
  • Olive oil, cold-pressed
  • A tear, freely given

Method:

Infuse comfrey and calendula in olive oil over low heat for one turning of the hourglass. Strain through linen, add beeswax, and stir with a wooden spoon until the salve sets. The tear must be added at the very last — it is the binding agent. Apply to the afflicted area with gentle intention.

⚠ A forced tear will sour the salve.
Chapter II

Pressed Flower Specimens

"Each petal holds a season, each leaf a story."

Wild Dog Rose

Rosa canina

Pressed on Midsummer's Eve

Properties: Heart-opening, gentle astringent. The hips hold more vitamin C than any citrus. A rose gathered at dawn carries the strongest medicine.

Foxglove

Digitalis purpurea

Pressed on the first frost

Properties: Powerful and dangerous. The fairy's glove holds the key to the heart's rhythm — both its mending and its breaking. Handle with reverence. Never use without a healer's guidance.

Venenum

Yarrow

Achillea millefolium

Pressed on Lammas morning

Properties: The soldier's woundwort. Stops bleeding, eases fevers, opens the pores. A fistful of yarrow thrown into a fire will draw truth from those nearby. Or so the old stories say.

Elderflower

Sambucus nigra

Pressed on the summer solstice

Properties: The Elder Mother watches over this tree. Its blossoms ease cold and flu, its berries build the blood. Never burn elder wood, and always ask permission before harvesting.

St. John's Wort

Hypericum perforatum

Pressed on St. John's Day

Properties: Chases away the dark — in mind and in spirit. The red oil from crushed buds mends nerve and skin alike. Hang above the door on Midsummer to keep ill will at bay.

Chamomile

Matricaria chamomilla

Pressed on a quiet August afternoon

Properties: The herb of patience. Calms the restless, soothes the stomach, brings peace to the teething child. Walk over chamomile and it will strengthen; walk over grass and it will wither. So choose your path wisely.

Chapter IV

Handwritten Remedy Notes

"From my desk to yours — may these notes find you well."

A Cough Syrup

Dear one,

For the cough that rattles like a small ghost in the chest — take honey and onion, layered in a jar. Let them sit overnight by the fire. In the morning, the honey will have drawn out the onion's medicine. One spoonful as needed.

Add thyme if the cough is deep. Add lemon if the throat is sore. Add a prayer if nothing else works.

— H.T.
For the Racing Mind

When the thoughts will not stop their spinning —

Brew equal parts lemon balm, linden flower, and skullcap. Drink slowly. Sit by water if you can. If not, hold a smooth stone in each hand and breathe as though you are filling a bellows.

This too shall pass. It always does.

— H.T.
A Burn Remedy

For when the kettle betrays you —

Break a leaf of aloe and apply the gel directly. If no aloe is at hand, the inside of an egg's membrane, pressed gently against the burn, will ease the pain and speed the healing.

Lavender essential oil may also be applied neat. The old wives know what they're about.

— H.T.
After Too Much Pudding

A tincture for the overfond stomach —

Fennel seed and peppermint, steeped strong. A pinch of ginger if there is nausea. Walk slowly around the garden. Do not lie down; the body must stand upright to honour what it has taken in.

Next time, listen to your belly before the third helping.

— H.T.
The Singer's Gargle

For a voice gone rough —

Warm salt water with a crushed sage leaf. Gargle three times, each time humming a low note. The vibration helps the sage reach where the salt cannot. Follow with a teaspoon of honey mixed with a grain of turmeric.

Do not speak for one hour after. The silence is part of the cure.

— H.T.
The Midnight Bath

When sleep will not come —

Fill the bath with water as warm as you can bear. Add a handful of Epsom salts, 7 drops of lavender, and 3 of cedarwood. Soak until the water cools. Do not look at the clock. Do not think about tomorrow.

Wrap yourself in wool. The sleep will find you.

— H.T.
