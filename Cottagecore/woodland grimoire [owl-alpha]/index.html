✦ Handwritten Remedy Notes ✦

Scrawled in haste by candlelight — remedies for common ailments

For Aching Heads

Steep willow bark in hot water for a quarter-hour. Add a pinch of dried peppermint and honey from the wild hive behind the old oak.

⚠ Do not take on an empty stomach, dear one.

~ Written on the 14th of Harvestmoon

For Restless Nights

A cup of chamomile and valerian tea, sipped slowly while the fire burns low. Hang dried lavender above your pillow — the moths will keep watch.

~ Written on the 3rd of Frostmoon

Winter's Cough

Ginger root, sliced thin. Simmer with lemon and a generous spoonful of thyme honey. Breathe the steam before drinking — let it warm you from within.

⚠ Add whiskey for the particularly stubborn coughs.

~ Written on the 22nd of Snowmoon

For Bruises & Bumps

Crush fresh comfrey leaves into a poultice. Bind with clean linen and leave overnight. By morning, the purple shall fade to gold.

~ Written on the 8th of Bloommoon

For Heartache

Rosehip and hawthorn berry tea, sweetened with wildflower honey. Sit beneath the oldest tree you can find and listen — the forest remembers how to heal.

⚠ Some remedies take longer than others. Be patient with yourself.

~ Written on the 1st of Heartmoon

For Splinters & Thorns

Draw out with a poultice of plantain leaf and a drop of honey. Clean with witch hazel. The forest gives its thorns freely — we must learn to receive them gently.

~ Written on the 19th of Bloommoon

🌱 Spring Foraging

Wild Garlic

📍 Forest floors, near streams

Gather young leaves before flowering. The bulbs are milder than cultivated varieties.

Hawthorn Blossoms

📍 Hedgerows, woodland edges

Pick on a dry morning. Excellent for heart tonics and love workings.

Nettle Tops

📍 Rich soil, disturbed ground

Wear gloves! The youngest tops make the finest spring tonic. Rich in iron and vitality.

☀️ Summer Foraging

Lavender

📍 Sunny banks, cottage gardens

Harvest when half the buds open. Bundle and hang to dry in a dark, airy place.

Wild Strawberries

📍 Woodland clearings, grassy banks

Tiny but impossibly sweet. The leaves make a gentle tea for digestion.

Elderflower

📍 Hedgerows, village edges

Shake flowers gently to remove insects before use. Cordial, fritters, or skin wash.

🍂 Autumn Foraging

Rosehips

📍 Hedgerows, after first frost

Best gathered after the first frost sweetens them. Rich in vitamin C — make syrup or jam.

Hazelnuts

📍 Hedgerows, coppiced woodland

Gather when husks begin to brown. Store in a cool, dry place. The squirrels will thank you for leaving some.

Rowan Berries

📍 Highland edges, old orchards

Bitter raw but makes a wonderful jelly. Powerful protective herb — never harvest all from one tree.

❄️ Winter Foraging

Wintergreen

📍 Pine forest floors, acidic soil

One of the few plants that stays green under snow. The leaves make a warming tea.

Dried Mushrooms

📍 Fallen logs, old stumps

Only gather what you can positively identify. Dried specimens keep their potency through winter.

Ivy Berries

📍 Old walls, tree trunks

⚠ For external use only. The dried berries make a powerful poultice for joint pain.
