Moonlight Slumber Draught

Gentle

☽ Ingredients

  • 3 sprigs of lavender, gathered at dusk
  • 1 handful chamomile flowers
  • 5 drops honey from the hillside hive
  • A pinch of powdered valerian root
  • Spring water, charged under full moon

✿ Method

  1. Steep lavender and chamomile in warm water as the sun sets
  2. Stir widdershwise seven times whilst whispering your intention
  3. Add honey slowly, watching it spiral like liquid gold
  4. Strain through linen before the first star appears
  5. Drink from a clay cup whilst gazing at the moon

Effect: Brings peaceful slumber and dreams of meadows

☽ Best brewed during the waxing crescent

Warding Hearth Tincture

Moderate

☽ Ingredients

  • Dried rosemary, blessed at dawn
  • Black salt from the eastern shore
  • Three cloves of garlic, peeled under protection
  • Rowan berries, exactly nine
  • Spring water and a copper coin

✿ Method

  1. Combine all dry ingredients in a cast-iron mortar
  2. Grind whilst reciting the old words of protection
  3. Transfer to a dark glass bottle and fill with water
  4. Drop the copper coin inside as anchor
  5. Place at each threshold and windowsill

Effect: Guards the home against ill spirits and bitter winds

🌑 Best brewed during the dark moon

Garden's Bloom Elixir

Gentle

☽ Ingredients

  • Comfrey leaves, fresh-picked
  • Nettle tips, handled with care
  • Crushed eggshells from the coop
  • A spoonful of rich forest soil
  • Rainwater collected in a wooden bowl

✿ Method

  1. Gather all ingredients whilst singing to the garden
  2. Steep in rainwater for three days and nights
  3. Strain and dilute until the colour of weak tea
  4. Pour at the roots whilst speaking gratitude
  5. Watch the garden answer your kindness

Effect: Encourages abundant growth and grateful blooms

🌓 Best brewed during the waxing gibbous

Spring Awakening

The earth stirs and green shoots emerge. Gather nettles with thick gloves for iron-rich teas and soups. Dandelion greens purify the blood after winter's heaviness.

Witch's Tip: Collect birch sap during the full moon for a cleansing tonic.
☀️

Summer Abundance

The meadow bursts with flowers and fragrance. Harvest elderflowers for cordial, rose petals for honey, and linden blossoms for the finest calming tea.

Witch's Tip: Dry herbs in bundles hung from the rafters — the warm air preserves their essence.
🍂

Autumn Harvest

The hedgerows bow under their bounty. Rosehips for vitamin C, elderberries for immune syrup, hawthorn for the heart. Gather generously before the frost.

Witch's Tip: Make a great hedge witch's syrup — elderberry, rosehip, and ginger for winter's darkest days.
❄️

Winter Rest

The forest sleeps but still gives. Pine needles for vitamin-rich tea, juniper for purification, and the inner bark of willow for pain. Work by the hearth with stored herbs.

Witch's Tip: This is the time for planning, seed-ordering, and crafting salves from summer's stored oils.

For the Throbbing Head

Written: 14th March, in the Year of the Willow

My dearest reader,

When the head pounds like a blacksmith's hammer and the light grows too sharp, I turn to the old ways that have served our mothers and their mothers before them.

Take feverfew leaves — fresh if you have them, dried if you must. Chew one leaf slowly (it is bitter, I know, but bear with it). The pain will ease like a retreating tide.

Alternatively, a compress of peppermint oil and cool water pressed to the temples works wonders. I keep a small vial in my apron pocket always.

For those headaches that come with the moon's turning (you know which ones I mean), begin taking feverfew three days before your cycle. Prevention, as always, is kinder than cure.

With warmth,
— M.

When Sleep Will Not Come

Written: 3rd November, by candlelight

Oh, the restless nights. I know them well — when the mind races like a startled deer and the body refuses its needed rest.

My recipe for the dream pillow:

  • One handful dried lavender
  • Half handful hops flowers
  • A pinch of mugwort (the dream herb!)
  • Three rose petals for sweetness

Sew these into a small linen sachet and tuck it beneath your pillowcase. Replace the herbs each new moon, or when the scent fades.

The tea I make on truly desperate nights: valerian root (small piece, it is strong), passionflower, and a spoonful of honey. Drink whilst reading something boring — the combination is unbeatable.

Sweet dreams,
— M.

The First Signs of a Cold

Written: 28th January, whilst sneezing

That familiar tickle in the throat, the slight chill beneath the skin — I must act swiftly!

The moment I feel it coming on, I make the Fire Cider that my grandmother taught me:

  • Grated ginger (generous handful)
  • Three cloves garlic, crushed
  • One onion, chopped
  • Horseradish root if available
  • Cayenne pepper (a good shake)
  • Apple cider vinegar to cover
  • Honey, to sweeten when ready

Steep all in a jar for two weeks, shaking daily. Strain and take one tablespoon at the first sign, three times daily. It burns going down — that's how you know it's working!

Gargle with salt water and elderberry syrup too. I shall write more on elderberry another day — it deserves its own entry entirely.

Stay well,
— M.

On Healing the Skin

Written: 17th June, after the garden party

A note to my future self (who always forgets): the skin is the largest organ and deserves the gentlest care.

For minor cuts and scrapes: yarrow poultice. Chew the fresh leaves (yes, chew them — the saliva activates the compounds) and press directly onto the wound. It stings briefly but stops bleeding remarkably.

For dry, cracked hands (the curse of the gardener): warm olive oil infused with calendula petals. Massage into hands before bed and wear cotton gloves. Wake to baby-soft skin!

For sun-kissed burns (I should learn to wear a wider hat): fresh aloe gel from the windowsill plant, or a cool compress of black tea. The tannins reduce inflammation beautifully.

And for general glow and vitality: drink nettle tea daily. It is the finest blood tonic known to herbalists. Your skin will thank you from the inside out.

With love for the skin we're in,
— M.
