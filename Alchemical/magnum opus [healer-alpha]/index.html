— Folio I —

De Nigredo

In the beginning of the Great Work, all matter must be reduced to its first principle. The Nigredo, or Blackening, is the stage of putrefaction and decomposition. Like a seed that must rot in the earth before it can sprout, so must the prima materia undergo its dissolution.

The alchemist must heat the vessel with the fire of philosophical sulfur, watching as the black crow (corvus) rises from the ash. This is the sign that the work has begun in truth.

Nota bene: The blackness must be absolute 🜏 Sulphur ☉ Mercury ☽
— Folio II —

De Albedo et Citrinitas

When the blackness has reached its zenith, the moon (Luna) begins to shine. The Albedo, or Whitening, is the washing of the dissolved matter with the dew of heaven. The substance becomes white as milk, pure as snow.

Following the whitening, the Citrinitas dawns — the yellowing, the sunrise of gold. This is the awakening of the solar principle within the matter. The red sun and white moon are now prepared for their sacred marriage.

☽ Rosa Alba — the white rose of purification Aurum non est aurum vulgaris
— Folio III —

De Rubedo

The final stage, the Rubedo or Reddening, is the culmination of all art. The Philosopher's Stone manifests in its full glory — the Red Lion, the Lapis Philosophorum, that which can transmute base metals into gold and grant the Elixir of Life.

Here the alchemist achieves the unio mystica — the sacred marriage of Sol and Luna, sulfur and mercury, masculine and feminine principles united in perfect balance. The Great Work is complete.
