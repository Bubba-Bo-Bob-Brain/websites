The Grand Ball at the Waldorf Astoria: A Night of Unparalleled Splendour

Thousands of golden lights reflected off crystal chandeliers as New York's elite descended upon the grand ballroom for an evening that would be spoken of for decades to come.

Read the Full Account
Scroll
Society & The Gilded Set

The evening of December the twelfth shall not soon be forgotten by those fortunate enough to have received a card bearing the Waldorf's unmistakable crest. Upon arrival, guests were greeted by a corridor of gilded columns, each entwined with ivory silk and fresh gardenias imported at no small expense from the islands. The air itself seemed perfumed with possibility, as if the very spirit of the age had taken material form and chosen to dance among us.

Madame Devereaux, ever the arbitress of taste, arrived in a gown of liquid gold that seemed poured rather than woven — a creation of the House of Worth that required no fewer than three attendants to arrange its train. Upon her arm, a bracelet of diamonds and onyx caught the light of a thousand candles, throwing prismatic reflections across the marble floor like captured starlight.

The orchestra, under the direction of Monsieur Renault, performed a programme that moved deftly from the refined strains of Strauss to the insistent, intoxicating rhythms that have arrived from the South Side of Chicago. It was in these latter numbers that the ballroom truly came alive — even the most reserved among the company found their shoulders swaying, their feet betraying an unwillingness to remain still.

We are not merely attending a ball, dear friend. We are witnessing the coronation of an era.

— Countess Alessandra Von Hesse

As the clock approached the witching hour, a hush fell upon the assembly. From the upper gallery, a shower of gold leaf descended upon the dancers — a theatrical masterstroke orchestrated by the hotel's creative director, who had reportedly spent six months in preparation. The effect was nothing short of alchemical: for one transcendent moment, every soul in that room appeared gilded, as though Midas himself had passed through their ranks.

The festivities continued until the first pale fingers of dawn crept across Fifth Avenue, and the final guests departed in motorcars humming through the frost-kissed streets, carrying with them the unmistakable glow of an evening that would echo through the salons and drawing rooms of Manhattan for seasons to come.

Travel & Leisure

The Orient Express Reimagined: Luxury Awaits the Bold Traveller

The Orient Express

There exists a train that defies the very notion of mere transportation. The revived Orient Express — with its Lalique panels, polished mahogany, and cuisine that rivals the finest tables of Paris — offers a journey not merely through geography, but through the heights of civilisation itself. From the gare de Lyon to the domes of Istanbul, every mile unfolds like a chapter of the grandest novel ever written.

We joined the maiden voyage of the 1925 season, accompanied by a select company of diplomats, artists, and those rare souls who understand that the journey itself is the destination. What we discovered aboard this palace on rails exceeded even our considerable expectations.

From the Editor's Desk

Dear Readers, as the year draws to its gilded close, we find ourselves in a moment of rare reflection. Never before has an era promised so much, delivered so lavishly, and whispered of yet greater wonders to come. The skyscrapers rise, the music swings, the champagne flows — and yet, beneath the shimmering surface, there beats a heart of earnest aspiration.

This issue is our tribute to that aspiration — to the builders and the dreamers, the dancers and the dissenters, all of whom together compose the magnificent symphony of our age. We raise a glass to you all.

Yours in elegance,
Edmund W. Hartley
