The Grandest Ballroom in All of Manhattan

By Eleanora Whitfield • Photographed by James Beaumont

The Grand Metropolitan Ballroom

The newly unveiled Metropolitan Ballroom, with its soaring coffered ceilings and twenty-four karat gold leaf embellishments.

The doors of the Metropolitan swung open on the evening of November fifteenth to reveal what can only be described as the most magnificent interior this city has ever witnessed. Crystal chandeliers, each containing precisely one thousand hand-cut prisms, descended from a ceiling painted with scenes from Greek mythology, while below, the cream marble floors reflected the golden glow like a frozen lake at dawn.

Society's most illustrious figures arrived in a procession of Rolls-Royces and Duesenbergs, their evening attire shimmering under the porte-cochère's artfully positioned floodlights. Mrs. Cornelius Vanderbilt III, resplendent in a Worth gown of midnight blue velvet, was among the first to grace the receiving line, followed closely by the ever-dashing Duke of Westminster and his entourage of European nobility.

“

One cannot help but feel that we are living in an age of unprecedented magnificence.

— Mrs. Cornelius Vanderbilt III

The orchestra, imported specially from Vienna, struck up a waltz at precisely nine o'clock, and the dance floor became a swirling kaleidoscope of silk, diamonds, and champagne-colored taffeta. Chef Auguste Escoffier himself had overseen the evening's menu, which included no fewer than twelve courses and culminated in a spectacular croquembouche that towered seven feet above the dessert table.

Architect Stanford White's protégé, Mr. Charles Whitmore, spoke exclusively to The Gilded Gazette about his vision for the space: "I wished to create nothing less than a temple to the art of celebration itself. Every line, every curve, every gilded surface exists to elevate the human spirit and remind us of civilization's highest aspirations."

Editorial & Commentary

The Editor's Desk

By William Randolph Ashford III

We find ourselves at a remarkable crossroads in the history of civilization. The war that was to end all wars has passed into memory, and from its ashes has risen a generation determined to seize every moment of joy, beauty, and magnificence that life has to offer.

This issue of The Gilded Gazette celebrates that indomitable spirit. Within these pages, you shall discover the grandest ballrooms, the finest motorcars, the most exquisite fashions, and the most captivating personalities of our age.

Let us revel in this golden moment, dear reader, for we are living through nothing less than a renaissance of human aspiration.

On Fashion

By Madame Colette Beaumont

The modern woman has declared her independence, and nowhere is this revolution more visible than in the silhouette she presents to the world. Gone are the constricting corsets and trailing skirts of our mothers' generation.

In their place: clean lines, daring hemlines, and fabrics that move with the body rather than imprison it. Coco Chanel's revolutionary designs continue to dominate, while Jean Patou introduces athletic wear that transitions seamlessly from the tennis court to the tea room.

The bobbed haircut, once considered scandalous, has become the definitive mark of the emancipated woman.

The Sporting Life

By Sir Reginald Pemberton

As the autumn mists settle over the polo grounds of Long Island, one cannot help but reflect upon the extraordinary evolution of gentleman's sport in our modern age. The season ahead promises unprecedented competition.

Young Mr. Bobby Jones continues his meteoric rise in the world of golf, while the thoroughbred Man o'War has retired to stud after a career that redefined what the sport of kings could achieve.

For those who prefer their excitement with rather more velocity, the automobile races at Brooklands draw ever-larger crowds of the sporting set.
