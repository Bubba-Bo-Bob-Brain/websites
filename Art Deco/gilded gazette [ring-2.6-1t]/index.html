The Cotton Club Reopens: How Harlem's Nightlife Is Reshaping American Culture

Eleanora Sinclair • July 14, 1927 • Society
The lights of Harlem burn brighter than any chandelier in Manhattan's finest establishments, and the city knows it. As we reported last month, the Cotton Club has once again expanded its reach beyond the boundaries of mere entertainment, becoming nothing less than a cultural embassy where the sounds of Duke Ellington mingle with the clinking of champagne flutes, and where the color line — at least for an evening — dissolves into the haze of cigarette smoke and syncopated rhythm.

The new season, which opened to a packed house of socialites, artists, and curious visitors from downtown, promises to be the most ambitious yet. Cab Calloway himself was spotted in the audience on opening night, and whispers circulate that the Ziegfeld Follies are scouting talent directly from the Savoy Ballroom stage.
