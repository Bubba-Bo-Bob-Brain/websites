Midnight at the Vanderbilt Estate

By Reginald Ashford III | December 15, 1924

The automobile pulled through the iron gates at precisely half past eleven, its headlights cutting through the December fog like golden blades. Behind the wheel sat Eleanor Whitmore, her silver beaded dress catching the moonlight, her eyes fixed upon the magnificent estate that rose before her like a palace from a fairy tale.

The Vanderbilt mansion blazed with a thousand lights, each window a warm invitation to the night's festivities. Crystal chandeliers visible through the tall Georgian windows cast pr manicured gardens, transforming the winter landscape into something from a dream.

"There has never been, nor will there ever be, a gathering quite like this one. The Vanderbilts have outdone themselves, and all of New York society agrees."

— Mrs. Astor, upon arriving at the ball

Inside, the grand ballroom had been transformed into a winter wonderland of silver and gold. Enormous arrangements of white roses and trailing ivy adorned every surface, while hundreds of candles flickered in crystal holders, casting a warm, romantic glow over the assembled guests.

The orchestra, positioned on a raised platform at the far end of the room, played the most enchanting waltzes, their melodies weaving through the crowd like silk ribbons on the evening breeze. Couples moved across the polished marble floor in elegant procession, their movements as graceful as any ballet.

Champagne flowed freely from silver fountains, the golden liquid catching the candlelight as it cascaded into crystal flutes held by white-gloved footmen. The finest vintage from the Vanderbilts' private cellars, it was said that tonight's champagne had traveled specially from the houses of Moët & Chandon in France.

Eleanor made her entrance down the sweeping staircase, and a hush fell over the assembled crowd. Her gown, designed specially by the House of Worth, was a masterpiece of silver silk and intricate beadwork that seemed to capture and reflect every ray of light in the room.

The Season's Most Anticipated Debutantes

By Vivienne Sterling

As the winter season approaches, society matrons and their daughters prepare for the most important events of the year. This season promises to be particularly spectacular, with no fewer than twelve young women making their formal introduction to society.

Among the most anticipated is Miss Catherine Pembroke, daughter of the esteemed Mr. and Mrs. Harold Pembroke of Fifth Avenue. Miss Pembroke, who completed her studies at Miss Porter's School in Farmington, Connecticut, is said to possess both beauty and wit in equal measure.

The debutante ball, to be held at the Waldorf-Astoria on the evening of December 20th, will feature decorations of white roses and silver ribbons, with champagne supplied by the house of Veuve Clicquot.

The Arts

The Metropolitan's New Acquisition

By Theodore Blackwood

The Metropolitan Museum of Art has announced the acquisition of a remarkable collection of Art Deco jewelry, featuring pieces by Cartier, Van Cleef & Arpels, and Boucheron. The collection, donated by the estate of the late Mrs. Cornelius Vanderbilt III, represents one of the most significant gifts in the museum's history.

Highlights include a stunning diamond and emerald brooch, circa 1925, featuring the geometric motifs characteristic of the period. The piece exemplifies the bold, architectural approach to jewelry design that emerged in the years following the 1925 Exposition Internationale des Arts Décoratifs.

Paris Couture: The New Silhouette

By Marguerite Fontaine

From the ateliers of Paris comes news of the most extraordinary collection from the house of Lanvin. Madame Lanvin has unveiled a series of evening gowns that redefine elegance for the modern woman.

The new silhouette emphasizes fluidity and movement, with dropped waists and hemlines that fall just below the knee. Fabrics of silk charmeuse and velvet in jewel tones of emerald, sapphire, and ruby dominate the collection, accented with intricate beadwork and embroidery.

Accessories include long pearl necklaces, feathered headbands, and the ever-essential cigarette holder, now available in gold and enamel from the house of Tiffany & Co.
