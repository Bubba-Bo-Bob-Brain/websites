The Exhibition of the Century: Art Deco Takes Paris by Storm

The 1925 Exposition Internationale des Arts Décoratifs transforms the City of Light into a spectacle of geometric grandeur and unprecedented luxury

❧ By Édouard Beaumont, Paris Correspondent ❧
The Grand Palais transformed for the Exhibition, showcasing the finest in modern decorative arts

N the shadow of the Eiffel Tower, where the Seine flows past ancient quays now lined with motorcars and gentleman drivers in tailored driving coats, an extraordinary gathering has commenced. The Exposition Internationale des Arts Décoratifs et Industriels Modernes has opened its gilded doors to an astonished world, and we are privileged to bring our readers this first-hand account of what can only be described as the most magnificent exhibition ever staged upon this earth.

The finest architects, designers, and artisans of our age have contributed their greatest works to this celebration of modern elegance. From the moment one passes through the magnificent entrance arch—designed by the renowned René Lochet and faced entirely in bronze and colored marble—one is transported into a realm of unprecedented beauty.

The Pavilion of Electricity alone defies description. Its facade, a symphony of chrome and colored glass, pulses with an ethereal light that changes hue throughout the day, from the soft rose of dawn to the golden amber of sunset. Within, visitors witness the marvels of modern technology presented not as mere machinery, but as objects of art worthy of display in the finest salon.

◆

"We stand at the dawn of a new aesthetic era, one that shall define the tastes of generations to come."

— Sir William Lethaby, Exhibition Juror

The Furniture Pavilion draws crowds who marvel at the clean lines and luxurious materials on display. Gone are the excessive ornamentations of the Victorian era; in their place, we find a new vocabulary of form that speaks to our modern sensibilities while never sacrificing comfort or opulence.

Perhaps the most discussed exhibit is the collaboration between the houses of Cartier and the renowned architect Charles Garnier, who have created a jeweled fountain that is nothing short of miraculous. Thousands of genuine precious stones—diamonds, rubies, emeralds, and sapphires—adorn its surface, catching the light and scattering rainbows across the assembled guests.
