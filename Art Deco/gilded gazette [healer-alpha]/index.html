SOCIETY

Grand Ball at the Waldorf Marks Height of Social Season

By Vivienne Hartington | December 15th, 1923

T

he magnificent ballroom of the Waldorf-Astoria was transformed into a veritable palace of light and sound last Saturday evening, as the crème de la crème of New York society gathered for what many are calling the most spectacular event of the social season. Crystal chandeliers cast their warm glow upon an assembly of the most distinguished personages of our time, their jewels catching the light like scattered stars upon a midnight sky.

The evening commenced promptly at eight o'clock, with the arrival of the host and hostess, Mr. and Mrs. Reginald Worthington III, who received their guests beneath an archway of ten thousand white roses. The orchestra, imported specially from Vienna for the occasion, struck up a spirited waltz as couples took to the polished marble floor.

"
Never before has such a constellation of society's finest graced a single evening. It was, in every sense, a night for the ages.
— The New York Herald

Among the notable guests were the Duchess of Marlborough, looking resplendent in a gown of midnight blue silk adorned with Persian embroidery, and the celebrated artist Mr. Salvador Rivera, who remarked upon the exceptional taste displayed in the evening's decorations.

The supper, served at midnight, featured seventeen courses prepared by Chef Auguste Escoffier himself, including his legendary Peach Melba and a most extraordinary display of spun sugar artistry depicting the Manhattan skyline.

CULTURE

The Metropolitan Announces Spring Programme

The Metropolitan Opera has unveiled its ambitious programme for the coming spring season, promising audiences a feast of dramatic and musical excellence. Principal among the offerings is a new production of Wagner's Tristan und Isolde, featuring the legendary soprano Frau Helga von Trapp in what promises to be a career-defining performance.

Season tickets are already fully subscribed, though a limited number of single performance seats may be obtained through the box office.

FASHION

Paris Couturiers Embrace the Modern Woman

In the ateliers of Paris, a revolution in women's fashion is quietly unfolding. The great couturiers—Chanel, Patou, and Poiret—are leading the charge toward a new aesthetic of elegant simplicity, discarding the excessive ornamentation of the previous decade in favor of clean lines and luxurious fabrics that move with the body.

The modern woman, we are told, desires freedom of movement above all else.

❧ Letters & Opinion ❧

On the Matter of Prohibition

Sir, I write to express my profound disagreement with your recent editorial concerning the Volstead Act. While I share your concerns regarding public morality, I cannot but observe that the prohibition of spirits has done nothing to diminish the appetite for them amongst our citizens.

— A.B. Pemberton, Boston

Regarding Modern Architecture

Dear Editor, your correspondent's dismissal of the modern architectural movement betrays a regrettable lack of imagination. The steel frame and the elevator have liberated us from the tyranny of the past, allowing us to build not merely upwards, but onwards into a brighter future.

— C.R. Wright, Chicago

The Jazz Question

Madam, while I confess to enjoying the syncopated rhythms of jazz as much as the next person, I must question whether such music truly represents the highest aspirations of our civilization. There is, I believe, a difference between entertainment and art.

— Prof. H. Whitmore, Yale
