The Rise of the Jazz Age

By Langston Whitmore | May 12, 1925

THere is no mistaking the rhythm of the times. From the smoky speakeasies of New York to the grand ballrooms of Paris, jazz has taken the world by storm. It is the heartbeat of a generation that refuses to be silenced, a symphony of rebellion wrapped in velvet and gold.

In this issue, we explore the luminaries of the movement—Louis Armstrong’s trumpet, Duke Ellington’s piano, and the incomparable voice of Bessie Smith. But jazz is more than music; it is a way of life. It is the clink of a cocktail glass, the swish of a sequined dress, the daring defiance of a world still clinging to old morals.

Fashion: The New Silhouette

Gone are the corsets of yesteryear. The modern woman demands freedom—freedom of movement, freedom of expression. Designers like Coco Chanel and Jean Patou are leading the charge with drop-waist dresses, bold geometric patterns, and fabrics that shimmer like liquid gold.

Society: The Gatsby Effect

Jay Gatsby’s legendary parties have set the standard for extravagance. But what is the cost of such spectacle? As the champagne flows and the orchestra plays, one must wonder: is this the golden age of excess, or the twilight of an empire built on dreams?

Arts: Deco’s Golden Touch

Art Deco is more than a style—it is a philosophy. From the towering skyscrapers of Manhattan to the intricate designs of a cigarette case, symmetry and opulence reign supreme. Gold leaf, chrome, and lacquer are the materials of choice for those who seek to leave their mark on eternity.

The Speakeasy’s Secret Code

By Dorothy Langley | May 15, 1925

BEhind the unassuming doors of a New York brownstone lies a world of whispered passwords and clandestine pleasures. The speakeasy is the pulse of the Prohibition era, a place where the law is forgotten and the night comes alive with the clatter of dice and the murmur of illicit deals.

But how does one gain entry? The answer lies in the language of the underground. A nod to the doorman, a knowing phrase, and the door swings open to reveal a paradise of dim lights, velvet booths, and the sweet, smoky scent of contraband liquor.
