THE JAZZ AGE CHRONICLES

An Evening of Unparalleled Splendor

By Cornelia van der Luyden

There was music from my neighbor’s house through the summer nights. In his blue gardens men and girls came and went like moths among the whisperings and the champagne and the stars. The bar was in full swing, and floating rounds of cocktails permeated the garden outside, until the air was alive with chatter and laughter and casual innuendo and introductions forgotten on the spot.

The orchestra played a dazzling arrangement of “Ain’t We Got Fun,” and the syncopated rhythm seemed to animate the very champagne flutes. Women in beaded gowns and feathered headbands twirled beneath Japanese lanterns, while gentlemen in crisp white suits discussed the latest on Wall Street between sips of gin rickeys.

As the hour grew late, a tower of champagne coupes was erected in the center of the ballroom, and guests were invited to pour from the top, a cascade of golden bubbles that mirrored the extravagant spirit of the age. The host himself, a man of mysterious origins and impeccable taste, observed the revelry from the marble steps, a faint smile upon his lips.

Society Columns

I
The Vanderbilt Ball

Mrs. Cornelius Vanderbilt III hosted a Venetian masquerade at the Plaza. Guests arrived in gilded gondolas, and the ballroom was transformed into a replica of St. Mark’s Square.

II
Polo at Meadow Brook

The season opened with a thrilling match between the Wanderers and the Hurricanes. Tea was served on the lawn, and the new fashions in sportswear drew much admiration.

III
Cotton Club Nights

Duke Ellington’s orchestra premiered a new composition, “The Mooche,” to a rapturous reception. The floor show featured the celebrated dancer Florence Mills.

THE GALLERIES

Tamara de Lempicka
Exhibition at Stieglitz

The sharp geometry and luminous color of Lempicka’s portraits capture the modern woman with a precision that is both sensual and unyielding. Her latest works, on view at the Intimate Gallery, are a testament to the machine-age aesthetic that defines our era.

Alfred Stieglitz himself remarked that Lempicka’s paintings possess “a metallic sensuality that mirrors the soul of the modern metropolis.” The exhibition continues through January.

THEATRE

“Strange Interlude” — Eugene O’Neill’s daring nine-act drama continues to astonish audiences at the John Golden Theatre. Lynn Fontanne delivers a performance of searing intensity.

MUSIC

Gershwin’s Rhapsody in Blue performed by Paul Whiteman’s orchestra at Aeolian Hall. A triumphant fusion of jazz and classical forms that left the audience breathless.
