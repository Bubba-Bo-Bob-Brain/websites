Society

The Great Gatsby: A New Era of Excess

By Mrs. Evelyn Ashworth  •  Illustrated by Mr. Clarence

A

t the stroke of midnight, when the jazz band strikes its final chord and the champagne flows like the Nile, one cannot help but wonder—are we witnessing the pinnacle of civilization or the twilight of an age?

The mansions of Long Island glitter with a thousand electric lights, each window a porthole into a world of impossible wealth. Our reporter recently attended one such affair, hosted by a mysterious gentleman known only as "G."

"The party lasted until dawn, and still the guests refused to leave—enchanted by the music, the magic, the merriment."

From the moment one steps through those marble halls, the senses are overwhelmed. Crystal stemware clinks against golden trays. Women in beaded dresses swirl across dance floors of inlaid mahogany. The air itself seems to shimmer with possibility.

Yet beneath the laughter and lilies lies a question that haunts the thoughtful observer: How long can this golden dream last?

Fashion

The Flapper Revolution

By Miss Penelope Clarke

T

he bob is back, and with it, a revolution. Our young women have cast off the corsets of their mothers and embraced a new freedom—a freedom expressed in shorter hemlines and longer cigarettes.

At the Coco Chanel salon, the mood is electric. "We are not merely fashion," declares the renowned designer, "we are liberation." And liberate they have. From Paris to New York, the flapper has arrived, and she is here to stay.

The Season's Finest: Evening Wear

The boyish figure is in—flat chests and slim hips are the height of sophistication. Silk stockings with seams up the back are a must, and never, ever leave home without your compact and your cigarette holder.
