Midnight in the Garden of Opulence

When the clock strikes twelve, the libraries transform into temples of jazz, and the old money dances with the new

November 14, 1924 ◈ 12 min read

The green light across the bay pulses with the rhythm of the age—steady, insistent, promising. It is 1924, and the world has shed the skin of war like a snake emerging renewed, glistening and dangerous in the morning sun. In the grand estates of Long Island and the speakeasies of Manhattan, a new culture is being written in gin and jazz, in the rustle of beaded dresses and the roar of Duesenberg engines.

They call it the Jazz Age, though it is so much more than music. It is a state of mind, a refusal to look backward, a manic determination to seize the moment before it slips away into the darkness. The young have inherited the earth, and they intend to spend their inheritance quickly, gloriously, and with maximum volume.

"

It was an age of miracles, it was an age of art, it was an age of excess, and it was an age of satire.

— F. Scott Fitzgerald "

In the ballrooms of the Plaza, the Charleston has replaced the waltz. Women have sheared their locks and raised their hemlines, revealing silk stockings rolled just so. The corset is dead, killed by Coco Chanel and the desire to move, to breathe, to live without constraint. Men have abandoned the stuffy formality of their fathers for tuxedos that shine under crystal chandeliers, for collars that allow the neck to turn freely.

The champagne flows not in streams but in rivers. It is the elixir of the era, golden and effervescent, matching the mood of a generation determined to drink deeply from the cup of life before the party ends. And behind every great fortune, as the saying goes, is a crime—but in 1924, the crimes are overlooked in favor of the celebration.

[The Green Light]
