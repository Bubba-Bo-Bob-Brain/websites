Incident Report

ORIGINAL

On the morning of January 15th, 1947, the body of an unidentified woman was discovered in a vacant lot at the intersection of Norton Avenue and 39th Street, Leimert Park, Los Angeles.

The victim was found bisected at the waist, with the body drained of blood and posed deliberately. The mouth had been slit from ear to ear in what appeared to be a post-mortem mutilation.

Initial assessment suggests the crime was committed elsewhere. No blood was found at the discovery site. The body had been meticulously cleaned prior to placement, indicating premeditation and a disturbing level of control by the perpetrator.

The press has already begun referring to the victim as "The Black Dahlia." The case has drawn unprecedented public attention. Pressure from City Hall is mounting. We need answers — and we need them yesterday.

— Det. Jack Calloway, Badge #2847

Investigator's Notes

1/18 The precision of the bisection suggests medical knowledge. Consulted Dr. de River — agrees the cut was clean, possibly surgical.
1/22 Elizabeth's mother Phoebe confirmed ID via telegram. Devastated. Elizabeth came to LA in '43, drifted through circles of servicemen and nightlife.
1/29 The "Man in the Black Hat" — witness at Biltmore Hotel. Need to locate him. Elizabeth was seen leaving with him on the 9th.
2/5 The press is circling like vultures. Examiner printing every rumor they get. Chief wants this closed before it becomes a political liability.
2/12 Something doesn't add up. The cleanliness, the posing, the taunting letters — this is personal. Or it's a performance. Either way, he wants us watching.

Witness Statements

Testimonies recorded during the investigation. Each statement transcribed and filed under oath.

Statement of Betty Bersinger

Discovering Witness — Civilian

January 15, 1947 10:47 AM

I was walking with my four-year-old daughter along the vacant lot on Norton Avenue when I saw what I thought was a broken store window mannequin. I told my daughter to stay back while I went to look closer. As I got closer, I realized it was a real person — a woman, and she was — she was in terrible condition. I called out to a neighbor, Mrs. Gwinn, and she called the police. I've never seen anything so — I can't describe it. The woman was cut in half. Her face was pale, like porcelain. It was like something out of a nightmare.

Betty Bersinger Recorded by Det. J. Calloway

Statement of Robert Manley

Last Confirmed Contact — Civilian

January 16, 1947 2:15 PM

I met Elizabeth — "Beth" she liked to be called — in San Diego. We drove up to LA together on the 9th. She was supposed to meet someone at the Biltmore Hotel downtown, some fella she'd been seeing. I dropped her off at the Biltmore around 6:45 PM. She seemed fine, in good spirits. She was carrying a small suitcase, the kind you carry on. She kissed me on the cheek and said she'd call me. I never heard from her again. I swear to God I had nothing to do with what happened. I loved that girl. When I saw her name in the paper — I couldn't believe it. I called the police myself.

Robert "Red" Manley Recorded by Det. J. Calloway

Statement of Phoebe Short

Mother of Victim — Long Beach, CA

January 17, 1947 9:30 AM

Elizabeth came to California in the summer of 1943. She was only 19. She wanted to see the world, meet people. She had this dream of being in pictures — not an uncommon thing for a girl out here, I suppose. She was always a good girl, gentle. She wouldn't hurt a fly. I don't know who could have done this to my baby. I've told the police everything I know. She mentioned a man once — a man in a black hat, she called him. She never gave me a name. I should have asked more questions. I should have —

Phoebe Short Recorded by Det. H. Hansen

Statement of Mark Hansen

Building Owner / Acquaintance

January 20, 1947 11:00 AM

Elizabeth stayed at my building on Norton Avenue briefly — just a few days. I ran the place, yes, and I knew most of the tenants. She was quiet, kept to herself. She told me she was waiting for someone to pick her up. I never saw who. She had visitors sometimes — men, always men. She never introduced them. I can tell you she was involved with some rough crowd. I heard things — names I didn't recognize, raised voices through the walls. I told her she needed to be careful out here. She just smiled and said she knew how to take care of herself.

Mark Hansen Recorded by Det. J. Calloway
