CASE BRIEFING

O

n the evening of November 8th, 1947, wealthy industrialist EDMUND BLACKWOOD was found dead in his study at the Blackwood Manor estate. Initial investigation ruled it a suicide, but circumstances suggest otherwise.

The victim was a prominent figure in the city's shipping industry, with alleged connections to organized crime. His death comes just days before a crucial Senate hearing on wartime contract irregularities.

Three suspects have emerged from the investigation. Each has motive, means, and opportunity. The truth lies somewhere in the shadows of deception.

DETECTIVE'S NOTES

The way this case smells... ain't natural. Someone's pulling strings, and I aim to find out who's dancing on the other end.

Blackwood wasn't the type to cap himself. Too stubborn. Too mean. Someone wanted him quiet.

The question is: what did he know? And who's willing to kill to keep it buried?

— Marlowe

EVIDENCE PHOTOGRAPHS

Seventeen pieces of photographic evidence have been collected. Five are presented here as most relevant to the investigation.

EXHIBIT A
#1 The study where Blackwood was found

Note the position of the body. The angle suggests the weapon was held by someone standing, not sitting as a suicide would indicate.

EXHIBIT B
#2 The murder weapon — .38 caliber revolver

No fingerprints on the weapon. Wiped clean. Professional job. Weapon traced to a 1942 military contract — same batch Webb distributed.

EXHIBIT C
#3 Anonymous letter received Nov 1st

Handwriting analysis inconclusive. Paper stock traced to City Hall supply room. Someone with government access wrote this.

EXHIBIT D
#4 Blackwood's safe — forced open after death

Contents missing. Safe combination was known only to Blackwood and his lawyer. Documents inside related to wartime contracts.

EXHIBIT E
#5 Footprints near servant's entrance

Size 11 dress shoes. Unusual tread pattern. Match boots recovered from Marcus Webb's office closet.

WITNESS STATEMENTS

WITNESS #1
MRS. ELEANOR FINCH
Housekeeper, Blackwood Manor
ST-2471-001
I found him there, just sitting in his chair with his head... oh Lord, I can't... I heard the gunshot from my quarters at quarter past ten. I ran immediately. The door was unlocked. He was still warm when I touched his hand. There was a letter on his desk — he had been writing when... when it happened. I called the police right away. No, wait... I called Mr. Crane first. Yes, that's right. I called him because he was like family to Mr. Blackwood. He told me to call the police. He was here within fifteen minutes.
DETECTIVE'S NOTE: She called Crane BEFORE police. Interesting priorities for a woman in shock.
WITNESS #2
JIMMY "THE RAILS" DONOVAN
Taxi Driver
ST-2471-002
Yeah, I picked up a fare near the Manor around nine-thirty that night. Big guy, expensive coat. He wanted to go to the train station but changed his mind halfway. Made me drive around for an hour. Back streets mostly. Wouldn't say much, just stared out the window like he was thinking hard about something. Paid me double in cash and told me to forget I ever picked him up. Funny thing is, I recognized him from the papers. Councilman Webb. What's a man like that doing sneaking around in cabs at night?
DETECTIVE'S NOTE: Webb admits to being in the city. Claims he arrived the morning after. This contradicts his alibi.
WITNESS #3
"BONES" MCCAULEY
Bouncer, Blue Velvet Club
ST-2471-003
That night? Yeah, Mr. Crane was there alright. Came in around eight, sat at the corner table, ordered whiskey. Left around eleven. I remember because Lola was just starting her midnight show and he missed it. Said he had somewhere to be. Now that I think about it, he got a phone call around ten-thirty. Went outside to take it. Came back looking nervous, like he'd seen a ghost. Paid his tab and split. Never seen him leave in such a hurry.
DETECTIVE'S NOTE: Crane had time to get to the Manor before Mrs. Finch found the body. The phone call is unaccounted for.
