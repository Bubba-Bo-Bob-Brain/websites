Case Summary

It was a dark and stormy night when the dame walked in...

The city's been gripped by fear. Three prominent citizens have vanished without a trace. The police are baffled, but I've been following the threads that connect them all.

What started as simple missing persons cases has unraveled into something far more sinister. A conspiracy that reaches the highest offices of this city. Corruption. Blackmail. Murder.

The evidence points to a shadow organization operating out of the Midnight Club. But proving it? That's where things get complicated...

- Det. Jack Marlowe

❧ Witness Statements ❧

👤

Rosie Malone

Waitress at The Midnight Club

CREDIBLE

"I seen plenty in my years workin' at the club, but that night was different. Mrs. Blackwood, she was there with one of the missin' men - Mr. Henderson. They were arguin' in the corner booth. I couldn't hear everything, but I heard her say somethin' about 'the money' and 'it wasn't supposed to happen like this.' She was scared, real scared. Then this tall man in a dark coat came over and they both went quiet. He slipped somethin' to Mr. Henderson, and they both left separately within the hour. That was the last time I saw Mr. Henderson alive."

Taken: Nov 12, 1947 Officer: Det. J. Marlowe
👤

Thomas Finch

Night Watchman, Harbor District

VERIFIED

"I was makin' my rounds at the old warehouse on 5th when I seen 'em. Three men carryin' somethin' heavy into a black sedan. It was about 2 in the mornin'. I called out, and one of 'em shined a light in my eyes. Couldn't see their faces, but I seen the car - a 1946 Buick, black as night. Had a dent on the left fender. They drove off fast toward the docks. Next mornin', I read about Mr. Morrison's disappearance. His car was found at that very warehouse. I reported it to Detective Cole, but he told me to forget what I saw. Said it was just kids. But those weren't no kids."

Taken: Nov 10, 1947 Officer: Det. J. Marlowe
👤

Dr. Eleanor Hayes

Physician, City General Hospital

ANONYMOUS

"I probably shouldn't be speakin' to you, but this has gone too far. Three weeks ago, a man was brought into the ER - gunshot wound. Before I could examine him properly, two men in suits came in claimin' to be police. They had badges, but somethin' didn't feel right. They took the man, said they were transferrin' him. I never saw him again, and there's no record of him ever bein' there. The man was conscious for a moment. He grabbed my arm and whispered 'Midnight Club... they're watching.' I've been too afraid to come forward until now. Please, you have to stop them."

Taken: Nov 13, 1947 Officer: Det. J. Marlowe
