CASE OVERVIEW

REF: HOM-47-1109 RESTRICTED ACCESS

On the evening of November 9th, 1947, the body of Raymond Sterling — industrialist, philanthropist, and chairman of Sterling Munitions Corp — was discovered in his private office on the 14th floor of the Sterling Building, downtown.

Initial assessment: single gunshot wound to the temple. Weapon recovered at scene — .38 Smith & Wesson, registered to the victim. The placement was wrong. The angle was wrong. This was no suicide.

Sterling had been preparing to testify before the Senate Committee on War Profiteering. His ledger — the one they never found — would have exposed a network spanning from the docks to city hall. Men with everything to lose were circling.

Four persons of interest have been identified. Three witnesses have provided statements. The evidence tells a story, but the threads don't weave straight. Someone is pulling them from the shadows.

The deeper I dig, the more this city pushes back. Someone wants this case closed. Someone wants me off it. But Sterling deserves justice, and I don't know how to quit.

— Det. J. Callahan, Homicide Division

PERSONS OF INTEREST

PID-001

VIVIENNE MOREAU

"The Velvet Widow"
AGE 32
OCCUPATION Nightclub Owner
CONNECTION Sterling's Mistress

Former torch singer at The Crimson Lounge. Inherited the club under suspicious circumstances following previous owner's "accidental" death. Last person seen with Sterling the night he died. She claims they argued and she left at midnight. The doorman tells a different story.

THREAT LEVEL
PID-002

FRANK CASTELLANO

"The Chairman"
AGE 51
OCCUPATION Business Partner
CONNECTION Co-Founder, Sterling Munitions

Sterling's partner of twenty years. Stood to gain full control of the company upon Sterling's death. Recently took out a $2M life insurance policy on the deceased. Has connections to the Castellano crime family of the east side. His alibi is a card game — none of the players will talk.

THREAT LEVEL
PID-003

FATHER THOMAS BRENNAN

"The Confessor"
AGE 58
OCCUPATION Parish Priest
CONNECTION Sterling's Confidant

Parish priest at St. Jude's — the church Sterling attended and lavishly funded. Heard Sterling's confession two days before the murder. Claims sealer of confession prevents him from speaking. But his church received a $50,000 donation the day after the body was found. Faith has its price.

THREAT LEVEL
PID-004

"SLICK" EDDIE MORANO

"The Fixer"
AGE 39
OCCUPATION Known Associate
CONNECTION Enforcer / Cleaner

Career criminal with ties to every racket on the waterfront. Suspected of at least six contract jobs but never convicted. His fingerprints were found on the windowsill of Sterling's office — on the 14th floor. Claims he was never there. The prints say otherwise.

THREAT LEVEL

EVIDENCE LOCKER

EXHIBIT A

.38 SMITH & WESSON REVOLVER

Recovered at scene, positioned in victim's right hand. Victim was left-handed. Serial numbers partially filed — traceable to a shipment delivered to Castellano Shipping, 1945.

ANOMALY DETECTED EV-001
EXHIBIT B

STERLING LEDGER — RED ENTRIES

Hidden compartment in desk drawer. Contains records of off-book transactions, bribes, and payoffs. Several entries reference "Operation Vermilion." Names are coded but cross-reference with known aliases.

KEY EVIDENCE EV-002
EXHIBIT C

TORN PHOTOGRAPH

Found in fireplace, partially burned. Shows Sterling at a gathering with Castellano and an unidentified third figure. The missing half would show who else was in that room. Someone didn't want this face seen.

PARTIAL RECOVERY EV-003
EXHIBIT D

MATCHBOOK — THE CRIMSON LOUNGE

Found in Sterling's coat pocket. Three matches used. Phone number scrawled inside cover in feminine handwriting — matches a disconnected line. The Crimson Lounge is owned by Vivienne Moreau.

LOGGED EV-004
EXHIBIT E

CODED TELEGRAM

Intercepted at Western Union office. Reads: "SPARROW FLIES AT MIDNIGHT. NEST MUST BE CLEANED. VERMILION PROCEEDS." Sent from an anonymous source three days before the murder. Cipher matches wartime intelligence protocols.

DECRYPTED EV-005
EXHIBIT F

BLOODY HANDKERCHIEF

Discovered in building incinerator room, missed by whoever tried to destroy it. Monogrammed "V.M." — Vivienne Moreau. Blood type matches victim. Lipstick stain in Vermilion shade. She was in that room.

FORENSIC MATCH EV-006

WITNESS DEPOSITIONS

MRS. ADA PRESCOTT

Neighbor — Sterling Building, Apt 14B
STATEMENT TAKEN NOV 10, 1947
DEPOSITION TRANSCRIPT — REF: WIT-001
"I've lived across the hall from Mr. Sterling for six years. Quiet man, kept to himself mostly. But that night — I heard voices. Not arguing, not exactly. It was... deliberate. Like a negotiation. Then a door slammed. Footsteps in the hall, quick ones, going toward the elevator. I looked through the peephole. A woman — dark hair, red coat — she was in a hurry. Didn't see her face proper. But I smelled her perfume. Gardenias and something else... something chemical. Never forgot it. Then, maybe ten minutes later, another sound. Heavier footsteps. A man. He took the stairs."
CREDIBILITY ASSESSMENT
Reliable — no known affiliations, consistent testimony

JIMMY "EYES" MORETTI

Night Watchman — Sterling Building
STATEMENT TAKEN NOV 10, 1947
DEPOSITION TRANSCRIPT — REF: WIT-002
"I was making my rounds at quarter to eight, like always. Door to Mr. Sterling's office was open — which ain't right, he always locks it. I called out, nothing. Called out again, still nothing. So I went in. He was slumped in his chair, and I... I've seen dead men before. The war. But this was different. His eyes were open, like he was looking at something on his desk. I didn't touch nothing. Called it in right away. But the first cops on scene — they moved things around before the detectives arrived. One of them pocketed something off the desk. I saw him. He saw me seeing him. That's when I got the visit. Two men. Told me maybe I didn't see nothing that night. Maybe I was mistaken. I get the message. But I'm telling you this because I can't sleep no more."
CREDIBILITY ASSESSMENT
HIGH VALUE — testimony suggests evidence tampering by officers

DOLORES SANTIAGO

Domestic Staff — Sterling Residence
STATEMENT TAKEN NOV 12, 1947
DEPOSITION TRANSCRIPT — REF: WIT-003
"I clean the house every Tuesday and Friday. Mr. Sterling, he was a tidy man, but that week he was different. Nervous. He had me wash all the windows, even the ones that were already clean. The day before... before it happened... he called me into his study. Gave me an envelope. Told me if anything happened to him, I should open it. I didn't. I was too scared. But then the police came, and they searched everything. I hid the envelope. It's still hidden. He told me to give it to someone who would ask the right questions. You're the first one who's asked the right questions, detective."
CREDIBILITY ASSESSMENT
CRITICAL — possesses undisclosed evidence, potential game-changer
