Evidence Gallery

What the dead left behind — and what we found

EVIDENCE #1

Crime scene — study

The Study

Pemberton's private study. Door locked from inside. Window open — rain entered. Bourbon glass, half-empty. A cigarette burned to the filter in the ashtray.

EVIDENCE #2

Personal ledger

The Ledger

Pemberton's personal accounts. Three names marked with red ink: Ashcroft, Mancini, Rosalind. Payments totalling $47,000 to unknown recipients over six months.

EVIDENCE #3

Cufflinks — sterling silver

Cufflinks

Sterling silver, monogrammed "H.P." Found on the windowsill. Not Pemberton's — he wore gold. Size suggests a woman's.

EVIDENCE #4

Burned matchbook

Matchbook

The Velvet Note — a jazz club. Single match used. Found in Pemberton's breast pocket. Matches a book on Dolores Vane's dressing table.

EVIDENCE #5

Prescription bottle

Prescription

Dr. Marsh prescribed Veronal, 200mg. Bottle found empty. Dosage was recently doubled. Pemberton's toxicology report pending.

EVIDENCE #6

Harbor district map

Harbor Map

Pemberton's hand-drawn map of dock shipments. Annotations in code. Matches Mancini's known delivery routes. A warehouse circled twice.

Witness Statements

Everyone saw something. Nobody's telling the whole truth.

STATEMENT #001 March 14, 1947 — 6:15 AM

Mae Tolliver, Housekeeper

I went to bring his morning coffee. The study door was locked. I could hear... nothing. I called out but got no answer. I went to get the spare key from Mr. Ashcroft's office. When I came back, the door was open. Mr. Pemberton was slumped over his desk.

I didn't touch anything. I swear on my mother's grave. But I did notice the window was open and the rain had come in. There were papers all over the floor.

STATEMENT #002 March 14, 1947 — 8:30 AM

Officer Danny Reeves, First Respondent

Got the call at half six. When I arrived, the front door was wide open. Mrs. Tolliver was in the hallway, white as a sheet. The study looked like a hurricane had hit it. Papers everywhere. The dead man hadn't fallen — he was sitting upright, like he'd just... stopped.

I checked the window. No signs of entry. The rain had been coming in for hours. There was a half-empty glass on the desk — bourbon, by the smell of it. One cigarette burned all the way down. And there, on the windowsill, a pair of silver cufflinks.

STATEMENT #003 March 14, 1947 — 10:00 AM

Bartender, The Velvet Note

Dolores Vane came in around midnight. She was drinking doubles — gin, not bourbon. She was upset. Cried into her drink for twenty minutes, then pulled herself together and left. Didn't say where she was going.

Two hours later, a man matching the description of Pemberton came in. Sat in the back booth. Ordered the same thing — doubles. Sat there until closing. I didn't see him leave. The next morning, I heard what happened.

STATEMENT #004 March 15, 1947 — 2:00 AM

Dock Worker (anonymous)

I saw a car at the warehouse on 4th and Vine around midnight. Dark sedan, no plates. Two people got out. One was tall — could've been a woman in a coat. The other was shorter. They went inside. I didn't stick around. I've got a family.

That warehouse belongs to Mancini's crew. Everyone knows that. Whatever deal Pemberton was trying to make, it went down there.

STATEMENT #005 March 15, 1947 — 9:00 AM

Rosalind Pemberton, Wife of the Deceased

I was asleep. I was asleep in our bedroom and I heard nothing. The police say the murder happened between midnight and two. I was in bed the entire time. Victor Ashcroft can confirm — he stopped by to discuss the will and saw me playing piano when he left.

I loved my husband. Whatever they're saying about me is lies. I have nothing to do with his death. I want the person who did this brought to justice.

Detective's Case Notes

Handwritten — for my eyes only

March 14, 1947 11:42 PM

The body was found at 6 AM. Pemberton, 54, sitting upright at his desk as if he'd just paused mid-thought. No visible wounds. The study door was locked from the inside — chain bolt on the inside. Window open. Rain came in.

Bourbon glass, half-empty. Cigarette burned to nothing. Silver cufflinks on the sill — not his. A woman's, maybe.

I'm pulling every thread I can. This one's going to unravel fast.

March 15, 1947 3:15 AM

Dolores Vane is gone. Checked The Velvet Note — no one's seen her since midnight. Bartender says she was crying into her gin. Then Pemberton showed up two hours later. They knew each other. That much is clear.

The ledger is the key. Three names in red. Ashcroft for the money. Mancini for the muscle. Rosalind for the motive that doesn't need explaining.

But someone wanted this quiet. The locked door, the chain bolt — this was staged. Pemberton didn't do this to himself.

March 16, 1947 9:00 AM

Dr. Marsh's records show the Veronal dosage was doubled six weeks ago. Pemberton was sleeping poorly. Or someone wanted him sleeping.

Checked the warehouse on 4th and Vine. Empty now. But the floor was recently cleaned — bleach and sawdust. Someone's covering tracks.

The rain washed away footprints outside the study window. Whoever did this knew the weather forecast.

March 17, 1947 1:30 AM

Found Mancini's car at the bus station. Plates removed. He's gone — slipped out of town. But he left a cigarette case in the glove compartment. Inside: a photograph of Pemberton and a note that reads —

« He owed us more than money. »

This is bigger than one dead man. I'm going to need backup.

March 18, 1947 ???:???

I've connected the threads. The truth is in the ledger, the ledger is in the study, and the study is where it all started. One of them knows I'm close.

If you're reading this — follow the red thread. It leads to the truth. Or to the next body.

— J. Calloway
