The Prophecy of Ashen Crowns

──── ✦

When the seventh moon turns to blood and the ancient wards dissolve like morning frost upon the Blackthorn Gates, then shall the Hollow King stir from his slumber beneath the Obsidian Throne.

The rivers shall run backward to their source. The wheat fields shall birth thorns. And the children of Mordenthvale shall forget the warmth of sunlight upon their skin.

Three tokens shall mark the end: the weeping stone, the crown of briars, and the last ember dying in the mouth of a raven.

Only the Unmarked Child, born of two bloodlines long thought extinct, shall hold the key to unmake what darkness has made — or bind the world to shadow eternal.

— From the Ashen Codex, Page 777, inscribed by the Blind Seer Thessaly

The Last Grimoire

──── ✦

The final personal journal of Arch-Scholar Elara Voss, recovered from the ruins of the Great Library. The final pages are stained with something the investigators refused to identify.

— 47 —

14th of Frostfall, Year 1347

The wards have failed. I write this knowing it may be the last coherent thought committed to paper in this cursed city. The sound from beneath the Throne grows louder — not a roar, but a breathing, vast and patient, as if the earth itself has developed lungs.

Aldric was right about the prophecy. We were fools to dismiss it as superstition. The signs were there for decades — the backward rivers, the black wheat, the children who stopped laughing. We chose comfort over truth, and now truth comes to collect its debt with interest.

I have hidden the key where only the Unmarked Child will find it. If you are reading this, young one, know that you did not choose this burden — but it is yours now, and yours alone. The darkness remembers you. It has always remembered you.

— 48 —

15th of Frostfall, Year 1347

It is here. Not beneath the Throne anymore — above it. The sky has torn open like wet parchment, and something looks down through the wound with eyes that are not eyes but absences, voids where light goes to die.

The others have stopped speaking. They stand at the windows, their faces slack, their mouths open, drinking in the dark air. I will not look up. I will not look up. I will not —

The ink is running. Or perhaps it is raining upward. I can no longer tell which way the world turns. If the child comes, tell them: the key is in the song their mother sang. The melody none of us could remember but all of us heard.

The remaining pages are blank, save for a single dark handprint pressed against the final leaf, fingers splayed in what might be desperation — or worship.
