Святая Книга The Slavic Bestiary

A Compendium of the Beasts & Spirits of the Deep Forest, Dark Waters & Hearthside Shadows

Illuminated by the Keeper of Old Ways

Copied in the Year of the Zhar-Ptitsa

He who reads these pages does so at their own peril. The creatures herein are not fables.

О Господстве Чудес

В начале создан Богом мир видимый и невидимый, и населил его твари всякие: великие и малые, летающие и ползающие, ходячие и водяные. И явились сие творения тайные, сокрытые от очей humanных, и обретают они обитель свою в чащах дремучих, в глубинах водных, в огнях печных, и в тьмах подземных.

In the beginning, God created the visible and invisible world, and filled it with creatures great and small — those that fly, crawl, walk, and dwell in water. And there appeared among these creations beings hidden from mortal eyes, dwelling in deep forests, dark waters, hearthside flames, and the shadows beneath the earth.

Let the reader beware, for not all that walks in shadow is of God's making, and not all that dwells in light is safe from the ancient powers.

СодержаниеContents

Forty and nine beings are herein catalogued, though others wander unnamed in the dark between the trees.

Fig. I.
Beast 🌲 Forest

Леший

Leshy · Lesnik · He-Who-Watches-From-Behind-Firs

The Forest Lord

The Leshy is the sovereign spirit of the deep forest, ancient beyond reckoning and vast as the oldest oak. He appears as a man of terrible height, seven feet or taller, though he may shrink to the height of a blade of grass or swell to the size of the tallest pine. His body is bark and moss, his antlers branch toward the canopy, and his eyes glow with the pale green phosphorescence of foxfire. His beard is lichen; his breath, the wind through dead pines.

He is the shepherd of wild beasts and the guardian of all that grows without the hand of man. To enter his domain without his leave is to invite madness — for the Leshy delights in leading travellers in circles, deeper and deeper into the wood, until they forget their own names and become beasts themselves.

Свидания и Предостережения
  • He wears a crown of living wood and walks without sound, though he is as tall as the birches.
  • His right shoe is on his left foot, and his left shoe upon his right — a sign to those who know.
  • He may be appeased with an offering of bread and salt left upon a stump at the forest's edge.
  • Those who make a pact with the Leshy are granted dominion over the animals of the wood, but they may never leave the forest alive.
  • When the trees whisper in a wind that does not blow, the Leshy is near.
Заклинание Против Заблуждения

"Лешый, Лешый, отпусти меня!
Хлеб тебе, соль тебе, да не гонишься за мной.
Колодец на дороге, печь на тропе —
туда иди, а не за мной."

"Leshy, Leshy, release me! Bread for you, salt for you, do not chase after me. A well upon the road, a stove upon the path — go there, not after me."

Alternate Names Леший, Лесник, Мифолог, Лес-Человёк
Known Habitats Pine forests, ancient groves, birch stands untouched by axe
Active Hours All night; never seen in full daylight
Weakness His head — if separated, his power vanishes. Bread and salt.
Season Most powerful at the summer and winter solstices

Fig. II.
Spirit 🌊 Water

Русалка

Rusalka

The Drowning Maiden

The Rusalka is the restless spirit of a woman who died by water — whether by drowning, murder, or her own hand beneath the willows. She is not merely a ghost but something transformed: her death in water has remade her into something that is part revenant, part elemental, wholly terrible.

By night she rises from the river, her wet hair trailing behind her like black weeds, her eyes luminous and her voice carrying across the water like a song no man can resist. She does not merely kill — she draws the living into the water to join her in death's cold embrace.

The Rusalka week — the first week after Trinity — is when she is most dangerous. The villages tie ribbons upon the birches and cast them into the water to appease her, and no maiden dares to bathe until the week is passed.

Свидания и Предостережения
  • She is most often seen combing her hair upon the riverbank with a golden or bone comb.
  • Her song is irresistible to men; those who follow it never return.
  • If a Rusalka's hair dries upon her head, she will die — this is why she is forever drawn to water.
  • She may be freed from her torment if someone avenges her death or if she completes an unrighteous task from her living days.
  • She can appear as a beautiful maiden, a pale horse, or a great white fish.
Заговор Против Русалок

"Русалочкa, Русалочкa,
мыли голову серою водою,
мыли яички серою водою,
по камешкам сиди,
по кочкам сушись."

"Rusalka, Rusalka, wash your head with grey water, wash your eggs with grey water, sit upon the stones, dry upon the stones."

Alternate Names Мавка, Водяница, Немилая
Known Habitats Rivers, lakes, millpools, deep forest springs
Active Hours Midnight until the first cockcrow
Weakness Blessed herbs, the sign of the cross, rye bread with salt
Season Rusalka Week (early June); full moons

Fig. III.
Witch 🌲 Forest

Баба-Яга

Baba Yaga · Baba-Ya·ga · Bone Mother

The Iron-toothed Hag

Baba Yaga is no mere witch — she is the boundary between the civilised world and the primordial dark. She dwells in a hut that stands upon great chicken legs and turns constantly to face the forest, so that no traveller may find it without her will. Around it winds a palisade of human bones topped with skulls whose eye-sockets blaze with ghostly fire.

Her nose is hooked iron, her teeth are bone, and her preferred method of travel is a mortar which she steers with a pestle, sweeping away her tracks with a birch-broom. She flies through the forest with the speed of tempest wind. She is neither wholly evil nor wholly good — she is ancient necessity, the test that all heroes must face.

Those who approach her hut with proper respect and correct gifts may receive wisdom, healing, or magical aid. Those who come unprepared are ground in her mortar and bones, or — worse — made to scrub her hut and become her servants forever.

Свидания и Предостережения
  • Her fence is made of human bones: legs, arms, and skulls with burning eyes.
  • She keeps three horsemen as servants: the white rider (Day), the red rider (Sun), and the dark rider (Night).
  • She may be summoned by turning one's clothes inside-out and whistling at a crossroads at midnight.
  • Offerings of porridge, ribbons, and gin secure her favour.
  • Those she eats become part of her forest — their spirits tend her bone-garden.
Заклинание К Бабе-Яге

"Бабушка-Ёжка, бабушка,
дай мне поесть, по-поить,
помоги мне, подскажи мне,
да направь меня верным путём."

"Grandmother Hedgehog, grandmother, give me to eat and drink, help me, advise me, and guide me on the right path."

Alternate Names Бабка Ежкина, Костяная Матерь, Железнозубая
Known Habitats Deepest forest, crossroads, the space between worlds
Active Hours Midnight, twilight, and the dark of the moon
Weakness Proper manners, porridge, iron, the sign of the cross
Season All year; strongest at the equinoxes

Fig. IV.
Dragon 💀 Underworld

Змей Горыныч

Zmey · Zmey Gorynych · Serpent of the Mountains

The Three-headed Dragon

The Zmey is the dread serpent of Slavic myth, a creature of fire and malice whose coils encircle the roots of the world. The greatest of them, Zmey Gorynych, bears three heads — each capable of breathing fire, ice, or venom — and may grow a new head for each one that is severed, if the stump is not cauterised with hot pitch.

He kidnaps maidens and princesses, guards treasures beneath mountains, and defiles the land wherever he dwells. His scales are harder than iron, his blood burns like molten copper, and his roar topples towers. Yet every Zmey has a weakness — a vulnerable spot beneath a scale, an enchanted sword that alone can pierce his heart, or a riddle whose answer he cannot resist.

It is said that the great hero Dobrynya Nikitich defeated the Zmey Gorynych upon the banks of the Don, not through strength alone but through cunning, driving the serpent into an enchanted sleep before binding him with magic chains.

Свидания и Предостережения
  • Three heads that breathe fire, frost, and pestilence.
  • Each head may speak a different language of magic.
  • His blood is so toxic that the earth where it spills becomes barren for seven years.
  • He hoards gold beneath mountains and will destroy any village above his treasure.
  • Only a hero marked by destiny — or a maiden of pure heart — may slay him.
Заговор Против Змея

"Змей-змей, тройноглый змей,
огненный, морозный, ядовитый,
не ходи по земле моей,
не лезь за облака моими.
С камня на камень, с горы на гору,
иди туда, не знаю куда."

"Zmey, Zmey, three-headed serpent, fiery, freezing, and venomous — walk not upon my land, reach not beyond my skies. From stone to stone, from mountain to mountain, go hence to where I know not."

Alternate Names Змей Горыныч, Змей Огненный, Змей-Горбун
Known Habitats Mountain caves, underground lairs, volcanic fissures
Active Hours Dawn and dusk; never sleeps fully
Weakness Cauterised stumps, the Баба-Яга's aid, enchanted swords
Season Spring thaw when underground fires stir

Fig. V.
Spirit 🏠 Hearthside

Домовой

Domovoi · Домовой · House Elder

The Hearth Guardian

The Domovoi is the ancestral spirit of the household — a small, bearded old man covered in hair, who dwells behind the stove or beneath the threshold. He is the oldest member of the family, older even than grandfather, for he remembers the house before it was built, when the forest stood where the hearth now stands.

He protects the home and its livestock, feeds the fire at night, and warns the family of danger by wailing, clanging pots, or appearing as the family cat or dog. When the house is prosperous, he sings at night. When offended, he howls like a wolf and extinguishes every candle.

To welcome a Domovoi into a new home, the family must carry the household idol or a coal from the old hearth, and set the table with bread and salt for the invisible guest. Neglect of this custom ensures misfortune.

Свидания и Предостережения
  • He appears as a small old man with a long grey beard, or as the family cat.
  • His footsteps in the attic at night mean all is well; silence means trouble.
  • He is appeased by a bowl of fresh milk left by the hearthstone.
  • Swearing in the house, especially near the stove, enrages him.
  • He will pull the hair of lazy children and spoil the work of slovenly housewives.
Призыв Домового

"Домовой, домовой,
ты мой старый дедушка,
ходи по дому, не засирай углы,
ходи по горшку, да по кринице,
да ходи по всему дому-сену."

"Domovoi, domovoi, you are my old grandfather — walk through the house, don't neglect the corners, walk through the chambers, walk through the well, walk through the whole house and granary."

Alternate Names Хозяин Дома, Дед-Домовик, Странник
Known Habitats Under stoves, thresholds, attics, barns
Active Hours Night, especially between midnight and cockcrow
Weakness Knives left blade-up, profanity, cold hearths
Season Year-round; strongest at Christmas Eve (Kolyada)

Threat: High
Fig. VI.
Spirit 🌊 Water

Водяной

Vodyanoy · Водяной · The Wet One

The Old Man of the Waters

The Vodyanoy is the ancient spirit of lakes, rivers, and millpools — an old man with a frog-green face, a beard of living algae, and eyes like lanterns glowing beneath the water. He is fat-bellied and powerful, with webbed hands and a long tail, and he rules his underwater kingdom with the authority of a drowned tsar.

Fishermen know better than to cast their nets without his permission. He drowns those who trespass in his waters, breaks mill-wheels that displease him, and drags the unwary beneath the surface to his halls of mud and river-stone. Yet a wise fisherman who leaves the first catch upon the bank will find his nets forever full.

Свидания и Предостережения
  • He walks the banks at night, dragging the river behind him like a cloak.
  • He sits on millstones beneath the water, spinning the currents.
  • Offerings of tobacco, bread, and strong drink left on the water's edge earn his favour.
  • He may teach fishermen the best spots in exchange for respect.
  • His laughter sounds like bubbles rising from deep water.
Заговор К Водяному

"Водяной-дедушка, ядовитый брат,
ты в своём доме, я — в своём.
я в своём доме безбожная головушка,
даю тебе рыбку, даю тебе раков,
даю тебе всего приплода.

"Old man Vodyanoy, venomous brother, you are in your house and I in mine. In my house I am an unbelieving head — I give you fish, I give you crayfish, I give you all the catch."

Alternate Names Водяной Дед, Шут, Болотный Царь
Known Habitats Deep pools, river bends, millpools, lake bottoms
Active Hours Night; especially new moons
Weakness Blessed bread, the sign of the cross, tobacco
Season Spring floods and late autumn

Fig. VII.
Spirit 🏠 Hearthside

Кикимора

Kikimora · Кикимора · The Weaver of Misfortune

The Flaxen Hag

Born from the feathers of a deceased owl and the flax-seed dust of an unwed maiden's spindle, the Kikimora is a creature of twilight mischief and domestic spite. No taller than a loaf of bread, she haunts the corners of homes, barns, and bathhouses, spinning webs of ill fortune wherever she dwells.

When a Kikimora inhabits a home, the sourdough refuses to rise, the butter refuses to churn, and the chickens cease to lay. She tangles the wool of spinners, causes milk to curdle, and fills the nights with the sound of her tiny loom — a portent of grief.

Yet she is not wholly malicious. If treated with respect, offered a saucer of milk and a corner of warm bread, she may become a protector of the household, spinning fortune rather than ruin.

Свидания и Предостережения
  • She leaves wet footprints wherever she walks.
  • If you hear her spinning at night, cover your face with your shirt and recite prayers.
  • She is the sworn enemy of the Domovoi, and the two spirits are often in conflict.
  • A Kikimora in a barn foretells the death of cattle.
  • She can be driven out with juniper smoke and the blessing of a priest.
Заговор Против Кикиморы

"Кикимора, Кикимора,
птичка совушья дочка,
иди отсюда, не мешай,
в баньку свою беги,
там свой веретено верти."

"Kikimora, Kikimora, daughter of the owl — go from here, don't trouble us, run to your bathhouse, spin your spindle there."

Alternate Names Шереника, Шелезница, Овинница
Known Habitats Bathhouses, barns, cellars, behind hearths
Active Hours Night; especially while spinning
Weakness Juniper smoke, rooster's crow, blessed bread
Origin Feathers of a dead owl + unwoven flax dust

Fig. VIII.
Celestial ☁️ Sky

Сирин

Sirin

The Bird of Paradise

The Sirin is a being of unbearable beauty and unbearable sorrow — half-woman, half-bird, with the face of a maiden lost in celestial rapture and the body of a great dark bird with vast wings. In the old days, she was a creature of pure joy, whose song could make the whole world stop and listen, but since the Fall she has become a being of divine grief.

She flies through the night sky wailing a song so beautiful it drives those who hear it to follow her, leaping from towers and cliffs in ecstasy. Only the righteous and the innocent may hear her song and survive; to the faithless, it is an irresistible call to oblivion. She weeps golden tears, and where they fall, flowers grow that bloom only at midnight.

The Sirin is forever paired with the Alkonost — together they sing the song of joy and the song of sorrow, and the wind carries their voices to the ends of the earth.

Свидания и Предостережения
  • Her song can be heard on quiet nights, especially near feast days.
  • She weeps tears of gold that become midnight flowers.
  • She is the companion of the Alkonost, her sister of joy.
  • To hear her song and live, one must have a pure heart and make the sign of the cross.
  • She nests atop the Tree of Life at the edge of the world.
Молитва Против Песни Сирин

"Не слушай, не смотри,
не впускай в своё сердце
песню Сирин-Птицы,
красною и сладкою.
Креста моего Господа,
да будет воля Его."

"Do not listen, do not look, do not admit into your heart the song of the Sirin-Bird, so red and so sweet. By the cross of my Lord, let His will be done."

Alternate Names Сирин-Птица, Сирена, Сладкоголосая
Known Habitats Night sky, edges of the world, apple orchards at midnight
Active Hours Deep night, holy eves, midsummer
Weakness Pure faith, the sign of the cross, loud church bells
Season Most often seen at the summer solstice

Fig. IX.
Demon 💀 Underworld

Лихо Одноглазое

Likho · One-Eyed Misfortune

The Evil-Eyed Hag

Likho is the living embodiment of misfortune — a one-eyed hag who wanders the crossroads and desolate paths, seeking those whose fate she may sour. She is misfortune given form: her single eye burns with malicious intent, and wherever her gaze falls, ruin follows. She is the sister of death, the companion of plague, and the shadow that follows an ill-omened soul.

She clings to the backs of travellers, presses her cold hand upon the brows of sleepers, and whispers terrible thoughts into the ears of the discontented. She rides with the wind and sits upon the roofs of the dying. To meet Likho on a journey is the worst of all omens, for she never travels alone — calamity always follows.

Свидания и Предостережения
  • She appears at crossroads, where paths diverge and fate is decided.
  • Her single eye can curse a man with a glance.
  • She carries a staff carved from a gallows-tree.
  • Iron and salt thrown at her feet may drive her off.
  • If she reaches out to touch your brow, spit three times and cross yourself immediately.
Заговор Против Лиха

"Лихо, Лихо, Одноглазое,
не ходи по дорожке моей,
не сиди на перекрёстке моём.
С солью и с железом,
да уйдёшь ты от меня!"

"Likho, Likho, One-Eyed One, walk not upon my path, sit not at my crossroads. With salt and iron, begone from me!"

Alternate Names Лихо, Несчастье, Злобная Бабка
Known Habitats Crossroads, abandoned villages, thresholds
Active Hours Twilight and the dark of the moon
Weakness Iron, salt, the evil eye against her, bread baked in ashes
Season Winter solstice; times of plague and famine

Fig. X.
Celestial ☁️ Sky

Жар-Птица

Zhar-Ptitsa · The Firebird

The Bird of Living Flame

The Zhar-Ptitsa blazes like a living coal against the night sky. Her feathers glow with golden fire — even when plucked and cast upon the ground, they continue to burn with a cold, magical flame that cannot be quenched by water. She is the most coveted prize in all of Slavic folklore, for a single feather from her tail can light an entire kingdom or destroy it.

Ivan Tsarevich, the third and youngest son of the Tsar, is the most famous seeker of the Firebird. It was the feather he picked up — though he had been warned not to — that set him upon the path of impossible quests, transforming him from a careless prince into a true hero. The Firebird does not give herself willingly; she is won through courage, kindness to magical helpers, and perseverance against impossible odds.

Свидания и Предостережения
  • Her feathers glow even in daylight and never cool.
  • She appears in orchards at midnight, especially apple orchards.
  • She eats golden apples from the Tree of Life.
  • A single plucked feather continues to burn forever.
  • She is served by the Grey Wolf, who is wiser than most princes.
Песнь о Жар-Птице

"Жар-Жар-Птица,
золото-огненная птица!
Ты летишь по тёмному небу,
хвостом оставляешь золото,
клювом — серебро,
крылом — жары-пламень.

"Fire-Fire Bird, golden-fire bird! You fly through the dark sky, leaving gold with your tail, silver with your beak, and blazing flame with your wings."

Alternate Names Жар-Жар-Птица, Огненная Птица
Known Habitats Apple orchards, beneath the Tree of Life, night sky
Active Hours Midnight, especially during Ivan-Kupala
Weakness None — she is beyond mortal harm
Season Summer solstice; Ivan-Kupala Eve

Fig. XI.
Undead 💀 Underworld

Вупырь

Upyr · Вупырь · The Blood Drinker

The Revenant

The Upyr is the risen dead — a corpse that refuses the grave. It is not the elegant undead of foreign lands, but something far more terrible: a bloated, blood-soaked revenant that claws its way from the earth to torment the living. The Upyr returns to its former home, seeking out its own family first, draining them of blood and life while they sleep.

The Upyr does not create others of its kind through bites, as the Western vampires do. Instead, it arises from a sinful life, an improper burial, or the malicious magic of a sorcerer. The body swells with dark blood, the skin turns ruddy, and the hair and nails grow long — and then it claws its way out of the earth to walk among the living once more.

To prevent the dead from rising as Upyrs, the old custom demands that a sickle be placed against the body's chest, and thorns and garlic stuffed into the mouth and nostrils. The corpse must be laid face-down, and a coin placed upon each eye.

Свидания и Предостережения
  • The Upyr's body does not decay in the grave.
  • It can shapeshift into a black cat or a large moth.
  • It suffocates its victims by sitting on their chests while draining blood.
  • It can only be destroyed by driving a stake of aspen wood through the heart.
  • The Upyr cannot cross running water and must return to its grave before dawn.
Похоронный Обряд Против Вупыря

"Кто на свете живёт,
того на смерть не похоронишь,
а кто на свете ото сна не проснётся,
того и в могилу не положишь.
Крестом помети, щитом закрой,
да спи ты в своём доме вечном."

"Who lives in this world cannot be buried in death; and who does not wake from sleep cannot be laid in the grave. Mark with the cross, shield with faith, and sleep in your eternal home."

Alternate Names Вупырь, Носферат, Мертвец
Known Habitats Graveyards, abandoned houses, former homes
Active Hours Midnight until before dawn
Weakness Aspen stake, garlic, thorns, running water, sunlight
Cause Sinful life, improper burial, sorcery, suicide

Святая Книга

The Slavic Bestiary

This grimoire was compiled over many winters by firelight, from the whispered accounts of village elders, the forbidden texts of wandering monks, and the author's own encounters in the deep places of the ancient forest.

Keep this book close when traveling. Keep it closer when the sun sets behind the trees. And whatever you do — never read it aloud after midnight.

Written in the Old Tongue · Translated for the modern seeker
