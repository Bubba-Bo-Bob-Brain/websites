☽ On the Assessment of Peril ☾

The following scale, drawn from the old reckonings of village elders and wandering volkhvs, measures the threat each being poses to mortal folk. The symbols are ancient — older than the churches, older than the principalities — and carry weight beyond mere warning.

Harmless

May be mischievous but bears no true malice. Offerings may earn their favour.

Wary

Exercise caution. Disrespect or ignorance may provoke retaliation.

Dangerous

Genuine threat to life. Observe all traditional protections and taboos.

Deadly

Fatal encounters are common. Avoid entirely unless armed with potent wards.

Abhorrent

The most perilous of beings. No mortal is safe in their presence. Flee without looking back.

I.

The Leshy

Леший — Лѣшій

"The Forest Guardian" • "He of the Woods" • "The Wood-Walker"

Dangerous
Fig. I — The Leshy as he appears to lost travellers, tall as the tallest pine, wearing his hat of bark and moss.

Nature & Domain

The Leshy is the ancient spirit and master of the forest, a being as old as the trees themselves. He appears in many forms: sometimes as a peasant man of ordinary height, sometimes towering above the canopy; sometimes as a withered old man with bark for skin, sometimes as a vigorous youth with leaves woven through his hair.

His eyes glow green as foxfire in the darkness, and his shadow is said to be absent — for the forest itself is his shadow. He wears his garments inside-out and his left shoe always upon the right foot, that mortals might know they are in the presence of the uncanny.

Behaviour & Customs

The Leshy is neither wholly benevolent nor entirely malevolent. He is the forest made manifest — protective of its creatures, wrathful toward those who harm it, and capricious in his dealings with humans. He may lead travellers astray, mimicking the voices of loved ones to lure the unwary deeper into the woods, or he may guide the respectful hunter to abundant game.

⚘ Traditional Protections ⚘

  • Turn your garments inside-out when entering deep forest
  • Carry bread and salt as an offering at the forest's edge
  • Never whistle within the woods — it invites his attention
  • If lost, sit upon a stump and wait — he may take pity
  • Address him as "Khozyain" (Master) to show respect

Habitat

The deepest, oldest forests — particularly those where ancient oaks and pines have stood undisturbed for centuries. He is strongest where the trees are thickest and the sunlight struggles to reach the forest floor. Some say he sleeps beneath the roots of the eldest tree and wakes only when the forest is threatened.

Domain Ancient Forests
Form Shapeshifter
Temperament Capricious
Weakness Iron, Salt
II.

The Rusalka

Русалка — Рѹсалка

"The Drowned Maiden" • "She of the Moonlit Waters" • "The Water's Lament"

Deadly
Fig. II — The Rusalka emerges at moonrise, her pale form glowing above the dark waters beneath the weeping willow.

Nature & Origin

The Rusalka is the restless spirit of a woman who died by drowning — often one who took her own life in despair, or one who was wronged in love and cast into the water. She is not the gentle nymph of Western fancy, but a being of profound sorrow and terrible vengeance, bound to the waters where she perished.

Her beauty is otherworldly and terrible: skin pale as moonlight on still water, hair long and green as river weed, eyes dark and depthless as the deepest pool. She sings with a voice of unbearable sweetness, and those who hear it feel compelled to follow, regardless of the danger.

Behaviour & Customs

During Rusalnaya Week in early summer, the Rusalki leave their waters and wander the fields and forests, dancing in moonlit circles and sitting upon the branches of birch trees. It is said that any field touched by their dancing will yield abundant harvest — but any mortal who witnesses their dance is marked for doom.

They are most dangerous to young men, whom they lure into the water with their song and embrace, tickling them to death beneath the surface. Their laughter echoes across the water on still nights, and it is the sound of joy turned to something hollow and cold.

⚘ Traditional Protections ⚘

  • Never swim alone, especially at night or during Rusalnaya Week
  • Wear a cross of cypress wood around your neck near dark waters
  • If you hear singing from the water, plug your ears with wax and walk away
  • Leave offerings of bread and embroidered cloth on the riverbank
  • Carry a branch of wormwood — they cannot abide its bitterness

Habitat

Rivers, lakes, and ponds — particularly those where a death has occurred. They are drawn to willow trees and water lilies, and are said to make their homes in underwater palaces of crystal and pearl, built from the belongings of their victims.

Domain Rivers & Lakes
Form Pale Maiden
Temperament Vengeful
Weakness Wormwood, Prayer
III.

Baba Yaga

Баба-Яга — Баба-Ѧга

"The Witch of the Iron Mortar" • "The Bone-Legged One" • "Grandmother of the Dark Forest"

Abhorrent
Fig. III — Baba Yaga's hut stands upon chicken legs, surrounded by a fence of human bones, each skull a lamp that burns with ghostly fire.

Nature & Identity

Baba Yaga is the most feared and enigmatic figure in all of Slavic folklore. She is ancient beyond reckoning — some say she is death itself given form, others that she is the last of the old gods, stubbornly refusing to fade from the world. She dwells in a hut that stands upon enormous chicken legs, turning to face those who approach, surrounded by a fence made of human bones, each topped with a skull whose eye sockets burn with unholy fire.

She flies through the air seated in a giant iron mortar, propelling herself with a pestle and steering with a broom of silver birch. Where she flies, she sweeps away her tracks with the broom, leaving no trace of her passage. Her nose is so long it touches the ceiling of her hut when she sleeps, and her teeth are iron.

Behaviour & Customs

Baba Yaga is not simply evil — she is something more complex and more terrible. She is a tester of worthiness, a giver of impossible tasks, and a dispenser of aid or destruction based on the character of those who come before her. She may help the brave and respectful hero with gifts and wisdom, or she may thrust the cowardly and rude into her oven for supper.

⚘ The Traditional Greeting ⚘

When approaching Baba Yaga's hut, one must speak the ancient words:

"Izbushka, Izbushka, turn your back to the forest and your face to me."

And if the hut turns, one must enter with respect, ask no questions, and answer all questions truthfully — though not too cleverly, for she despises trickery in those who lack the strength to back it up.

Habitat

The deepest part of the dark forest, where no paths lead and the trees grow so thick that day becomes twilight. Her hut appears only to those she chooses to receive — or those desperate enough to wander until all hope is lost. Some say the hut exists in the space between worlds, and can only be reached by those who have already died a little.

Domain Deep Forest
Form Ancient Crone
Temperament Unfathomable
Weakness Respect, Courage
IV.

Zmey Gorynych

Змей Горыныч — Змѣй Горынычъ

"The Three-Headed Wyrm" • "The Mountain Dragon" • "Scourge of the Steppes"

Abhorrent
Fig. IV — Zmey Gorynych rises above the mountains, his three heads surveying the land for villages to plunder and heroes to devour.

Nature & Lineage

Zmey Gorynych — the Mountain Dragon, son of the Mountain — is the greatest of the serpent-dragons in Slavic myth. He is a creature of immense size and terrible power, most commonly depicted with three heads (though some accounts speak of six, nine, or even twelve), each capable of breathing fire that can reduce a village to ash in moments.

His scales are black as volcanic glass and hard as iron. His wings cast shadows over entire towns. His voice is the rumble of the mountain itself, and when he speaks, the earth trembles. He is the enemy of the bogatyr — the great heroes of old — and many a tale tells of the epic battles between dragon and champion.

Behaviour & Customs

The Zmey is a creature of pride and appetite. He demands tribute from nearby settlements — often in the form of livestock, treasure, or, in the darkest tales, young maidens. He hoards gold and precious objects in his mountain lair, not merely from greed but from an ancient dragon-instinct that treasures beauty and value.

Despite his fearsome nature, some tales portray the Zmey as capable of taking human form — a handsome stranger who courts a maiden unaware of his true nature. In these stories, the dragon is as much a seducer as a destroyer, and the danger lies as much in his charm as in his fire.

⚘ Slaying the Dragon ⚘

The old tales speak of only one way to kill a Zmey:

  • A blade forged from the metal of a fallen star
  • A hero of pure heart and unshakeable courage
  • All heads must be severed simultaneously, lest the remaining heads regrow the lost ones
  • The tongue must be cut out, for the dragon's words are as dangerous as his fire

Habitat

High mountain peaks, volcanic regions, and deep caves within the earth. He is associated with the Carpathian Mountains and the wild steppes beyond. Some say he sleeps beneath the earth and will rise again when the world grows weak.

Domain Mountains & Caves
Form Three-Headed Dragon
Temperament Wrathful & Proud
Weakness Star-Metal, Heroism
V.

The Vodyanoy

Водяной — Водѧный

"The Grandfather of the Waters" • "The Drowned One" • "Lord of the Deep"

Deadly
Fig. V — The Vodyanoy rises from the murk, his bloated form draped in weed and his eyes like the cold light of drowned stars.

Nature & Domain

The Vodyanoy is the master of all waters — rivers, lakes, ponds, and mills. He is the spirit of drowning itself, a bloated, ancient being with the face of a drowned man, skin covered in green slime, and a body draped in waterweed and silt. His eyes bulge like those of a frog, and his beard flows like seaweed in a current.

He is said to be the drowned soul of a man who died by water and was not properly buried — condemned to become the very thing that killed him. He commands all the fish and creatures of his domain, and the water itself bends to his will.

Behaviour & Customs

The Vodyanoy is a jealous and solitary being. He demands respect from all who use his waters — fishermen must offer him the first catch, millers must pour libations into the stream, and swimmers must never dive into unknown waters without leaving an offering upon the bank.

He drags the unwary beneath the surface, not out of malice alone but out of loneliness — he seeks companions in the deep, and the souls of the drowned serve as his servants in his underwater kingdom. Those who drown in his domain become part of his court, their faces visible just beneath the surface on moonless nights.

⚘ Appeasing the Water Grandfather ⚘

  • Throw a coin and a lock of hair into the water before swimming
  • Fishermen must toss the first fish back into the water
  • Never bathe after dark, especially near mills or whirlpools
  • If the water suddenly grows cold, leave immediately — he is near
  • Millers must leave a black rooster at the millstone once a year

Habitat

Deep pools, millponds, river bends where the current slows, and any body of water where a drowning has occurred. He makes his home at the bottom in a palace of waterlogged timber and sunken treasures, surrounded by the preserved bodies of his victims who serve him in eternal silence.

Domain All Fresh Waters
Form Bloated Old Man
Temperament Jealous & Lonely
Weakness Offerings, Iron
VI.

The Domovoi

Домовой — Домовѣй

"The House Spirit" • "Grandfather of the Hearth" • "The One Behind the Stove"

Harmless
Fig. VI — The Domovoi peeks from behind the great stove, the warm heart of every Slavic home, watching over the sleeping household.

Nature & Role

The Domovoi is the guardian spirit of the home, one of the most beloved and enduring figures in Slavic folk tradition. He is a small, hairy being — resembling an old man covered in fur — who lives behind the great stove or beneath the threshold of every house. He is the spirit of the hearth itself, as essential to a home as the fire that warms it.

Each household has its own Domovoi, and when a family moves to a new home, they must invite their Domovoi to come with them, calling out: "Domovoi, Domovoi, don't stay here — come with our family." A house without a Domovoi is a house without a soul.

Behaviour & Customs

The Domovoi is generally benevolent and protective. He watches over the household while its members sleep, tends to the livestock, and warns of danger by making small noises — tapping on walls, rattling pots, or breathing heavily. If the household is well-kept and respectful, he will ensure prosperity and good fortune.

However, he is easily offended by laziness, swearing, or fighting within the home. A displeased Domovoi may pinch sleepers, tangle hair, break dishes, or — in extreme cases — set fire to the house. He particularly dislikes those who sleep with their feet uncovered and will tug at them in the night.

⚘ Keeping the Domovoi Happy ⚘

  • Leave a small bowl of milk or bread near the stove each evening
  • Keep the house clean and orderly — he despises mess
  • Never whistle indoors — it drives him away
  • When moving, formally invite him to accompany the family
  • If you feel a warm presence in the night, thank him — he is watching

Habitat

Behind the stove, beneath the threshold, in the corners of the house, or in the barn among the livestock. He is bound to the dwelling itself and cannot exist far from it. When a house falls to ruin, the Domovoi fades with it, mourning the loss of his purpose.

Domain The Home & Hearth
Form Small Hairy Man
Temperament Protective
Weakness Disrespect, Neglect
VII.

The Kikimora

Кикимора — Кикимора

"The Swamp Hag" • "She Who Sits in the Corner" • "The Nightmare Weaver"

Dangerous
Fig. VII — The Kikimora sits in the swamp, spinning thread from the hair of the unwary, her spindle turning with the rhythm of nightmares.

Nature & Origin

The Kikimora is a creature of the swamp and the household's dark corners — a being that exists between worlds, neither fully of the wild nor fully of the home. She is thin to the point of translucence, her body like a skeleton wrapped in wet rags, her fingers impossibly long and always busy at some unseen task.

There are two kinds of Kikimora: the bolotnaya (swamp) Kikimora, who dwells in marshes and bogs, and the domashnyaya (household) Kikimora, who lives behind the stove or in the cellar. The household variety is sometimes said to be the wife of the Domovoi, though she is far less benevolent than her husband.

Behaviour & Customs

The Kikimora is the bringer of nightmares and the weaver of bad dreams. She sits upon the chests of sleeping victims, pressing down until they cannot breathe, whispering terrible visions into their minds. She spins thread at night — and if you hear the sound of spinning in the dark, it means death is coming for someone in the house.

The swamp Kikimora lures travellers into bogs with the sound of weeping or the appearance of a lost child. Those who follow find themselves sinking into the mire, her cold hands pulling them down from beneath the surface.

⚘ Warding Off the Kikimora ⚘

  • Place a needle and thread in the corner of the room — she will stop to count the threads
  • Draw a cross with chalk on the doorframe
  • Never leave spinning tools out overnight
  • Keep a broom standing in the corner — she cannot pass it
  • If you hear spinning, speak aloud: "I hear you, Kikimora. Stop your work."

Habitat

Swamps, marshes, and bogs for the wild variety. For the household variety: dark corners, cellars, behind stoves, and under floorboards. She is drawn to places of neglect and sorrow, growing stronger in homes where the inhabitants are unhappy or careless.

Domain Swamps & Dark Corners
Form Gaunt Hag
Temperament Malevolent
Weakness Iron, Prayer, Broom
VIII.

Poludnitsa

Полудница — Полѹдница

"The Lady Midday" • "She of the Scorching Sun" • "The Noon Witch"

Dangerous
Fig. VIII — Poludnitsa stands in the blazing wheat field, her white dress shimmering in the heat, her sickle gleaming in the merciless noon sun.

Nature & Manifestation

Poludnitsa — Lady Midday — appears only at the height of noon, when the sun stands directly overhead and the heat presses down upon the fields like a physical weight. She manifests as a tall woman dressed entirely in white, her form shimmering and indistinct in the heat haze, carrying a scythe or sickle.

She is the embodiment of sunstroke and heat exhaustion, the personification of the danger of labouring in the midday sun. In the old days, when peasants worked from dawn to dusk, the noon hour was sacred — a time to rest, to eat, to shelter from the sun. Those who ignored this wisdom risked an encounter with Poludnitsa.

Behaviour & Customs

Poludnitsa wanders the fields at noon, seeking those who are still labouring when they should be resting. She approaches them and asks impossible questions — riddles about the harvest, the weather, or the nature of the earth itself. If the worker cannot answer, she strikes them with her sickle, bringing on sunstroke, madness, or death.

Even if the questions are answered correctly, she may simply stand and stare until the victim collapses from the heat. Her gaze is said to be as burning as the sun itself, and those who meet her eyes feel the world spin and the sky go white.

⚘ Surviving the Noon Hour ⚘

  • Never work in the fields at noon — rest in the shade
  • If you see a white figure in the heat haze, close your eyes and walk away slowly
  • Carry a piece of bread with salt — offering it may distract her
  • If she asks you a question, answer truthfully but briefly, then retreat
  • Wear a hat of straw and carry water at all times during harvest

Habitat

Open fields, particularly wheat and rye fields during harvest season. She appears only on the hottest days of summer, when the sun is at its zenith and the air shimmers above the crops. She is most commonly encountered during the harvest festivals of late summer.

Domain Open Fields at Noon
Form Woman in White
Temperament Relentless
Weakness Shade, Bread & Salt
IX.

Alkonost

Алконост — Алконостъ

"The Bird of Paradise" • "She of the Enchanted Song" • "The Messenger of Joy"

Wary
Fig. IX — Alkonost, the bird of paradise, sings with the voice of a maiden, her song so beautiful that those who hear it forget all earthly cares.

Nature & Origin

Alkonost is a creature of wonder rather than terror — a magnificent bird with the body of a peacock and the head of a maiden, said to dwell in the mythical land of Iriy, the Slavic paradise. Her song is of such unbearable beauty that those who hear it forget everything — their homes, their families, their very names — and wander the earth in a blissful daze, forever seeking to hear it again.

She is one of three mythical birds of Slavic lore, alongside her sisters Sirin (who brings sorrow) and Gamayun (who brings wisdom). Where Sirin sings of loss and Gamayun of knowledge, Alkonost sings of pure, uncomplicated joy — which is, perhaps, the most dangerous song of all.

Behaviour & Customs

Alkonost appears rarely, and always as an omen — though whether the omen is blessing or curse depends on the listener. Her song can bring profound happiness, but it can also strip a person of their will, their memory, and their connection to the mortal world. Those enchanted by her song may simply sit and listen until they starve, lost in rapture.

In some tales, Alkonost is associated with the changing of seasons. She lays her eggs on the shore of the sea, and when she does, the water grows calm for seven days. During this time, sailors speak of hearing a beautiful singing carried on the wind, and the wisest among them plug their ears and sail on.

⚘ The Song of Alkonost ⚘

The old tales warn:

"Her voice is sweeter than honey, warmer than sunlight, more comforting than a mother's embrace. And that is precisely why you must flee when you hear it — for no mortal joy is worth the forgetting."

Habitat

The mythical land of Iriy, beyond the eastern edge of the world, where the sun rises and the eternal spring never ends. She occasionally visits the mortal world, appearing above still lakes or in the mists above rivers at dawn.

Domain Paradise of Iriy
Form Bird-Maiden
Temperament Enchanting
Weakness Iron, Willpower
X.

The Bannik

Банник — Банникъ

"The Bathhouse Spirit" • "He of the Steam" • "The Third Calling"

Deadly
Fig. X — The Bannik lurks in the steam of the bathhouse, his shadowy form visible only in the corner of your eye, waiting for the third calling.

Nature & Domain

The Bannik is the spirit of the banya — the traditional Slavic bathhouse. He is a small, wrinkled creature with skin like a boiled potato, covered in soap suds and steam, his eyes red from the heat. He dwells in the darkest corner of the bathhouse, behind the stove, and he is the master of the steam, the water, and the birch branches used for beating the skin.

The banya was considered a liminal space — neither fully inside the home nor fully outside in the world — and as such, it was a place where the boundary between the mortal and spirit worlds was thin. The Bannik is the guardian of this threshold, and he takes his role very seriously.

Behaviour & Customs

The Bannik is dangerous because he is proud. The bathhouse must be used in a specific order: the family bathes first, then the guests, and only then — in the third calling — does the Bannik take his bath. Anyone who bathes during the third calling without first asking permission invites his wrath.

An angry Bannik will scald the skin with boiling water, throw stones at the bather, or — in the worst cases — drag them beneath the hot stones of the stove, never to be seen again. He particularly dislikes those who swear, fight, or behave indecently in his domain.

⚘ Bathhouse Etiquette ⚘

  • Never bathe alone after dark — the Bannik is strongest at night
  • Always leave a bucket of water and a birch branch for the Bannik
  • Before entering the third calling, speak aloud: "Bannik, Bannik, may we share your warmth?"
  • Leave an offering of bread and salt on the windowsill
  • If you feel hot hands on your back, thank the Bannik and leave immediately
  • Never bathe during the third calling without explicit invitation

Habitat

The bathhouse — specifically the dark corner behind the stove, where the steam is thickest and the heat is most intense. He is bound to the banya and cannot leave it, but his influence extends to anyone who enters his domain uninvited.

Domain The Bathhouse
Form Small Wrinkled Man
Temperament Proud & Vindictive
Weakness Respect, Offerings

Here Endeth the Bestiary

These accounts have been gathered from the whispers of village elders, the songs of wandering minstrels, and the cautionary tales told to children beside the fire. They are not mere stories — they are warnings, handed down through generations of those who knew that the forest is older than memory, the waters deeper than thought, and the darkness between the trees alive with things that do not bear the names we give them.

Keep this book close. Read it by firelight. And when you walk through the deep forest, remember: you are a guest in their domain.

Храните лес. Уважайте воду. Помните старых богов.
Keep the forest. Respect the water. Remember the old gods.
