Leshy (Леший)

Also known as: Lesovik, Leszy, The Wild Man

☦ Nature & Aspect

The Leshy is the undisputed sovereign of the deep woodland, a shapeshifting spirit as old as the birches themselves. He appears as a man grown from bark and moss, with eyes like green fire and feet that point backward to confuse those who would track him. His size shifts with his mood—towering as the canopy when wrathful, small as a mushroom when sly.

☦ Habitat & Domain

Found exclusively in the oldest growth forests where the birch whispers to the oak. His presence is marked by sudden silence in the woods, by rings of mushrooms, and by paths that seem to lead travelers in circles. The Leshy claims all that dies within his borders, hanging bones in the trees as trophies and warnings.

☦ Temperament & Warding

Capricious and proud, the Leshy may guide the respectful through safe paths or lead the boastful to their doom. To ward him, carry bread and salt, wear your clothes inside-out, and never—never—cut wood without asking permission first. Those who please him may find lost livestock returned; those who anger him wander until madness takes them.

❧
"He who enters the wood without greeting the guardian, enters as meat for the ravens."
— Old Believer's Warning, Pskov Region, 1642

Rusalka (Русалка)

Also known as: Vilenka, Water Maiden, Drowned One

☦ Nature & Aspect

Neither fully ghost nor water nymph, the Rusalka is the restless spirit of a maiden drowned—whether by suicide, murder, or unfortunate accident. She appears breathtakingly beautiful, with pale skin and hair that drips with river weeds, seated upon the bank combing her tresses with a golden comb. Do not be fooled by her song.

☦ Habitat & Domain

Freshwater only—rivers, lakes, mill ponds, and deep wells. She favors willow-shaded banks and places where the water runs black with peat. During Rusalka Week (the week following Pentecost), she is most active, dancing in circles upon the water's surface and calling to lonely travelers.

☦ Temperament & Warding

Tragic and malevolent in equal measure. She lures men to watery graves with songs of such sweetness that listeners forget to breathe. To protect oneself: carry wormwood, avoid water at twilight, and if you see her comb, look away immediately. She may be laid to rest only by proper Christian burial rites or by avenging her death.

❧
"Her hair is the color of river clay, her eyes the color of deep water. She smells of lilies and rotting leaves."
— Chronicle of Novgorod Fishermen

Baba Yaga (Баба-Яга)

Also known as: Baba Jaga, The Bone Mother, Iron-Toothed Crone

☦ Nature & Aspect

The most ambiguous and terrifying of all Slavic spirits—a witch of immense power who dwells in a hut that stands upon chicken legs, surrounded by a fence of human bones topped with skulls that glow with spectral fire. She rides in a giant mortar, steering with a pestle, erasing her trail with a broom made of silver birch.

☦ Habitat & Domain

The dark forest's deepest heart, where three riderless horses roam—one white, one red, one black—representing the day, the sun, and the night. Her hut spins and crouches low to hide from the pious, and only the command "Little hut, little hut, stand with your back to the forest and your face to me" will make it settle.

☦ Temperament & Warding

She is Fate itself—neither wholly evil nor good, but elemental. She devours the lazy and the rude, but aids those with pure hearts and clever tongues. There is no warding from Baba Yaga, only the hope that you may be found worthy. Bring her bread, show respect, and never ask more than three questions.

❧
"She is the one who knows the names of all the bones in the earth. She is the one who grinds the years in her mortar."
— The First Tale

Zmey Gorynych (Змей Горыныч)

Also known as: The Mountain Dragon, The Three-Headed Serpent

☦ Nature & Aspect

A dragon of the old world—winged sometimes, always with three heads (or seven, or nine, depending on the telling), each capable of breathing fire, poison, or ice. He is vast as a mountain ridge, scales like iron shields, and eyes that glow like forge-fires. He hoards not gold, but princesses and storm clouds.

☦ Habitat & Domain

The high mountain peaks where lightning strikes thrice, deep caves behind waterfalls of blood, and the storm clouds themselves. He blocks rivers with his bulk, causing floods, and his breath scorches villages miles away. Only the greatest heroes dare his lair.

☦ Temperament & Warding

Ravenous and proud. He demands tribute of maidens and livestock, and his laughter is thunder. To defeat him requires magical swords, the help of wise horses, and knowledge of his secret heart—usually hidden outside his body in an egg, inside a duck, inside a hare, inside a chest, on an island, on a chain.

❧
"Where he flies, the wheat burns black. Where he sleeps, the stone melts."
— Byliny of Dobrynya Nikitich

Poludnitsa (Полудница)

Also known as: Lady Midday, Noonwraith, Polevik's Wife

☦ Nature & Aspect

A demon of the harvest fields, appearing as a woman all in white, her face hidden by a wide-brimmed hat or by the blinding sunlight itself. She carries a scythe or shears, and her presence brings the midday paralysis—heatstroke, sun sickness, and madness. She is the reason field workers must rest when the sun is highest.

☦ Habitat & Domain

Grain fields, meadows, and anywhere the sun beats down without mercy at exactly noon. She appears most often during the harvest season, when the grain is high and golden. She asks impossible questions or challenges workers to spinning contests. Those who fail, she beheads.

☦ Temperament & Warding

Cruel and capricious, but bound by strict rules—she can only appear at noon, and only in open fields. To escape her, answer her riddles correctly, or better yet, do not work in the fields when the church bells ring twelve. Children are warned: "Don't go to the rye at noon, or Poludnitsa will cut your head off."

Vila (Вила)

Also known as: Veela, Cloud Maiden, Spirit of the Whirlwind

☦ Nature & Aspect

Beautiful, immortal, and terrible—the Vila are spirits of wind and storm, appearing as swans or wolves when they wish, but most commonly as maidens of unearthly beauty with long hair that reaches their feet. They ride the whirlwinds and shoot arrows of disease at those who offend them.

☦ Habitat & Domain

Mountaintops, cloud banks, and the hearts of storms. They dance in circles that leave the grass scorched or permanently bent. They control the weather and may be petitioned by leaving offerings at certain trees—never oaks, which they despise.

☦ Temperament & Warding

Proud and vengeful, but capable of great love. They have been known to teach heroes magical songs or adopt human children. However, if a man breaks a promise to a Vila, she will force him to dance until death. They cannot drown, and they know the language of all animals.

Kikimora (Кикимора)

Also known as: She-Spirit, The Weaver, House-Bane

☦ Nature & Aspect

A small, hunched creature formed from the souls of unbaptized babies or women who died in childbed. She lives behind the stove or in the cellar, weaving ceaselessly on invisible looms. She is thin as a thread, with hair like straw and eyes that glow green in the dark.

☦ Habitat & Domain

The dark corners of houses, particularly those built improperly or without the proper blessings. She brings mischief—entangling hair while people sleep, souring milk, whistling in the chimney. In the swamp, her counterpart is more monstrous, half-woman, half-bird, luring men to sink in peat.

☦ Temperament & Warding

More annoying than deadly, but her constant presence drains the life from a home. To banish her: find her invisible spindle and break it, leave a broom by the door crossed with a rake, or have the house blessed by a priest. Some keep her placated with leftover bread and porridge.
