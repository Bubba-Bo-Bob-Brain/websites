Лѣтопись Чудесъ

A Chronicle of Wonders from the Slavic Lands

✦

A Word to the Reader

— Напутствие Читателю —

In the deepest reaches of the ancient forests, where the birch trees whisper secrets older than memory and the rivers run dark with forgotten tears, there dwell creatures born from the fears and wonder of the Slavic peoples. For a thousand years, the stariki — the elders — have passed down their tales by firelight.

This compendium gathers the most formidable and mysterious beings of Slavic folklore — from the shape-shifting lords of the forest to the drowned maidens who haunt moonlit waters, from the fearsome witch of the chicken-legged hut to the dragon whose many heads breathe death upon the land.

⚠ Heed this warning: These are not idle tales for amusement. Each entry has been verified by those who have encountered these beings and lived. Read with care, traveler. The forest is listening.

❧

I. Leshy

Лѣшій — Lord of the Forest

Leshy

Forest Guardian Spirit · Лѣсной Хозяинъ

Habitat Deep forests, ancient groves, crossroads
Danger Rating ✦ ✦ ✦ ✦ ✦
Shapeshifter Trickster Forest Lord

The Leshy is the master of the forest, a towering spirit whose form shifts between that of a peasant farmer, a whirlwind, a talking tree, or a creature of monstrous proportions. His eyes burn like green fire beneath a crown of living branches.

Those who enter his domain without respect find themselves walking in circles for days. Yet the Leshy is not wholly malevolent — those who leave offerings of bread, salt, or tobacco may find him a willing guide.

Appearance

Appears as a colossal man with bark-like skin, eyes like deep forest pools, and a beard of moss. Sometimes he appears as a simple old peasant, recognizable only by his backwards-facing shoes.

Protection & Signs

Wearing your clothing inside-out confuses the Leshy. If you hear your name called from the trees but see no one — do not answer. His laughter sounds like breaking branches.

"When the wind speaks your name between the birches and no one is there — that is the Leshy, deciding your fate."

— Oral tradition of the Vologda region
❧

II. Rusalka

Русалка — The Drowned Maiden

Rusalka

Water Spirit · Водяная Нимфа

Habitat Rivers, lakes, millponds, willow groves
Danger Rating ✦ ✦ ✦ ✦ ✦
Drowned Maiden Enchantress Siren

The Rusalki are the restless spirits of young women who met their end in the waters. They appear as hauntingly beautiful maidens with pale, luminous skin and long, unbound hair that streams behind them like river weed.

Protection & Signs

Never swim alone during Rusalka Week. Carrying fern flowers offers protection. If you hear laughter from an empty riverbank, leave immediately.

"She sat upon the willow branch, combing her endless hair with a comb of bone, and the river ran backward to hear her sing."

— Tale from the Dnieper region
❧

III. Baba Yaga

Баба Яга — The Bone Mother

Baba Yaga

Witch of the Wild · Костяная Нога

Habitat The chicken-legged hut, deep in the dark forest
Danger Rating ✦ ✦ ✦ ✦ ✦
Cannibal Ambiguous Morality Iron-Toothed

Baba Yaga is the fearsome crone of Slavic myth — a being of terrible power who dwells in a hut that stands on great chicken legs. She flies through the sky in a giant mortar, propelling herself with a pestle and sweeping away her trail with a broom.

Protection & Signs

Address her as "Babushka" and show no fear. Accept whatever task she gives you. If you hear the grinding of bone on bone in the forest at midnight, that is her mortar approaching.

"Turn your back to the forest and your front to me," cried the hut, and the forest itself shuddered.

— A.N. Afanasyev
❧

IV. Zmey Gorynych

Змѣй Горынычъ — The Three-Headed Dragon

Zmey Gorynych

Dragon · Трёхглавый Змѣй

Habitat Mountain caves, volcanic lairs, dark valleys
Danger Rating ✦ ✦ ✦ ✦ ✦
Fire-Breathing Multi-Headed Shapeshifter

Zmey Gorynych is the great dragon of Slavic legend — a creature of immense power with three heads, each capable of breathing fire hot enough to melt stone. Unlike Western dragons, the Zmey is often intelligent and cunning, capable of taking human form.

Protection & Signs

The Zmey's only true weakness is its hidden heart — said to be kept outside its body. Find and destroy this, and the dragon dies. Holy water can drive it back temporarily.

"The sky turned black with the beating of his wings, and where his shadow passed, the wheat withered and the rivers boiled."

— Bylina of Dobrynya Nikitich
❧

V. Vodyanoy

Водяной — The Old Man of the Waters

Vodyanoy

Water Spirit · Хозяин Воды

Habitat Deep river pools, millponds, lake bottoms
Danger Rating ✦ ✦ ✦ ✦ ✦
Ancient Drowner Master of Fish

The Vodyanoy is the ancient lord of all waters — a bloated, toad-like creature with the body of an old man, the face of a fish, and a beard of living green algae. He drowns those who disturb his waters and rules from his palace of mud beneath the waves.

Protection & Signs

Always ask permission before fishing. Throw bread or tobacco into the river as an offering. If the water suddenly grows warm, the Vodyanoy is near. Never whistle on a riverbank.

"The old miller threw his first fish into the deep pool every morning, and the river never took his sons."

— Belorussian folk saying
❧

VI. Domovoy

Домовой — The Household Guardian

Domovoy

Household Spirit · Домашний Дух

Habitat Behind the stove, in the hearth, under the threshold
Danger Rating ✦ ✦ ✦ ✦ ✦
Guardian Protector House Spirit

The Domovoy is the benevolent spirit of the household — a small, bearded old man who lives behind the stove and protects the family from evil. He tends the livestock, guards against fire, and warns of coming danger through knocks and creaks.

Protection & Signs

Leave him offerings of bread and milk by the hearth. Never curse or quarrel in the house — it angers him. When moving, invite the Domovoy to come with you by calling his name at the old hearth.

"Leave a bowl of porridge by the fire, and the Domovoy will guard your dreams."

— Russian household saying
❧

VII. Kikimora

Кикимора — The Nightmare Wife

Kikimora

Night Spirit · Ночная Мара

Habitat Behind the stove, swamps, dark corners
Danger Rating ✦ ✦ ✦ ✦ ✦
Nightmare Bringer Spinner Troublemaker

The Kikimora is a female house spirit, often the wife of the Domovoy or a creature of the swamps. She appears as a small, hunched woman who spins endlessly at night. Her presence brings nightmares, restless sleep, and mischief to the household.

Protection & Signs

Place a spindle with black thread by the stove to appease her. If you hear spinning at night but find no spinner, the Kikimora is near. Iron objects hung by the bed ward off her nightmares.

"She spins and spins through the darkest hours, and by morning, your dreams are tangled like her thread."

— Northern Russian folklore
❧

VIII. Poludnitsa

Полудница — The Lady of Noon

Poludnitsa

Field Spirit · Полевая Ведьма

Habitat Wheat fields, crossroads, noon-tide shadows
Danger Rating ✦ ✦ ✦ ✦ ✦
Noon Wraith Whirlwind Deceiver

The Poludnitsa is a spectral woman who appears in fields at the hottest hour of noon. She appears as a beautiful woman in white or as a whirlwind of dust and chaff. She asks riddles of those working in the fields — answer incorrectly, and she whips off your head with her sickle.

Protection & Signs

Rest during the noon hour and do not work in the fields. If a sudden whirlwind rises in a still field, the Poludnitsa dances there. Bow to the wind and she may pass you by.

"At the stroke of noon, put down your scythe and bow to the four winds — lest the Lady of Noon take your head instead of the wheat."

— Czech field proverb
❧

IX. Strigoi

Стригой — The Living Dead

Strigoi

Undead Vampire · Нежить

Habitat Graveyards, crossroads, ancestral homes
Danger Rating ✦ ✦ ✦ ✦ ✦
Undead Blood Drinker Shapeshifter

The Strigoi are troubled spirits of the restless dead — those born with a caul, who died unnaturally, or who were buried improperly. They rise from their graves at night to drain the life from sleeping victims, appearing as pale, hollow-eyed revenants with ruddy, blood-stained faces.

Protection & Signs

Drive a stake of aspen through the heart, decapitate, and burn the body. Garlic, hawthorn, and iron repel them. If livestock waste away mysteriously, a Strigoi feeds nearby. Bury the dead face-down to prevent their return.

"The dead do not always rest. Some hunger still, and their hunger is endless as the night."

— Romanian proverb
❧

X. Simargl

Симарглъ — The Sacred Hound

Simargl

Divine Beast · Священный Пёс

Habitat The celestial realm, sacred groves, hearth fires
Danger Rating ✦ ✦ ✦ ✦ ✦
Divine Winged Seed Guardian

Simargl is a sacred winged dog or wolf from the Slavic pantheon — a divine beast associated with seeds, fertility, and the hearth. As a guardian of the sacred flame and protector of growing crops, Simargl represents the beneficial power of fire and the divine protection of the harvest.

Protection & Signs

Simargl is generally benevolent. Seeds blessed in his name are said to grow strong. If a dog howls at the fire, Simargl may be passing between worlds. Keep the hearth fire burning to honor him.

"Where the sacred hound flies, the seeds stir in the earth and the fire burns eternal."

— Slavic cosmological text
