🌳 Forest Dwellers

⚹⚹⚹⚹⚹

Leshy

The Forest Guardian

A shapeshifting spirit of the forest, the Leshy appears as a towering figure with moss for hair, bark-like skin, and eyes that glow like embers. His form may blend with the trees, making him nearly invisible to those who do not know his signs.

The Leshy is a trickster who delights in leading travelers astray, but he may also protect the forest from those who seek to harm it. He is known to mimic voices, create illusions, and even tickle the unwary to death with his long, leafy beard.

To appease the Leshy, leave offerings of bread, salt, or tobacco at the forest's edge. Wearing clothing inside-out or carrying a piece of iron may ward off his mischief.

In Slavic tradition, the Leshy is married to the forest witch Lesovikha, and together they rule the woodland creatures. Some tales claim he can shrink to the size of a blade of grass or grow as tall as the highest oak.

⚹⚹⚹⚹⚹

Baba Yaga

The Bone-Legged Crone

Baba Yaga is a hag with iron teeth, a long crooked nose, and legs made of bone. She flies in a mortar, wielding a pestle as a weapon, and lives in a hut that stands on chicken legs, surrounded by a fence of human bones.

She is both a fearsome cannibal and a wise seer. Baba Yaga may devour the unwary or aid heroes who prove their worth with cleverness or kindness. She often sets impossible tasks for those who seek her knowledge.

To survive an encounter with Baba Yaga, one must be polite, answer her riddles wisely, and avoid eating her food. Carrying a comb and a towel may also turn the tide in your favor.

Baba Yaga is a figure of ambiguity—neither purely evil nor good. She is associated with the crone aspect of the Triple Goddess (Maiden, Mother, Crone) and is sometimes said to be the mother of the Slavic gods.

💧 Water Spirits

⚹⚹⚹⚹⚹

Rusalka

The Drowned Maiden

The Rusalka is the spirit of a woman who died violently or by drowning. She appears as a beautiful maiden with long, wet hair, pale skin, and a greenish tint. She lures men to their deaths with her enchanting songs.

Bound in the year of the Great Oak, by the hands of the unseen.
