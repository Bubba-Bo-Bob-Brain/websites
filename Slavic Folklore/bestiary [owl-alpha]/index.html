Лѣшій

Leshy

Also known as: Leshii, Borovoi, Lesovik, Leszy

Forest
◈◈

The sovereign of the deep forest, the Leshy commands every root and branch within his domain. He appears as a towering figure — sometimes a giant among trees, sometimes merely a man of unusual height — his skin like rough birch bark, his hair a cascade of living moss and fern. His eyes burn with foxfire, green and unblinking, and from his brow sprout antlers like those of the oldest stag.

He is a shapeshifter without equal, able to become any beast, any tree, any shadow between the pines. Those who wander into his wood may find themselves walking in circles for days, led astray by his laughter echoing from every direction at once. He is neither wholly evil nor wholly good — he is the forest itself, and the forest cares nothing for the morality of man.

Habitat & Domain

Deep, ancient forests, particularly dense birch and pine groves. Leshy claim specific territories — an old grove, a mossy ravine, a stand of trees untouched by axe for a hundred years. They dwell within hollow trees, beneath root systems, in invisible palaces deep within the earth beneath their chosen wood.

Behavior & Nature

Leshy are capricious guardians. A hunter who asks permission and shows respect may find game plentiful. One who whistles carelessly or cuts a living tree without cause may be led into the deepest thicket and never seen again. They herd wolves and bears as a shepherd tends sheep, and can command the wind to create storms of leaves and fallen branches.

Wards & Protections

Carry bread and salt into the forest as offerings. Turn your clothes inside out to confuse the Leshy's enchantments. Speak the old words: "Дорога лесная, дорога лесная, проведи меня домой." Never whistle in the forest — it is the Leshy's call, and he will answer.

Collected Sayings

"The forest does not hate you. The forest does not love you. The forest simply is." — Old Believer proverb
"When the wind speaks through the pines in a voice almost like words, do not answer. The Leshy is testing whether you are tree or man." — Village elder of Vologda Oblast

Русалка

Rusalka

Also known as: Vodyanitsa, Kupalka, Shutovka, Lokhmatka

Waters
◈◈

She rises from the black water at dusk — skin pale as river foam, hair flowing like living waterweed, eyes the deep green of a summer pond. The Rusalka was once a young woman who died unbaptised, or drowned herself from a broken heart, or was cursed before her wedding day. Now she dwells in the water forever, neither dead nor alive, beautiful and terrible as the river itself.

In the week of the Rusalki (Rusalka Week, the seventh week after Easter), they climb from their rivers to dance in the moonlit fields. They comb their endless hair with fingers like white water-lilies, and sing songs that make strong men weep and weak men walk into the water. Their laughter sounds like bubbles rising through deep pools.

Habitat & Domain

Deep pools in rivers, millponds, lakes with dark bottoms, and any body of water where a death has occurred. Each Rusalka claims a specific stretch of water — the bend where she drowned, the pool where the current eddies and the water is always cold, even in August.

Behavior & Nature

Rusalki are most dangerous during Rusalka Week, when they leave the water entirely. They are playful in a cruel way — tickling men to death, pulling swimmers beneath the surface with hands cold as river stones. Yet some tales tell of Rusalki who loved the living, who would secretly help a kind farmer with his crops, or warn a pure-hearted maiden of danger.

Wards & Protections

Cross yourself before entering any body of water at dusk. Carry a cross of wormwood or garlic. During Rusalka Week, avoid rivers entirely after sundown. If you hear singing from the water, do not look — the Rusalka's reflection shows not your face, but your death. Offerings of eggs and cloth left on the riverbank may appease them.

Collected Sayings

"Her song is the river's song, and the river has drowned better men than you." — Fisherman's warning, Volga region
"A Rusalka's tears are the morning dew. If you find a blade of grass wetter than its neighbors at dawn, walk the other way." — Village saying, Smolensk

Баба Яга

Baba Yaga

Also known as: Yaga Yagishna, Baba Yagishna, Yeh Visage, The Bone-Legged

Threshold
☠

She who dwells at the edge of the world, where the living forest meets the realm of the dead. Baba Yaga — the Bone-Legged, the Iron-Nosed, the Terrifying. Her hut stands upon a single giant chicken leg, turning and shifting so that no one finds it unless it wishes to be found. A fence of human bones surrounds it, each post crowned with a skull whose eyes burn red in the darkness.

She flies through the night sky in a giant wooden mortar, steering with a pestle, sweeping away her tracks with a broom of birch. She is ancient beyond reckoning — older than the oldest tree, older than the deepest well. She devours the impure of heart without mercy, yet to the brave and the clever, she grants wisdom, fire, and gifts of terrible power. She is not evil. She is nature — the cold truth that does not care whether you live or die.

Habitat & Domain

At the threshold between worlds — the far side of the thrice-nine lands, in the thrice-tenth kingdom, where the border between the living and dead grows thin. Her hut stands in a clearing that exists in all times at once. The path to her door is different for every traveler.

Behavior & Nature

Baba Yaga tests all who come to her. She sets impossible tasks — sort the poppy seeds from the earth, clean her impossible hut, cook a feast for twelve with nothing. Fail, and she eats you. Succeed, and she gives you what you need — a skull of fire, a magical comb, a towel that becomes a bridge. She respects courage, cleverness, and good manners above all else.

Wards & Protections

Never enter her hut without greeting her first. Bring salt and bread. Do not ask for anything — state your need plainly. If she offers food, refuse politely (it may be enchanted). Keep your answers short and honest. The word "Нет" spoken with conviction is your strongest shield. If pursued, throw salt over your left shoulder and run toward the rising sun.

Collected Sayings

"There is no monster in the forest so terrible as the one who tests your heart." — Old tale of the Firebird
"Baba Yaga does not eat the brave. She has no taste for courage — it is as bitter as unripe berries." — Village grandmother, Kostroma

Змѣй Горыныч

Zmey Gorynych

Also known as: Zmey, Zmei, Zmij, The Mountain Dragon, Three-Headed Serpent

Sky
☠

The Zmey Gorynych — born of the mountain itself, its three heads each thinking its own terrible thoughts. The left head schemes, the right head rages, and the center head knows all that the other two plan. From its mouths pour not one but three kinds of fire: the fire that burns flesh, the fire that burns spirit, and the fire that burns memory itself.

It darkens the sky when it flies — wings vast as thunderclouds, casting shadows over entire villages. Its scales are harder than iron, black as a starless night, and each scale is inscribed with a name — the name of a warrior who has challenged the beast and failed. It hoards not gold but brides — beautiful maidens stolen from villages, kept in a cave of ice atop the highest mountain.

Habitat & Domain

Mountain peaks, volcanic caverns, and caves of eternal ice. The Zmey Gorynych makes its lair in the Kuchelaar Mountains or in caves beneath the Carpathians, where the earth breathes heat and the stones glow red at night. It nests in places where the boundary between this world and the underworld is thin as spider silk.

Behavior & Nature

The Zmey is intelligent, cunning, and possesses a cruel sense of humor. It enjoys riddles and will sometimes spare a challenger who can pose a question it cannot answer. It is vain and can be flattered into carelessness. Each of its three heads argues with the others, and in these moments of internal discord, the beast is most vulnerable. Cut off one head, and it will regrow — unless the stump is cauterized with holy fire.

Wards & Protections

The Zmey fears the sound of church bells, which remind it of the voice of Perun. A cross carved into stone will bar its path. The herb chernobyl(wormwood) burned as incense confuses its three noses. Water from a spring that flows uphill will extinguish its fires. Only a hero of pure lineage and pure heart can truly slay the Zmey — and even then, only by striking all three necks in a single blow.

Collected Sayings

"Three heads, three hundred tricks, and not one true thought among them." — Dobrynya Nikitich, Dragon-Slayer
"When the sky goes dark at midday and the wind smells of sulfur, the Zmey is hungry. Bar your doors and say your prayers." — Village chronicle, Kiev

Водяной

Vodyanoy

Also known as: Vodyanoi, Vodnik, The Water Grandfather, Topielec

Waters
◈

The Water Grandfather — bloated, ancient, and perpetually damp. His body is covered in black scales and green algae, his eyes are as large and round as millstones, and his beard trails behind him like a living river of waterweed. He sits upon a great submerged log of black wood, and from this throne he commands every fish, every frog, every drop of water in his domain.

He was not always a creature of the water — the elders say he is the soul of a man who drowned without blessing, condemned to serve the river for all eternity. He is jealous of his territory and demands tribute from every fisherman and every mill. Pay him the first catch of the season, and the nets will always be full. Ignore him, and he will tear the mill-stones from their axles and drag the careless swimmer to the bottom.

Habitat & Domain

Deep pools beneath waterfalls, millponds, river bends where the current swirls counterclockwise, and any body of water deep enough to drown a horse. The Vodyanoy's true palace lies at the bottom of the deepest pool — a castle of black stone where he keeps the souls of the drowned as his servants.

Behavior & Nature

The Vodyanoy is greedy, moody, and surprisingly social. He sometimes visits villages in the guise of a wet old man, dripping and smelling of pond-water, to trade fish for tobacco. He is especially active at night, when he rides the river as a great fish or a floating log. He favors black animals — a black goat or black rooster left on the bank will please him greatly.

Wards & Protections

Always toss the first fish back into the water with a word of thanks. Never swim at noon or midnight — these are the Vodyanoy's hours. If caught in his grip, offer him tobacco or bread soaked in blood — he may accept the trade. Cross yourself before entering the water. Pigs, being sacred to the water, are immune — a pig can swim where a man would drown.

Collected Sayings

"The river always takes its due. The only question is whether you pay it willingly or not." — Miller's wisdom, along the Dnieper

Домовой

Domovoi

Also known as: Dedushka, Khozyain, The House Master, Stopan

Threshold
◌

The little grandfather who lives behind the hearth — no taller than a child's knee, with a beard of grey wool and eyes that glow like embers. Every house has one, or should have one. The Domovoi is the spirit of the home itself, bound to the family that dwells there, sharing their joys and sorrows across generations.

He is the most benevolent of all the spirits — protecting the household from fire, thieves, and malevolent creatures of the night. He tends the livestock while the family sleeps, combs the horses' manes (which is why they shine in the morning), and warns of coming misfortune by knocking, rattling dishes, or pulling the housewife's hair gently in the night. He is a member of the family, not a servant, and demands to be treated as such.

Habitat & Domain

The Domovoi lives behind the stove, under the threshold, or in the attic. His presence is strongest at the four corners of the house and at the threshold — the boundary between the family's world and the wild outside. When a family moves, they must invite the Domovoi to come with them, carrying coals from the old hearth to the new.

Behavior & Nature

The Domovoi reflects the family he serves. A happy, orderly household makes a happy Domovoi; a family that fights and curses will have a Domovoi that growls and throws things. He plays tricks — hiding small objects, tangling thread, making the fire smoke — but these are gentle warnings to be more careful, more respectful of the home. He especially loves cats, and a cat with a tuft of fur on its ears is said to be his favorite.

Wards & Protections

Leave a bowl of porridge behind the stove at night. On the feast of the Domovoi (October 10th), set aside a special meal and speak words of thanks. Never curse in the home — the Domovoi takes it personally. When moving to a new house, carry the old Domovoi in a pot of coals and say: "Дедушка домовой, пойдем с нами жить." (Grandfather Domovoi, come live with us.)

Collected Sayings

"A house without a Domovoi is like a body without a soul — it stands, but it is not alive." — Housewife's wisdom, Novgorod
"If you lose your keys at night, do not search with anger. Apologize to the Domovoi, and by morning they will be found." — Domestic saying, widespread

Кикимора

Kikimora

Also known as: Kikimora Bolotnaya (Marsh Kikimora), Shishimora, The Nightmare Spirit

Threshold
◌◌

She comes at night, when the fire burns low and the household sleeps. The Kikimora — thin as a spindle, small as a child, with a voice like the creak of old floorboards. She sits in the darkest corner and spins, spins, spins — her thread is made of nightmares, and every spindleful brings another restless night to the household.

There are two kinds: the house Kikimora, who dwells in the home, and the marsh Kikimora, who rises from the swamp and attaches herself to a man who builds his house on cursed ground. The house Kikimora is mischievous — she tangles yarn, breaks dishes, and whistles in the dark. The marsh Kikimora is darker — she suffocates sleepers, drives cattle mad, and can be heard at night stamping her tiny wet feet in the cellar.

Habitat & Domain

House Kikimori live in dark corners, behind furniture, in cellars, and in the space between walls. Marsh Kikimori live in swamps and bogs, attaching themselves to homes built on former wetlands. They are most active after dark, between midnight and the third cock's crow.

Behavior & Nature

The house Kikimora punishes laziness and disorder. A home that is dirty, where the wife neglects her spinning, or where the husband is cruel, will attract a Kikimora. She tangles thread, breaks pots, and causes insomnia. The marsh Kikimora is more dangerous — she enters through keyholes, sits on sleepers' chests, and can cause madness. Both can be appeased by cleanliness and order.

Wards & Protections

Keep your home clean and orderly. Never leave a broom standing bristle-up at night. Place a piece of bread and a bowl of milk in the cellar on Fridays. Turn your shoes toe-outward before bed. If plagued by a Kikimora, turn all your clothes inside out and place a smudge stick of wormwood in each corner. A broom placed across the threshold will bar her entry.

Collected Sayings

"The Kikimora does not enter a clean house. Laziness is the door she opens." — Saying of the northern villages

Чернобог

Chernobog

Also known as: The Black God, Crni Bog, Czarnobog, The Dark One, Zerneboh

Underworld
☠

The Black God — he who dwells in the root of the world-tree, where the light of the sun has never reached. Where Belobog brings the dawn, Chernobog brings the endless night. Where one creates, the other unmakes. He is not evil in the way a man is evil — he is entropy itself, the necessary darkness without which light would have no meaning.

His presence is felt rather than seen — a cold wind from the north, a shadow that falls across the sun at midday, the sudden silence of birds. He speaks in the creak of frozen branches, in the howl of wolves on the winter solstice, in the silence of a house where someone has just died. Those who have seen him and survived describe a figure of absolute darkness, a void in the shape of a man, with eyes like dying stars.

Habitat & Domain

The roots of the world-tree, the space beneath the earth where the dead gather, the dark side of the moon, and every place where shadow pools too thickly to be natural. He has no single dwelling — he is present in every darkness, every ending, every silence between heartbeats.

Behavior & Nature

Chernobog does not act — he simply is. He is the force that ensures all things end, all fires cool, all stars go dark. He is not cruel; cruelty requires intent. He is the winter that kills the harvest, the frost that cracks the stone, the age that weakens the bone. The Slavs did not worship him in temples, but they acknowledged him in their hearts — for without the Black God, the White God's gifts would be meaningless.

Wards & Protections

You cannot ward against Chernobog, for he is the end of all things. But you can delay him: keep your hearth-fire burning, tend your relationships, plant in spring what you would harvest in autumn. Light is his opposite — candles at the window, fire in the hearth, the first torch lit at dawn. The cross and the circle-sun (kolovrat) are his bane. Above all, do not speak his name at night, in darkness, or in winter — for he listens when he is named.

Collected Sayings

"The Black God does not chase you. He simply waits, and you come to him." — Helgoland Chronicle

Банник

Bannik

Also known as: Banny, Banya Spirit, The Bathhouse Guardian, Laznik

Threshold
◌◌

The spirit of the bathhouse — wild, naked, covered in leaves and dirt from the birch branches used for steam. His hair is long and matted, his skin pale from the heat, and his eyes glow faintly in the steam. He is the guardian of the banya, that sacred and profane place where the body is cleansed and the boundary between worlds grows thin as steam.

The banya is a liminal space — too hot for the living, too damp for the dead, perfect for the Bannik. Women give birth in the banya. The dead are washed there. Fortune-telling happens there on Christmas Eve. The Bannik sees all of this and judges accordingly. A clean, respectful bather who asks permission before entering will be left alone. A drunk, loud, or disrespectful visitor may be scalded, strangled with a birch branch, or turned around so many times they stumble out into the snow and freeze.

Habitat & Domain

The bathhouse, always built at the edge of the yard, farthest from the home — for the Bannik is wild and must be kept at a distance. He dwells behind the hot stones, under the benches, and in the darkest corner where the steam collects. The third steaming (the third batch of water thrown on the stones) is his — no human should be present during it.

Behavior & Nature

The Bannik is a diviner — if you stand in the doorway with your back exposed, he will stroke your back softly if the future is good, or claw it if misfortune awaits. He enjoys a clean bathhouse and fresh birch brooms. He is jealous of his space and becomes angry if the bathhouse is used for washing clothes or other mundane purposes. He sometimes invites Rusalki and other spirits to bathe with him.

Wards & Protections

Always say: "Банник, банник, пусти попариться" (Bannik, Bannik, let me steam) before entering. Never enter during the third steaming. Leave a basin of clean water and a piece of soap for the Bannik after each visit. If the bathhouse catches fire, walk around it with bread and salt — the Bannik's anger can be soothed. Never whistle in the bathhouse.

Collected Sayings

"The bathhouse is a church of steam. Enter it as you would enter a temple — with respect and a clean heart." — Northern village saying

Полудница

Poludnitsa

Also known as: Lady Midday, Noonwraith, Rzhunitsa, The Harvest Maiden

Sky
◈

She appears at high noon, when the sun stands directly overhead and shadows vanish beneath your feet. A tall woman in white — or sometimes a young girl in a wheat wreath, or a hunched crone with fingers like reeds. She carries a sickle, enormous and gleaming, and she walks the golden fields asking questions. Answer correctly, and she lets you go. Answer wrongly, and she takes your head.

She is the spirit of the noon hour — that dangerous moment when the sun is at its peak and the world holds its breath. She punishes those who work in the field at midday (for even the sun rests at noon), and she tests the wisdom of those she encounters. Her questions are riddles of the harvest, of the seasons, of life and death. She is the heat-stroke made manifest, the shimmer above the wheat given form and fury.

Habitat & Domain

Wheat fields, rye fields, and any grain field at high noon during the summer months, particularly during harvest season. She appears most frequently on the hottest days of July, when the air shimmers and the cicadas scream. She is bound to the field — she cannot cross into forest or village unless invited.

Behavior & Nature

Poludnitsa poses riddles and enforces the noon rest. She may grab a worker's hair and hold them motionless until sunset, or tickle them until they lose their mind. She sometimes helps with the harvest, cutting grain with impossible speed, but her help comes at a price — she demands songs, stories, or a lock of hair. She is less malicious than she is absolute — the noon hour belongs to her, and woe to those who trespass.

Wards & Protections

Stop all work at noon — this is the law of the Poludnitsa. If you must work, wear a cross and carry wormwood. If you encounter her, answer her riddles with confidence, even if you are guessing. Never show fear. If she grabs your hair, recite the Lord's Prayer backwards (a dangerous gambit). The herb odolen (valerian) worn in the hat protects against her. Crossed sickles placed at the field's edge form a barrier she cannot cross.

Collected Sayings

"At noon, the sun sleeps, and the Poludnitsa wakes. Work in her hour, and you will not finish your harvest." — Farmer's almanac, Tambov region

Навка

Navka

Also known as: Mavka, Navia, Nyavka, Lalka, The Unbaptised Dead

Underworld Forest
◈

The most sorrowful of spirits — the Navka is the soul of a child who died before baptism, or a young woman who perished before her time. They appear as beautiful maidens with hair like midnight and eyes that hold the sadness of all the years they will never live. But beware: from behind, they have no back. Their spine and ribs are visible, dark and glistening — for they were never given the protection of the sacred rite.

They dance in moonlit clearings, sing songs of unbearable beauty, and weave garlands of wildflowers. They are drawn to the living — especially to young men — with a longing that is both tender and terrible. They may tickle their victims to death (for they know no other way to touch), or lead them into the swamp to drown. Yet some tales tell of Navki who merely wish to be baptized, to find peace, to be remembered.

Habitat & Domain

Willow groves, riverbanks, marshes, and forest clearings where wildflowers grow thick. They are drawn to places where children used to play. During Green Week (the week after Pentecost), they emerge in great numbers to dance and sing. They cannot cross running water that has been blessed.

Behavior & Nature

Navki are driven by loneliness and a desperate desire for the life they were denied. They lure young men with beauty and song, then either kill them through tickling (a form of magical suffocation) or lead them into water to drown. They can be merciful if shown compassion — if you baptize them with holy water and speak their name with pity, they may find peace and disappear forever.

Wards & Protections

Baptize all children as soon as possible after birth. During Green Week, avoid forest clearings after dark. If you encounter a Navka, sprinkle holy water and say: "Во имя Отца, и Сына, и Святого Духа". Garlic and wormwood repel them. Iron nails driven into the earth around a clearing will trap them within. The most effective protection is pity — name the child, speak words of mourning, and the Navka may weep and depart in peace.

Collected Sayings

"She was beautiful as a summer morning, but her back was a wound that never healed." — Ballad of the Navka, Volhynia
