Fig. I — The Leshy, Lord of the Forest

Леший

Leshy

Peril
⍟ ⍟ ⍟ ⍟ ⍟
Domain: Deep Forest
Nature: Guardian & Trickster

The Leshy is the tutelary deity of the forests in Slavic mythology. He is the master of all woodland creatures, able to change his size from that of a blade of grass to the tallest tree. His appearance is that of a man, yet his features are wild: his hair and beard are made of moss and living grass, his skin is like bark, and his eyes glow with an emerald fire.

He is known to lead travelers astray, mimicking voices of loved ones to lure them deeper into the woods. Those who respect the forest and leave offerings of bread and salt may earn his favor, but those who harm his domain rarely find their way home.

◈ Shape-shifting
◈ Voice Mimicry
◈ Forest Control

Wards & Protections

Wear your clothes inside out. Carry a piece of bread baked with salt. Recite a prayer backwards. Never whistle in the forest after dusk.

Fig. II — The Rusalka, Spirit of the Waters

Русалка

Rusalka

Peril
⍟ ⍟ ⍟ ⍟ ⍟
Domain: Rivers, Lakes & Dark Waters
Nature: Vengeful Spirit

The Rusalka is a female spirit associated with water, often the ghost of a young woman who died a violent or untimely death near a body of water. By day she appears as a beautiful maiden with long, unbraided green or golden hair, combing it with a fish-bone comb as she sits upon the banks.

By night, her beauty becomes terrible. She lures young men with her enchanting songs, then drags them beneath the surface to drown them. During Rusalka Week in early June, she leaves the water to dance in the moonlit fields, and it is perilous for any mortal to cross her path.

❦ Enchanting Song
❦ Water Manipulation
❦ Terrifying Beauty

Wards & Protections

Throw wormwood or lovage into the water. Carry garlic and wear a cross of rowan wood. Never swim alone during Rusalka Week.

Fig. III — Baba Yaga, The Crone of the Woods

Баба Яга

Baba Yaga

Peril
⍟ ⍟ ⍟ ⍟ ⍟
Domain: The Deep Woods, The Hut on Chicken Legs
Nature: Ambiguous Witch

Baba Yaga is one of the most iconic figures in Slavic folklore, a ferocious-looking old witch who lives in a hut that stands on chicken legs and is surrounded by a fence made of human bones. She flies through the air in a mortar, steering with a pestle and sweeping away her tracks with a broom.

She is neither wholly evil nor wholly good. She may devour those who fail her impossible tasks, yet she also guides and gifts worthy heroes. Her bony leg, iron teeth, and long nose that touches the ceiling when she sleeps are all marks of her otherworldly nature.

✧ Flight in Mortar
✧ Dark Sorcery
✧ Animated Hut

Wards & Protections

Show unwavering courtesy. Address the hut with the phrase: "Hut, hut, turn your back to the forest, your front to me." Bring a gift of fresh milk and black bread.

Fig. IV — The Zmey, Dragon of the Mountains

Змей

Zmey

Peril
⍟ ⍟ ⍟ ⍟ ⍟
Domain: Mountains, Caves & Skies
Nature: Malevolent Dragon

The Zmey is a great dragon or serpent of Slavic legend, often depicted with three, six, or twelve heads that regenerate if not all severed at once. It dwells in mountain caves, hoarding treasure and demanding tribute of maidens from nearby villages.

Unlike the benevolent dragons of Eastern lore, the Slavic Zmey is almost always a force of destruction, breathing fire and bringing storms. It is the ultimate adversary for the hero, representing chaos and greed that must be vanquished to restore balance to the land.

☬ Fire Breath
☬ Storm Summoning
☬ Head Regeneration

Wards & Protections

Strike all heads with a single blow. Use a sword blessed by three waters. The herb raskovnik can unlock any cave it hides within.

Fig. V — The Domovoy, Guardian of the Hearth

Домовой

Domovoy

Peril
⍟ ⍟ ⍟ ⍟ ⍟
Domain: The Household
Nature: Protective House Spirit

The Domovoy is a household spirit who lives behind the stove or under the threshold. He appears as a small, bearded old man, covered in soft fur, and is generally benevolent, protecting the home and its inhabitants from harm.

However, if he is angered by slovenliness, disrespect, or if the family fails to leave him offerings, he becomes a poltergeist. He will cause milk to sour, break dishes, and make terrible noises at night. A happy Domovoy hums contentedly and braids the manes of horses.

⌂ Household Protection
⌂ Invisibility
⌂ Fortune Influence

Wards & Protections

Leave out bread, salt, and milk overnight. Keep a clean house. When moving, invite him to come with the family in an old shoe.

Fig. VI — The Kikimora, The Nightmare Spinner

Кикимора

Kikimora

Peril
⍟ ⍟ ⍟ ⍟ ⍟
Domain: The Home (Unclean Corners), The Swamp
Nature: Malevolent Trickster

The Kikimora is a female house spirit, but unlike the Domovoy, she is almost always malevolent. She is the wife of the Domovoy, or sometimes the Leshy, and she emerges from the darkest, most neglected corners of the house to torment the inhabitants.

She is described as a tiny, thin woman with a head the size of a thimble and a body as thin as a straw. She steals thread, tangles yarn, breaks pottery, and whistles in the night to induce nightmares. If she is not appeased, she will drive a family from their home with her endless mischief.

◈ Nightmare Weaving
◈ Object Animation
◈ Whistling Curses

Wards & Protections

Sweep the house with a juniper broom. Hang a stone with a natural hole (a "chicken god") near the door. Never leave spinning fiber out overnight.
