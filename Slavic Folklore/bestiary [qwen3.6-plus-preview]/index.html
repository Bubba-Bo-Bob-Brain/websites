— 1 — Chapter I
The Leshy — Guardian of the Deep Forest

The Leshy

Леший — Guardian of the Deep Forest

Domain Deep Forest
Origin East Slavic
Danger ☽ ⚔ ✦ ☠

Tall as the ancient pines when he wills it, small as a blade of grass when he wishes to hide — the Leshy is the master and guardian of the forest, a shape-shifting spirit whose laughter echoes through the birch groves and whose footsteps leave no trace upon the forest floor.

He appears as a peasant, yet his eyes gleam with an unnatural light, his boots are fastened on the wrong feet, and his shadow does not match his form. Travelers who wander off the marked path may find themselves hopelessly lost, led in circles by a force they cannot comprehend.

Yet he is not wholly malicious. Those who show respect to the forest — who ask permission before cutting a branch, who leave offerings of bread and salt at the forest's edge — may find the Leshy a benevolent guide, leading them safely to their destination or warning them of dangers ahead.

⚔ Warnings for the Traveler

  • Never enter the forest without crossing yourself and asking permission aloud.
  • Turn your garments inside out if you suspect the Leshy is leading you astray.
  • Do not answer if you hear your name called from the deep woods.
  • Leave an offering of bread and salt at the forest boundary before entering.

— 2 — Chapter II
The Rusalka — Spirit of the Dark Waters

The Rusalka

Русалка — Spirit of the Dark Waters

Domain Rivers & Lakes
Origin East Slavic
Danger ☽ ⚔ ✦ ☠

Born of drowned maidens — those who met tragic ends in rivers, lakes, and wells — the Rusalka is a creature of terrible beauty. By day she slumbers beneath the surface, her hair fanning like dark waterweed through the cold depths. By moonlight she rises, dancing upon the banks with a grace that entrances all who witness it.

Her voice is sweeter than any nightingale's song, and men who hear it are drawn irresistibly toward the water. She laughs as she dances, a sound like silver bells ringing across the still surface of a midnight lake. But behind that laughter lies a sorrow so profound it has curdled into something dark and hungry.

Those she draws into the water never return. Their bodies are found days later, bloated and pale, with strange marks upon their faces — not of violence, but of rapturous terror, as though they died in the grip of a beauty too terrible to survive.

⚔ Warnings for the Traveler

  • Never swim alone after dusk during the week of Rusalnaya (early June).
  • Do not approach a figure dancing alone by moonlit water, no matter how beautiful.
  • Carry a branch of wormwood or blessed thistle when traveling near rivers at night.
  • If you hear laughter from the water, cross yourself and walk away without looking back.

— 3 — Chapter III
Baba Yaga — The Witch of the Iron Wood

Baba Yaga

Баба Яга — The Witch of the Iron Wood

Domain The Deep Woods
Origin Pan-Slavic
Danger ☽ ⚔ ✦ ☠

She lives in a hut that stands upon chicken legs, turning on command at her whispered word. A fence of human bones surrounds her domain, each skull atop a post glowing with an eerie inner fire — a lantern for those lost in the iron wood. Baba Yaga flies through the night sky in a great mortar, propelling herself with a pestle and steering with a broom of silver birch.

She is neither wholly good nor purely evil — she is the forest itself, ancient and indifferent, bound by rules older than memory. Those who approach her with respect and courage may find her a source of impossible wisdom and gifts beyond imagining. Those who come with greed or deception will find her oven waiting, hungry and hot.

Her nose is so long it touches the ceiling when she sleeps. Her teeth are iron. She speaks in riddles and tests those who seek her aid with tasks that seem impossible — tasks that separate the worthy from the foolish, the brave from the cowardly.

⚔ Warnings for the Traveler

  • Speak politely and use the proper forms of address: "Grandmother" or "Baba Yaga, mistress of the wood."
  • Never refuse a task she sets before you — failure means death.
  • Accept food and drink with caution; some say eating her food binds you to her service.
  • The hut must be commanded to "turn its back to the forest and its front to you" before approaching.

— 4 — Chapter IV
The Zmey — Serpent of the Mountain Caverns

The Zmey

Змей — Serpent of the Mountain Caverns

Domain Mountains & Caverns
Origin South Slavic
Danger ☽ ⚔ ✦ ☠

The Zmey is a dragon of immense power and cunning, a creature of fire and storm that makes its lair in the highest mountain peaks where the clouds gather and the lightning strikes. Unlike the mindless beasts of western legend, the Zmey is intelligent, articulate, and possessed of a pride as vast as the skies it commands.

It is said that a Zmey may have three, seven, or even twelve heads, each with its own will and voice. When one head is severed, two may grow in its place — a fact that has claimed the lives of many overconfident heroes. Its breath is fire, its scales are harder than iron, and its gaze can wither crops and poison wells.

Yet the Zmey is also capable of taking human form — that of a darkly handsome man who may court mortal women and father children of great power. In some tales, the Zmey is a protector of the land; in others, a terrible invader who demands tribute of gold and maidens.

⚔ Warnings for the Traveler

  • Never challenge a Zmey to combat — only a hero blessed by the gods can prevail.
  • If a storm approaches with unnatural speed, seek shelter immediately; it may be a Zmey in flight.
  • Do not accept gifts from a stranger of great beauty and dark eyes — it may be the Zmey in disguise.
  • Fire and blessed steel are the only weapons said to harm its scales.

— 5 — Chapter V
The Vodyanoy — Master of the Drowned Depths

The Vodyanoy

Водяной — Master of the Drowned Depths

Domain Deep Waters
Origin East Slavic
Danger ☽ ⚔ ✦ ☠

Beneath the surface of every deep lake and slow river, the Vodyanoy holds court in a palace of waterweed and drowned timber. He is the male counterpart to the Rusalka — older, more powerful, and infinitely more dangerous. His body is bloated and dark, his skin covered in slime and algae, his eyes bulging like those of a frog. A crown of drowned reeds sits upon his head, and his beard trails behind him like a net of living weeds.

The Vodyanoy is the master of all waters within his domain. He commands the fish, controls the currents, and decides who may safely cross his waters and who will be pulled beneath. Millers and fishermen make offerings to him — a drop of oil, a pinch of tobacco, the first catch of the season — for without his favor, the waters turn hostile and the nets come up empty.

When angered, he breaks dams, floods villages, and drowns the careless. He is said to keep the souls of those he has drowned as his servants, forcing them to tend his underwater gardens for all eternity.

⚔ Warnings for the Traveler

  • Never swim in unfamiliar waters without first making a small offering to the Vodyanoy.
  • Millers should pour a libation of oil into the millrace each spring to ensure the Vodyanoy does not break the dam.
  • If the water suddenly grows cold while swimming, leave immediately — the Vodyanoy is reaching for your ankles.
  • The first fish caught each day should be thrown back as tribute.

— 6 — Chapter VI
The Domovoi — Keeper of the Hearth and Home

The Domovoi

Домовой — Keeper of the Hearth and Home

Domain The Hearth & Home
Origin Pan-Slavic
Danger ☽ ⚔ ✦ ☠

Behind the stove, in the warm shadows of the hearth, lives the Domovoi — the household spirit, the guardian of the home. He is small and old, covered in soft grey hair, with eyes that gleam like embers in the firelight. He is rarely seen, but his presence is felt in every creak of the floorboards, every warmth of the hearth, every moment when something lost suddenly reappears exactly where you first looked.

The Domovoi is the spirit of the household itself, bound to the home and the family that dwells within it. He is gentle and protective when treated with respect — he tends the fire, watches over sleeping children, and warns of impending danger by pulling hair or knocking on walls. But neglect him, and the house itself turns against its inhabitants: food spoils, tools go missing, and the nights grow cold no matter how much wood is fed to the fire.

When a family moves to a new home, they must invite the Domovoi to come with them, calling his name and leaving offerings of bread and salt. A Domovoi left behind will howl through the empty house, and the new inhabitants will know no peace.

⚔ Warnings for the Traveler

  • Always leave a small bowl of milk or a crust of bread behind the stove for the Domovoi.
  • When moving, call out: "Domovoi, Domovoi, come with us to our new home" and leave an offering.
  • Never whistle inside the house — it angers the Domovoi and invites misfortune.
  • If you hear gentle knocking at night, it is the Domovoi warning you — heed it.

— 7 — Chapter VII
The Kikimora — Wraith of the Swamp and Shadow

The Kikimora

Кикимора — Wraith of the Swamp and Shadow

Domain Swamps & Homes
Origin Pan-Slavic
Danger ☽ ⚔ ✦ ☠

She is the spirit of the liminal places — the swamp, the marsh, the dark corner behind the stove, the threshold between the living world and the realm of shadows. The Kikimora is thin as a reed, pale as fog, and nearly invisible to the mortal eye. She is seen most clearly in the corner of one's vision, a pale shape that vanishes when stared at directly.

There are two kinds of Kikimora: the swamp Kikimora, who lures travelers into the bog with her ceaseless spinning, and the house Kikimora, who lives behind the stove and tangles the household's flax and thread while they sleep. The house Kikimora is the wife of the Domovoi, but where he is protective, she is mischievous and petty, harboring grudges that last for generations.

Her spinning is the most feared sound in the Slavic household — the soft whir of a wheel turning in the dead of night. Those who hear it know that a death in the family is imminent, for the Kikimora spins the thread of fate, and when her spinning stops, so does a life.

⚔ Warnings for the Traveler

  • If you hear spinning at night, place a needle and thread on the windowsill — it will distract her.
  • Never build a house on land that was once a swamp; the Kikimora will never leave.
  • Keep your spinning tools covered at night and never leave thread untangled.
  • A broom placed handle-down behind the door will keep the Kikimora at bay.

— 8 — Chapter VIII
The Firebird — Жар-птица, The Blazing Omen

The Firebird

Жар-птица — The Blazing Omen

Domain The Sky & Gardens
Origin Pan-Slavic
Danger ☽ ⚔ ✦ ☠

She is beauty incarnate, a bird of impossible splendor whose feathers glow with the light of a thousand embers. When she flies, her wings illuminate the night sky as though the dawn has come early, and the single feather she drops — worth more than any king's ransom — continues to glow with warmth and light long after she has vanished into the stars.

The Firebird dwells in a magical garden beyond the edge of the known world, in a golden cage hung from a tree of silver leaves and crystal fruit. She eats only golden apples, and her song can heal the sick and blind, restore the lame, and make the old young again. But she cannot be captured — not truly. Those who catch her do so only because she allows it, and she always finds a way to return to her garden.

Many heroes have quested for the Firebird, lured by the promise of her feather or her song. Most never return from their journey. Those who do come back changed — their eyes forever holding a glimmer of impossible light, their dreams filled with a garden they can never forget and can never find again.

⚔ Warnings for the Traveler

  • Do not pursue the Firebird — she chooses who is worthy, and pursuit leads only to ruin.
  • If you find a glowing feather, guard it with your life; it brings both blessing and terrible burden.
  • The Firebird's golden apples are not for mortal hands — to steal them is to invite catastrophe.
  • Those who have seen the Firebird are forever restless; the ordinary world will never satisfy them again.

Colophon

◆

This bestiary has been compiled from the oral traditions, folk tales, and whispered warnings of the Slavic peoples — from the dense forests of Russia to the misty rivers of Ukraine, from the mountain villages of the Balkans to the coastal settlements of the Baltic.

The creatures herein are not merely stories. They are the living memory of a people who understood that the world is older and stranger than we dare admit, and that respect for the unknown is the first wisdom.

Keep these pages close. Read them by firelight. And remember: the forest is always watching.
