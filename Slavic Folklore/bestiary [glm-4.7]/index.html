The Leshy

Also known as: The Forest Lord, Lesovik

Habitat

Deep pine forests, birch groves.

Prowess

Shape-shifting, Mimicry, Size manipulation.

The Leshy is the tutelary spirit of the forests in Slavic mythology. He is not inherently malevolent, but his moods shift with the seasons. He protects the wild animals and the trees, but he is known to lead travelers astray, causing them to loop endlessly through the woods until they perish from exhaustion.

He can change his size from that of a blade of grass to a towering fir tree. His appearance is often described as anthropomorphic but with features of the forest: blue blood, pale skin, and hair made of vines and moss. He sometimes has a tail, hooves, or horns. The Leshy is the shepherd of the forest wolves and often rides them.

How to Ward Him: To escape the Leshy's maze, you must turn your clothes inside out and wear your shoes on the wrong feet.

The Rusalka

Also known as: The Water Maiden, The Drowned One

Habitat

Riverbanks, Lakes, Unmowed fields.

Prowess

Hydrokinesis, Hypnotic Song, Deadly Tickle.

Often confused with mermaids, the Rusalka are the vengeful spirits of women who died by drowning—often due to violent circumstances or suicide. They emerge from the water at night, climbing the branches of willow or birch trees to sing enchanting songs.

Exquisitely beautiful, with pale translucent skin and flowing green hair, they use their allure to lure young men into the water. Once the victim is close enough, the Rusalka wraps her long hair around his limbs and drags him to the river floor to his death, where he may become a spirit servant.

How to Ward Her: Carry a piece of wormwood or iron. Never look directly at her reflection in the water.

Baba Yaga

Also known as: The Bony Leg, The Arch-Witch

Habitat

The Thrice-Tenth Kingdom, Deep Woods.

Prowess

Flight, Mortar & Pestle combat, Barrier Magic.

Baba Yaga is perhaps the most famous and fearsome figure in Slavic folklore. She is a supernatural being (or a series of witches) who lives in a hut that stands on giant chicken legs. The hut is surrounded by a fence made of human bones and skulls with glowing eye sockets.

She flies through the air in a giant mortar, using the pestle to steer and sweeping away her tracks with a broom made of silver birch. She is neither wholly good nor evil; she is a guardian of the fountains of the water of life and a tester of heroes. However, she eats those who fail her tests or are rude to her.

How to Ward Her: Politeness is your only shield. If you offer the right gift or solve her riddle, she may aid you instead of boiling you for dinner.

The Zmey

Also known as: Zmey Gorynych, The Dragon

Habitat

Mountains, Caves, The Underworld.

Prowess

Fire breath, Flight, Regeneration.

The Zmey is a Slavic dragon, distinct from its Western counterparts. Often depicted with multiple heads (most commonly three, but sometimes up to twelve), the Zmey is a symbol of chaos and destruction. Like the Hydra, cutting off one head often results in two growing back unless the stump is cauterized.

Despite their beastly nature, Zmeys are sometimes intelligent and capable of speech. They are known to kidnap princesses and hoard treasure. They represent the harsh, untamable forces of nature, capable of scorching the earth and blocking the rivers with their bodies.

How to Ward Him: Only a hero of legendary strength and a magical sword forged in the earth's core can slay the Zmey.

The Vodyanoy

Also known as: The Water Spirit, Vodnik

Habitat

Murky Ponds, Mill wheels, Deep Swamps.

Prowess

Drowning, Flood control, Animal control.

The Vodyanoy is the male water spirit, master of the depths and the drowned. He is often portrayed as an old man with a long, tangled beard covered in slime and river mud. His body is covered in scales and algae, and his eyes burn like coals.

He is malicious compared to the Rusalka. He enjoys drowning those who swim at midnight or after sunset. He is said to keep the souls of the drowned in porcelain cups on his shelf. Local fishermen used to offer him the first catch and tobacco to prevent him from capsizing their boats.

How to Ward Him: Do not swim after sunset. Feed him tobacco or black hens to appease his hunger.
