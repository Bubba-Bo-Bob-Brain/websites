I

Spirits of the Forest

ᛉ 🌲

Leshy

Леший
Classification: Forest Spirit — Lord of the Woods
Danger: ᛉ ᛉ ᛉ ᛉ ᛉ Alert

The Leshy is the ancient guardian and master of the forest, a shapeshifting spirit who leads travelers astray with his echoing laughter and twisting paths. He appears as a tall man with bark-like skin, glowing green eyes, and hair of living moss and lichen. His shadow stretches east at dawn and west at dusk, never matching the sun's direction — the surest sign of his presence.

Habitat Deep old-growth forests, birch groves, crossroads of woodland trails
Appearance Shapeshifter; often a tall figure with bark skin, antlers, and backward-pointing feet
Behavior Capricious; may guide the lost to safety or lead them deeper into the woods
Appeasement Leave bread at crossroads; turn clothing inside-out; never whistle in his domain

"When the trees close in and the path you walked is gone, you have entered the Leshy's parlor. Be polite, for he decides whether you leave."

— The Chronicle of Polesian Wanderers
ᛜ 🏠

Domovoy

Домово́й
Classification: Household Spirit — Guardian of the Hearth
Danger: ᛜ ᛜ ᛜ ᛜ ᛜ Watchful

The Domovoy is the diminutive spirit who dwells beneath the hearth of every home, a keeper of domestic order and quiet protector of the household. He appears as a small, bearded old man covered in soft fur, no taller than a five-year-old child. When respected, he tends the fire, protects livestock, and warns of coming misfortune. When angered, he spills milk, tangles yarn, and rattles the pots at night.

Habitat Beneath the hearth, behind the stove, in the shadows of the home
Appearance Small, furry old man; sometimes seen as a cat or dog near the hearth
Behavior Benevolent when honored; mischievous when neglected or disrespected
Appeasement Leave a dish of porridge or bread near the stove; keep the hearth clean

"A quiet house is a house the Domovoy has fled. Better the rattle of pots than the silence of his absence."

— Village Proverb, Novgorod Region
ᛉ ♨

Bannik

Ба́нник
Classification: Bathhouse Spirit — Keeper of Steam & Shadow
Danger: ᛉ ᛉ ᛉ ᛉ ᛉ Alert

The Bannik is the fierce and unpredictable spirit of the bathhouse, a liminal space between the clean and the unclean, the living and the dead. He manifests as an old man with long claws and eyes that glow through the steam. The third or fourth batch of steam belongs to him — to enter the bathhouse during his turn is to invite scalding, madness, or worse. He may invite forest spirits and demons to bathe with him in the darkest hours.

Habitat Traditional Slavic bathhouses (banya), particularly behind the stove bench
Appearance Old man with claws, glowing eyes, covered in soot and steam
Behavior Highly temperamental; can foretell the future or scald the unwary
Appeasement Never enter during the third or fourth steam; leave soap and water; knock before entering

"The banya has two doors — one for the living, one for the Bannik's guests. You cannot see the second, but they will see you."

— The Bathhouse Rites, Karelian Oral Tradition

II

Daughters of Water & Mist

ᛏ 🌊

Rusalka

Русалка
Classification: Water Spirit — Ghost of the Drowned Maiden
Danger: ᛏ ᛏ ᛏ ᛏ ᛏ Fear

The Rusalka is the restless spirit of a woman who drowned — whether by her own hand, by curse, or by the cruelty of others. She rises from lakes and rivers with pale, luminous skin and hair of dark green that never dries. She sits upon rocks and riverbanks, combing her hair with a bone comb and singing songs that no mortal can resist. Those who follow her voice into the water are pulled beneath and never return.

Habitat Lakes, rivers, forest pools; appears on banks during the new moon
Appearance Pale maiden with flowing green hair; translucent skin; often naked or in white shifts
Behavior Lures the living into water; especially dangerous during Rusalka Week in early June
Appeasement Wear wormwood in your hat; avoid swimming at night; tie red thread to birch trees near water

"She sang and the fisherman walked into the river with his boots still on. They found him smiling at the bottom, his lungs full of song."

— Tales of the Dnieper Crossing
ᛏ 🐸

Vodyanoy

Водяно́й
Classification: Water Demon — Lord of the Deep
Danger: ᛏ ᛏ ᛏ ᛏ ᛏ Fear

The Vodyanoy rules over lakes, rivers, and mill ponds as their terrible sovereign. He appears as a bloated old man with greenish skin, algae in his beard, and the red eyes of a boiled fish. His webbed hands and the long claws of his feet drag swimmers to the depths, where he keeps the drowned as his underwater servants. Millers and fishermen must pay him tribute, for a Vodyanoy denied his offering will smash dams and flood entire villages.

Habitat Deep lakes, mill ponds, river bottoms; dwells in submerged palaces of ice and silt
Appearance Bloated green man with red eyes, webbed hands, algae beard; sometimes appears as a log or fish
Behavior Demands tribute from those who use his waters; drowns those who anger him
Appeasement Offer first catch of fish or first flour from the mill; never swim after sunset

"The miller's son saw him rise from the pond, dripping with century-mud and crowned with river pearls. He said: 'The deep does not forgive debts.'"

— Millers' Ledger, Volhynia, 1582

III

Witches & Weavers of Fate

ᚦ 🦴

Baba Yaga

Баба-Яга́
Classification: Witch — Ancient Crone of the Bone Fence
Danger: ᚦ ᚦ ᚦ ᚦ ᚦ Dread

Baba Yaga is the most feared and revered figure in all Slavic folklore — an ancient witch of impossible age who dwells in a hut that stands upon giant chicken legs, surrounded by a fence of human skulls topped with glowing eyes. She flies through the night sky in a mortar, steering with a pestle and sweeping away her tracks with a broom. She is neither good nor evil in the mortal sense; she tests those who seek her, devouring the foolish and aiding the worthy — though her aid often comes at a terrible price.

Habitat A hut on chicken legs deep in the darkest forest; the border between worlds
Appearance Terrifying crone with iron teeth, a long nose that touches the ceiling, and a single bony leg
Behavior Tests those who come; eats the unworthy; may aid those who show proper respect and cunning
Appeasement None — one does not appease Baba Yaga. One survives her tests by wit and purity of heart

"The fence of skulls watched Vasilisa as she approached. Their eyes burned like tallow candles, and the hut turned on its legs to face her. The old woman's voice came from within: 'Have you come of your own will, or were you sent?' And the right answer is always: 'Both, grandmother.'"

— The Tale of Vasilisa the Beautiful
ᛟ 🧵

Kikimora

Кикимора
Classification: Household Spirit — Spinner of Nightmares
Danger: ᛟ ᛟ ᛟ ᛟ ᛟ Wary

The Kikimora is a sinister house spirit, the dark counterpart to the benevolent Domovoy. She dwells behind the stove or beneath the floorboards, emerging at night to spin unspinnable thread, tangle the household's yarn, and pinch sleeping children. She is the cause of nightmares and sleep paralysis — the invisible weight upon the chest in the dark hours. In her swamp-dwelling form, she is the wife of the Leshy, a creature of muck and bramble who lures travelers into bogs.

Habitat Behind the stove, beneath floorboards; also swamps and marshes as Leshy's consort
Appearance Tiny, gaunt woman with a pin-like head; sometimes described as a shapeless shadow
Behavior Spins at night; causes nightmares; whistles to foretell disaster; torments lazy householders
Appeasement Keep the house clean; place a broken pot near the hearth; do not leave spinning unfinished at night

"If you wake and cannot move, and feel the weight upon your chest — that is her, spinning your breath into her thread."

— The Night-Spinner's Lullaby, Pskov

IV

Beasts of Scale & Shadow

ᚦ 🐉

Zmey Gorynych

Змей Горы́ныч
Classification: Dragon — The Three-Headed Scourge of Mountains
Danger: ᚦ ᚦ ᚦ ᚦ ᚦ Dread

Zmey Gorynych is the great three-headed dragon of Slavic legend, a beast of fire and ruin that dwells in the mountains and demands tribute of gold and maidens from the kingdoms below. Each of his three heads speaks with a different voice — one roars, one whispers lies, and one laughs at the despair of mortals. His scales are iron-hard, his breath is flame, and his tail can level a fortress wall. Only the greatest bogatyrs have ever faced him, and fewer still have survived.

Habitat Mountain caves, volcanic peaks; sometimes nests in ancient, ruined fortresses
Appearance Enormous three-headed dragon with iron scales, flame breath, and a devastating tail
Behavior Demands tribute; kidnaps princesses; burns villages; sleeps for centuries between rampages
Appeasement None — the Zmey cannot be appeased, only slain by a hero of extraordinary might

"Dobrynya rode to the Saracen Mountains and found the Zmey sunning its three heads upon the stone. 'I have come,' said the bogatyr. 'So have many before you,' said the first head. 'So have many before you,' said the second. The third head only laughed."

— The Bylina of Dobrynya Nikitich
ᚦ 👁

Likho

Ли́хо
Classification: Embodiment — The Personification of Evil & Misfortune
Danger: ᚦ ᚦ ᚦ ᚦ ᚦ Dread

Likho is not merely a creature — it is the very essence of misfortune made flesh. She appears as a tall, gaunt woman dressed in black, with a single enormous eye that burns with cold fire. Where Likho walks, crops wither, children sicken, and fortune drains from the world like water through sand. To meet her gaze is to be cursed with ruin. She cannot be killed, for she is older than the gods — she can only be outwitted, and even then, she always returns.

Habitat Everywhere and nowhere; crossroads, desolate places, the moments before catastrophe
Appearance Tall, emaciated woman in black with a single burning eye; sometimes appears as a one-eyed old man
Behavior Embodies misfortune itself; attaches to those who invite bad luck; cannot be destroyed
Appeasement None — Likho cannot be bargained with. The proverb says: "Do not wake Likho while it is quiet"

"The tailor shared his last crust with Likho, and she ate his shadow. The smith drove her away, and she took his sight. Only the fool who pretended not to see her was spared — for Likho cannot afflict those who do not acknowledge her."

— The Parable of Likho One-Eye
ᚦ ⛧

Chernobog

Чёрнобог
Classification: Deity — The Black God, Lord of Night & Ill Fortune
Danger: ᚦ ᚦ ᚦ ᚦ ᚦ Dread

Chernobog, the Black God, stands as the darkest power in the Slavic pantheon — the lord of night, misfortune, and the cold that creeps from the earth. He is the balance against Belobog, the White God of light and fortune. His name was spoken only in whispers, for to call upon Chernobog was to invite his attention, and his attention meant catastrophe. He is depicted as a towering figure of shadow with eyes like dying coals, his presence heralded by the sudden silence of all living things.

Habitat The void between stars; the deepest winter; the moment before death
Appearance Shadow made manifest; towering figure with coal-red eyes; sometimes as a great black beast
Behavior Embodies all that is dark and unfortunate; receives offerings to prevent his notice
Appeasement Offerings at crossroads during the new moon; never speak his name after dark

"Helmold wrote that the Slavs, when feasting, would pass a cup over which they spoke words — not of prayer, but of propitiation. 'I give this to you, Black God, that you may not take more.' For what Chernobog claims, no one can reclaim."

— Helmold's Chronica Slavorum, 1168 AD
