The Black Death

❧
🐀

The Arrival

In the year of our Lord 1347, twelve Genoese ships docked at the Sicilian port of Messina. The harbormasters who boarded the vessels found a horror beyond comprehension — nearly all sailors lay dead, and those few still clinging to life were covered in black boils that oozed blood and pus.

💀

The Spread

Within five years, the pestilence claimed the lives of over 25 million souls — nearly one-third of Europe's population. Entire villages were consumed. Fields lay fallow. Churches could not bury the dead fast enough, and great pits were dug to receive the bodies by the cartload.

🩸

The Symptoms

The afflicted suffered grievously. First came the swellings — great buboes in the groin and armpits, hard as eggs, then bursting forth with foul corruption. Dark blotches appeared upon the skin, signifying bleeding beneath. Death came swift, within three to five days of first symptoms.

Village Life

❧

Observe the daily toil of common folk in these darkened times.

🌅

Dawn Prayers

All work begins with prayer. The village rises before the sun to attend Mass, for the Church is the center of all life.

Before Sunrise
🌾

Field Labor

Peasants toil in the lord's fields using the three-field rotation system. Wheat, then legumes, then fallow — an eternal cycle.

Morning to Midday
🍲

The Midday Meal

A simple pottage of vegetables and bread, eaten in the fields. Meat is a luxury reserved for feast days and the nobility.

High Noon
🔥

Evening Hearth

As darkness falls, families gather by the fire. Stories are told, tools mended, and the day's work is discussed by candlelight.
