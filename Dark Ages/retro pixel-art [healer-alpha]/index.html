The Dark Ages

The Dark Ages, also known as the Early Middle Ages, was a period of perceived decline in Western Europe following the fall of the Western Roman Empire. Characterized by warfare, invasion, and societal collapse, this era was anything but "dark" in terms of cultural transformation.

From the 5th to the 10th century, Europe underwent profound changes as Germanic tribes established new kingdoms on Roman foundations. The period saw the rise of Christianity, the development of feudalism, and the preservation of classical knowledge in monasteries.

The Black Death

The Black Death (1347-1351) was one of the most devastating pandemics in human history, resulting in the deaths of an estimated 75 to 200 million people in Eurasia. It peaked in Europe from 1347 to 1351.

Legacy & Influence

Though called "Dark," this age forged the foundations of medieval Europe. The period saw the blending of Roman, Christian, and Germanic traditions that would shape Western civilization for centuries to come.

Languages

Vulgar Latin evolved into Romance languages while Germanic tongues developed into English, German, and others.

Religion

Christianity spread across Europe, with monasteries becoming centers of learning and preservation.

Feudalism

The hierarchical system of lords, vassals, and peasants defined social structure for centuries.

Art & Architecture

From illuminated manuscripts to Romanesque churches, artistic expression flourished in new forms.
