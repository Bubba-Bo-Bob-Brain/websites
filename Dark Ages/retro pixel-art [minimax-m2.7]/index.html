The Chronicle of Ages

476 AD

The Fall of Rome

The last Western Roman Emperor, Romulus Augustulus, is deposed by Odoacer. This event traditionally marks the beginning of the Dark Ages, as Europe's political landscape transforms into feudal kingdoms.

597 AD

St. Augustine Arrives

Pope Gregory I sends Augustine to convert the Anglo-Saxons. He arrives in Kent and establishes monasteries that become centers of learning, preserving classical knowledge through the centuries.

793 AD

Viking Raid on Lindisfarne

Norse Vikings raid the monastery at Lindisfarne, marking the beginning of the Viking Age. These seafaring warriors terrorize coastal settlements and leave their mark across Europe.

800 AD

Charlemagne Crowned Emperor

Pope Leo III crowns Charlemagne as Emperor of the Romans on Christmas Day. This unites much of Western Europe and fosters a cultural renaissance known as the Carolingian Renaissance.

1066 AD

The Norman Conquest

William the Conqueror defeats King Harold II at the Battle of Hastings. This transforms English society, introducing feudalism and Norman French culture to Britain.

The Medieval Bestiary

In the manuscripts of learned monks, fantastical beasts were recorded alongside real animals, each carrying moral significance and medieval wisdom.

The Dragon

Symbol of Satan and chaos, the dragon represents the eternal battle between good and evil. Knights seek them for glory, saints conquer them through divine power.

Legend: "The beast that breathes fire guards treasures beyond measure."

The Unicorn

A pure creature that can only be tamed by a virgin maiden. Its horn possesses healing powers and can purify poisoned water.

Legend: "Only the pure of heart may approach the horned one."

The Griffin

Half-eagle, half-lion, the griffin represents duality—earthly strength and heavenly wisdom. They mate for life and guard treasures fiercely.

Legend: "Neither air nor earth can claim the king of beasts."

The Basilisk

The King of Serpents, whose gaze turns men to stone. Born from a chicken's egg hatched beneath a toad, it spreads death with a single look.

Legend: "Look not upon the king of serpents, lest you become stone."

The Black Death

In the year of our Lord 1347, ships arrived at the ports of Sicily bearing more than trade goods. The sailors were dead or dying, their bodies covered in black boils that oozed blood and pus. The plague had come to Europe.

Within five years, the Black Death would kill between 30 and 60 percent of Europe's population—perhaps 50 million souls perished. Villages were abandoned, fields lay fallow, and the social order that had endured for centuries crumbled.

The Plague Doctor walked among the dying, their strange beaked masks filled with herbs and spices, believing the sweet scents would ward off the miasma that carried the disease. Whether they brought hope or terror, none can say.

"I have walked through the valley of the shadow of death. The miasma hangs thick, yet I endure."
