I

n the year 847, when the Great Plague had scoured the lands and left but whispers of the living, there arose from the mists a darkness most profound. The old kings were dead, their line extinguished like candles in a tempest, and the people knew not whom to follow.

From the eastern marches came a knight, clad in armor black as a raven's wing, bearing a shield upon which was emblazoned a silver crescent moon. He spoke not his name, yet the people called him "The Pale Rider," for his face was never seen beneath his helm.

"I shall bring order from chaos," he declared, his voice echoing through the hollow halls of the abandoned castle. "I shall rebuild what the plague hath torn asunder."

Of the Gathering Storm

T

he winter that followed was the cruelest in living memory. Snow fell not in gentle drifts but in great walls of white, burying cottages and their sleeping inhabitants. The river Blackwater froze solid, and upon its surface the children would play, not knowing that beneath the ice, dark currents still flowed.

The Pale Rider's castle grew taller with each passing moon, its spires reaching toward the heavens like the fingers of a grasping hand. From its windows, a sickly green light would emanate on nights when the moon was new.
