The Age of Shadow

n the year of our Lord, after the fall of Rome, darkness descended upon the lands of Christendom. The ancient roads crumbled, the aqueducts ran dry, and the great libraries burned. Thus began the age they call Dark — though perhaps it is we who were blind to the embers that glowed still beneath the ash.

From the mist-shrouded isles of the north to the sun-baked ruins of the Mediterranean, kingdoms rose and fell like tides. Plague swept the land, carrying souls to their maker by the millions. Yet in the scriptoriums, monks bent over vellum, painting worlds of gold and crimson, preserving what light they could against the encroaching night.

✝

The castles of petty kings dotted the hills like broken teeth. In the fields, serfs toiled from dawn to dusk, their lives measured by the harvest and the hunt. And always, from the east, came whispers of pestilence — a black wind that carried the stench of death upon its breath.

This chronicle records those times. Read, and remember that even the longest night must yield to dawn.

The Great Pestilence

Anno Domini 1347 — The Black Death Cometh

0 Souls Lost to the Pestilence

The plague doctor, cloaked in dark robes and a mask shaped as a bird's beak — filled with aromatic herbs to ward off the miasma — walked the streets as both healer and harbinger. None who saw his silhouette upon the threshold could doubt that death had come calling.

Within five years, a third of Europe's population perished. Entire villages vanished from the map. Fields went unplowed, harvests rotted, and the bells tolled without cease for the dead.

A Chronicle of Ages

From the fall of Rome to the scourge of pestilence

410 AD

Rome falls to the Visigoths. The light of the ancient world dims, and the roads that once connected empires begin to crack and crumble.

476 AD

The last Western Emperor, Romulus Augustulus, is deposed. The Dark Ages begin in earnest as the remnants of order dissolve into chaos.

541 AD

The Plague of Justinian ravages the Byzantine Empire. Tens of millions perish. Constantinople buries its dead in towers along the sea.

800 AD

Charlemagne is crowned Emperor by the Pope. A flicker of renewal in the darkness — the Carolingian Renaissance kindles hope.

1066 AD

The Norman Conquest reshapes England at Hastings. The Bayeux Tapestry is woven, recording in thread what ink could not.

1347 AD

The Black Death arrives on merchant ships. Europe will never be the same. One in three souls shall be taken before the pestilence ends.
