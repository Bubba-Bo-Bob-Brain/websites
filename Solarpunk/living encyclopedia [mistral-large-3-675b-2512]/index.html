Mycorrhizal Networks: The Wood Wide Web

Ecosystems Last edited: 15 Nov 2023

Mycorrhizal Networks

  • Type: Fungal symbiosis
  • Distribution: Global (except Antarctica)
  • Key Species: Amanita muscaria, Laccaria bicolor

Read more →

Beneath the forest floor, a hidden internet pulses with life. Mycorrhizal networks—fungal threads called hyphae—connect the roots of trees and plants, forming a vast, decentralized communication system...

These networks enable trees to share water, nutrients, and even warnings about pests. Some scientists call it the "Wood Wide Web," a testament to nature's ingenuity in creating resilient, cooperative systems.

How It Works

  1. Symbiosis: Fungi colonize plant roots, trading nutrients (like phosphorus) for sugars produced via photosynthesis.
  2. Interconnection: Hyphae link multiple plants, creating a shared resource pool.
  3. Signal Transmission: Trees send stress signals (e.g., drought) through the network, prompting others to adjust their defenses.

References

  • Simard, S. W. (2018). Finding the Mother Tree: Discovering the Wisdom of the Forest. Penguin.
  • Networks of mycorrhizal fungi (Nature, 2010)
