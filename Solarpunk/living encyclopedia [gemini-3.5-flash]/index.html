Living Architecture: Mycorrhizal Integration in Urban Infrastructure

Last mutated: Solar Cycle 104.22 • Compiled by: Guild of Symbiotic Architects

B

efore the Great Restoration, human dwellings were constructed of static, dead matter—concrete and steel that fought against the elements and degraded over time. Modern Living Architecture turns this paradigm on its head, treating structures as dynamic, self-healing biomes. By inoculating porous clay structures with modified strains of Pleurotus ostreatus and companion microalgae, our cities now breath, sequester carbon, and share nutrients through a decentralized mycorrhizal grid.

1. The Myco-Concrete Hybrid

The core structural medium of contemporary Cascadian architecture is Myco-Concrete. Standard Portland cement has been entirely replaced with a mixture of locally-sourced volcanic ash, agricultural hemp fiber, and a dormant mycelial inoculant. When structural fissures occur, the introduction of moisture reactivates the fungal spores, which rapidly grow to bridge the gap, secreting calcium carbonate to fuse the fracture back together.

"The building does not resist the storm; it grows stronger through the hydration the storm brings."
— Arch. Nesta Vance, Symbiotic Guild of Cascadia

2. Energy & Resource Loop Integration

These living walls do not simply exist; they produce. Embedded within the southern-facing facades of our cooperatives are Algae Bio-Reactors. These micro-channels are filled with Chlorella vulgaris, which absorb intense solar radiation (optimized by our dynamic sunlight tracking arrays) to produce biomass for energy, while simultaneously oxygenating the interior living spaces.
